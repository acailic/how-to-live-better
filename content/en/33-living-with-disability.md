# 33. Living with a disability

This section covers life after disability has happened. Preventing disability belongs to Sections 1 and 13, not repeated.

Four metrics, never converted. Emergencies and complications count mortality; subsidies, benefits, and litigation count money; schooling and procedures count time; psychiatric hospitalization, capacity, and guardianship count personal freedom.

WHO's fact sheet gives three numbers. About 1.3 billion people — 16% of the world, roughly one in six — live with significant disability. People with disabilities may die up to 20 years earlier. They run double the risk of depression, asthma, diabetes, stroke, obesity, and poor oral health. Much of that gap traces not to the disability itself but to untreated illness, unattended school, unemployment, and inability to leave home. So half this section is the body, half procedures and rights.

Five things already written elsewhere are pointed to, not repeated: the disability certificate (Section 24, item 10); disability grading timing (Section 24, item 9); the two disability subsidies (Section 7, item 8); work-injury labor-capacity appraisal and their money (Section 19, item 14); and long-term care insurance for severe disability (Section 17, item 7 — filed under elders, but with no age floor; the young draw it equally).

Most beneficiaries here are you and family — the top two tiers. Points touching others are noted separately.

Entries in this section are grouped by theme, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Body and rehabilitation**: sudden severe headache after spinal cord injury (item 1), pressure-relief cushions for wheelchairs (item 5), no paralysis "cures" (item 6), rehabilitation at proper institutions (item 16), hearing aids (item 17).

**Benefits and subsidies**: ask the federation for the full list at once (item 7), children's rehabilitation assistance (item 8), ramp-and-grab-bar subsidies (item 9), individual income tax reduction (item 11).

**Work and rights**: disclose the certificate when job hunting (item 10), sue over disability-based refusal (item 20).

**School and travel**: guide dogs in public places (item 12), exam accommodations (item 13), schools may not refuse disabled children (item 14), driving with C5 after losing both legs (item 15).

**Mind and caregivers**: suicide risk in the first decade (item 2), caregivers watching their own bodies (item 4).

**Psychiatric disability and guardianship**: voluntary psychiatric admission (item 3), capacity adjudication through the court (item 18), statutory guardian order (item 19).

### 1. After spinal cord injury: sudden severe headache and sweating — sit upright, loosen clothing, call 120 alongside
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Three actions, tens of seconds.
- In plain terms: With spinal cord injury, part of the body's alarm wiring is cut. A full bladder or tight clothing may go unfelt while blood pressure spikes. The signals: sudden severe headache and sweating. Sit the person upright, loosen tight clothing, check for a full bladder — while calling 120.
- Benefit: The episode is autonomic dysreflexia. A systematic review across Medline, CINAHL, EMBASE, and PsycINFO, 31 studies including 6 randomized trials: acute non-drug management is three steps — upright positioning, loosening tight clothing, removing the trigger — backed by clinical consensus and physiological data (evidence level 5). Antihypertensives come only with persistent elevation, prazosin carrying level-1 evidence and nifedipine and prostaglandin E2 level-2; prevention strategies target the genitourinary and surgical triggers, mostly supported at levels 4–5
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Krassioukov A, Warburton DE, Teasell R, Eng JJ; Spinal Cord Injury Rehabilitation Evidence Research Team (2009). A systematic review of the management of autonomic dysreflexia after spinal cord injury. Archives of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, 90(4):682-695. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2008.10.017>
- Note: Grade B: no randomized trials behind the three field steps, only consensus and physiology; the review itself flags the mostly uncontrolled support. Medication belongs to physicians — no home antihypertensives. Lying flat raises pressure further, hence upright. Family and aides must know the three steps; never outlast it as ordinary headache.

### 2. The first decade after disability is the suicide high-risk window — never "look on the bright side" material
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One phone call or one registration.
- In plain terms: Emotional collapse in the early post-injury years — your own or family's — is common, not a willpower matter. Two UK spinal centers' long-term follow-up ran five times the general suicide rate, with over 90% of suicides inside the first decade post-injury; those injured by attempted suicide run higher still.
- Benefit: The study followed 2,304 traumatic spinal cord injury patients admitted 1991–2010, surviving year one, discharged with neurological deficit, followed to end-2014. 63 (2.7%) were injured by their own suicide attempts. 533 died, 4.2% by suicide, 91% within ten years of injury. The age-standardized suicide rate ran 62.5 per 100,000 yearly (95% CI 36.4–88.6, the confidence range) — five times England and Wales' 2014 rate of 12.2. Attempted-suicide injuries versus other causes: death odds ratio 4.32 (P<0.001), suicide 9.46 (P<0.001)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Savic G, DeVivo MJ, Frankel HL, Jamous MA, Soni BM, Charlifue S (2018). Suicide and traumatic spinal cord injury - a cohort study. Spinal Cord, 56(1):2-6. <https://doi.org/10.1038/sc.2017.98>
- Note: Spinal cord injury only — extrapolating to blindness, amputation, or burns is inference. What it establishes is the window: risk doesn't leave at discharge; watch for ten years. Someone to talk to: 12356 (Section 29, item 11). No stockpiled sedatives or pesticides (Section 1, item 25). Grief stuck past six months: psychiatry (Section 29, item 8).

### 3. Psychiatric hospitalization runs on the voluntary principle — only two situations override consent
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. Memorize both situations so you can say them aloud when needed.
- In plain terms: Neither family nor an employer can hold someone in a psychiatric ward by say-so. The law recognizes two situations only: self-harm done or threatened, and danger to others done or threatened — the first requiring guardian consent. Disagreement with the diagnosis draw re-diagnosis within three days and an independently commissioned appraisal thereafter.
- Benefit: Mental Health Law Article 30: "hospitalization for mental disorders implements the voluntary principle." Compulsory admission requires a severe-mental-disorder diagnosis plus either "self-harm already occurred or danger of self-harm" or "harm to others' safety already occurred or danger thereof." Article 31: the first class needs guardian consent — refusal bars admission. Article 32: disputes over the second class support re-diagnosis demanded within 3 days of the conclusion, by two psychiatrists other than the initial diagnosticians; still disputed, an independently commissioned forensic appraisal. Article 35: re-diagnosis or appraisal failing to confirm severe disorder or hospitalization need bars admission. Article 78: deliberately hospitalizing a non-patient bears legal damages (national; since 2013-05-01, amended 2018)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国精神卫生法（2018 年修正，第三十条、第三十一条、第三十二条、第三十五条、第七十八条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7448a066d>
- Note: The reverse also holds — nobody can just refuse to send. Article 28: suspected patients who have harmed themselves or others, or pose that danger, are to be stopped and delivered for diagnosis by close relatives, the employer, or local police, and institutions may not refuse the diagnosis. "Send for diagnosis" and "keep hospitalized" are two gates, the first low, the second high. Article 75 sanctions improper restraint and misdiagnosis up to license revocation. Handling threats from someone nearby: Section 8.

### 4. The person long-caring for a disabled family member needs someone watching their own body
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Farm out part of the care; get your own physical.
- In plain terms: Caregiver burnout isn't fragility. A US study followed 800-odd elders for four years: those caring for disabled spouses while reporting mental strain ran about 60% higher mortality than non-caregivers; caregiving without strain showed no elevation. What needs watching isn't caregiving itself but the chronic tension.
- Benefit: The prospective cohort, 1993–1998, average 4.5 years, four US communities: 392 caregivers and 427 non-caregivers, 66–96, all living with spouses. 103 died (12.6%) within four years. After adjusting for demographics, morbidity, and subclinical cardiovascular disease, caregiving-plus-strain ran 63% higher mortality than non-caregiving controls (RR 1.63, 95% CI 1.00–2.65, the confidence range); caregiving without strain (RR 1.08, 0.61–1.90) and disabled-spouse-without-caregiving (RR 1.37, 0.73–2.58) showed no adjusted elevation
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Schulz R, Beach SR (1999). Caregiving as a risk factor for mortality: the Caregiver Health Effects Study. JAMA, 282(23):2215-2219. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.282.23.2215>
- Note: Contested: the confidence interval's lower bound sits exactly at 1.00 — no-difference not fully excluded. The population is US spouse-cohabiting elders caring for spouses; extrapolating to those caring for younger disabled family is inference. The direction holds: the strained run higher. Farm out what can be farmed — respite and day-care services through the federation and civil affairs. The caregiver's own chronic medication never stops (Section 16, item 1). Beneficiary: you.

### 5. Long-term wheelchair users: pressure-relief cushions, and a daily check of the ischial and tail bones
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A relief cushion runs hundreds to over a thousand yuan; the daily check takes a minute.
- In plain terms: Ordinary foam breaks down skin under long pressure; sores heal slowly and infect. Nursing-home elders on pressure-relief cushions showed clearly fewer ischial pressure sores, though adding the sacrum erased the difference. Chairs themselves must be fitted, never one-size-fits-all.
- Benefit: The randomized trial ran in 12 nursing homes, 2004–2008, 232 residents over 65, 6-plus wheelchair hours daily, Braden scores under 18. Everyone got fitted wheelchairs, then randomized: skin-protection cushions (air, viscous fluid plus foam, or gel plus foam) versus 7.6-cm segmented foam, followed 6 months or to ulcer. Ischial-tuberosity ulcers: segmented foam 8 (6.7%) versus protection 1 (0.9%), P=0.04. Ischial plus sacrococcygeal combined: 21 (17.6%) versus 12 (10.6%), P=0.14 — no significant difference
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Brienza D, Kelsey S, Karg P, et al. (2010). A randomized clinical trial on preventing pressure ulcers with wheelchair seat cushions. Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, 58(12):2308-2314. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2010.03168.x>
- Note: Grade B twice: 232 participants with the primary endpoint's P=0.04 barely clearing, and a frail nursing-home population extrapolated to younger users. Everyone was chair-fitted first — fitting and cushion work together; never swap the cushion alone. Bed-bound pressure care — air mattresses and scheduled turning: Section 17, item 8. Assistive-device subsidies: item 7.

### 6. Never buy therapies or devices claiming to "cure paralysis, blindness, or deafness"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free — it saves money. The hard part: family hope resisting persuasion.
- In plain terms: The Advertising Law closes the door: medical, drug, and device ads may not guarantee efficacy, cite cure or effectiveness rates, or feature spokespatients. So "walking after three courses" and "so many already recovered" are themselves illegal ads — no need to study the theory. Non-devices can't so much as mention treating disease.
- Benefit: Advertising Law Article 16: medical, drug, and device ads may not contain "assertions or guarantees of efficacy or safety; cure rates or effectiveness rates; comparisons with other drugs, devices, or institutions; or recommendations by advertising spokespersons." Article 17: outside those categories, "no advertisement may involve disease-treatment functions, nor use medical terminology or terms confusing the goods with drugs or devices." Article 4: ads may not deceive or mislead; advertisers answer for truthfulness (national; 2021 amendment)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国广告法（2021 年修正，第四条、第十六条、第十七条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817ab231eb017abd6bd860052d>
- Note: One test: whether it dares overpromise. Legitimate rehabilitation departments never promise walking in N courses. Report such marketing to 12315 or the local market regulator. Health-product scripts run the same route (Section 6, item 10). Refunds for purchases already made: Section 5, item 29. Beneficiaries: you and family.

### 7. After the disability certificate: one visit to the county federation asking for everything applicable
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One morning at the federation; several departments follow.
- In plain terms: The certificate itself pays nothing — it's a key. Behind it sit several independent benefits, each self-applied, never automatic. List them all in the first visit; cheaper than repeat trips.
- Benefit: At least six benefits attach. First, the living and nursing subsidies (Section 7, item 8). Second, children's rehabilitation assistance (item 8). Third, basic assistive-device subsidies under Article 26 of the Disability Prevention and Rehabilitation Regulations. Fourth, home barrier-free renovation subsidies (item 9). Fifth, quota employment and employment services (item 10). Sixth, individual income tax reduction (item 11). Article 26 adds: "state-provided basic rehabilitation services for poor and severely disabled urban and rural residents through key rehabilitation projects, with subsidies for basic assistive-device configuration per state rules." Article 20: county-plus departments and federations use community resources to run rehabilitation venues or purchase services — rehabilitation guidance, daily-living training, rehabilitation nursing, device fitting, information, and referral (national; since 2017-07-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十条、第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Note: The certificate process itself: Section 24, item 10. Standards and procedures are mostly provincial, differing severalfold between provinces. Record each item's name and re-verify against the local federation's site — never take counter oral assurances alone. No single visit exhausts it; re-ask yearly as policy shifts.

### 8. A child under 7 with disability or autism: apply for rehabilitation assistance at the county federation
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One application at the county federation, then choosing among designated institutions.
- In plain terms: The state runs a rehabilitation-assistance system for disabled and autistic children 0–6, covering surgery, assistive devices, and rehabilitation training — prioritizing difficult households, with localities free to widen ages and relax income conditions. This window carries the best rehabilitation returns; never wait for school age. Institutions come from the federation's designated list.
- Benefit: The State Council opinion covers qualified 0–6 children with visual, hearing, speech, physical, or intellectual disabilities and autism — including dibao households, registered poor households, welfare-institution children, disabled orphans, those under destitution support, and other economically difficult families, with the remaining determination by county-plus governments. "Regions with capacity may expand the age range and relax family-economic restrictions." Funding standards are set by county-plus governments by local capacity, beneficiary numbers, and disability category, with dynamic adjustment. The statutory basis — Article 26 of the regulations: "gradually achieving free surgery, assistive-device provision, and rehabilitation training for 0–6 visually, hearing, speech, physically, and intellectually disabled children and autistic children" (national; since 2018)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2018). 关于建立残疾儿童康复救助制度的意见（国发〔2018〕20 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-07/10/content_5305296.htm>；国务院 (2017). 残疾预防和残疾人康复条例（第二十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/27/content_5171308.htm>
- Note: Standards are provincial — search the local federation's site for the implementation measures; the designated-institution list sits with the federation. The documents require entry-and-exit supervision of designated institutions, periodic inspection, comprehensive evaluation, and blacklisting. Undesignated institutions mean full self-payment and hard recourse. Beneficiary: your child. Institutions promising "guaranteed recovery" get item 6's treatment.

### 9. Home ramps, grab bars, and bathroom renovations: apply for subsidies from county-plus governments
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Application free; renovations mostly subsidized with possible self-top-ups. Runs through the federation and subdistrict.
- In plain terms: The Barrier-Free Environment Law writes it: county-plus governments support and guide home barrier-free renovation, granting appropriate subsidies to qualifying disabled and elderly households. Projects usually lead through the federation with annual quotas — register early. Amounts and scopes sit locally.
- Benefit: Article 19: "county-plus people's governments shall support and guide home barrier-free renovation, granting appropriate subsidies to qualified disabled and elderly households." Article 18: existing non-compliant buildings, complexes, public venues, transport, and roads get targeted renovation plans. Article 62: any organization or individual may complain of violations; authorities "shall handle and reply promptly." Article 63: procuratorates may issue procuratorial suggestions or public-interest litigation over public-interest violations (national; since 2023-09-01)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法（第十八条、第十九条、第六十二条、第六十三条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>
- Note: Grade B: the law says only "appropriate subsidies," amounts and eligibility wholly local — no national figures. Search the federation's site for "difficult severe-disability household barrier-free renovation." Quotas run annually — queue early. Elevator retrofits ride a separate housing-construction process. Non-compliant public facilities: complain under Article 62, which obliges answers.

### 10. Job hunting: disclose the disability certificate — hiring you saves the employer real money
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One extra sentence at the interview. The hard part: saying it.
- In plain terms: Employers must staff at least 1.5% disabled workers (provincially set); shortfalls pay the disability-employment fund per missing head at last year's average wage. Hiring you isn't charity — it's real savings. Say it plainly; the employer does the math.
- Benefit: The Employment Regulations, Article 8: "no less than 1.5% of total staff, specific ratios set provincially." Article 9: shortfalls pay the fund. The fund formula, Article 8 of the collection measures: "annual payment = (last year's headcount × provincial ratio − last year's actual disabled staff) × last year's average wage." Article 16: small enterprises under 20 staff within 3 years of registration exempt. The Protection Law, Article 36: employers meeting or exceeding the ratio enjoy tax preferences (national; regulations since 2007-05-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2007). 残疾人就业条例（第八条、第九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6646.htm>；财政部、国家税务总局、中国残联 (2015). 残疾人就业保障金征收使用管理办法（财税〔2015〕72 号，第六条、第八条、第十六条）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2015-09/15/content_5650063.htm>；中华人民共和国残疾人保障法（第三十六条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Note: The argument fails for under-20 firms inside three years — don't bank on it. Fund relief keeps changing; the year's MOF notices govern. Quota employment demands real work: nominal-employment wage-drawing schemes defraud the fund and implicate you. Employment-difficulty certification and social-insurance subsidies: Section 7, item 12; training subsidies: item 13. Beneficiary: you.

### 11. Disability reduces individual income tax — call the provincial tax bureau for the amount
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=small metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One checkbox at filing or one filed record.
- In plain terms: The Income Tax Law allows reduced tax on disabled persons' income — amounts and durations delegated to provinces, so local rules differ, some capping yearly reductions, some cutting by percentage. Call 12366 or check the income-tax app's deduction items.
- Benefit: Article 5: "reductions may apply, with specific magnitude and duration set by provincial governments and filed with the standing committees: income of the disabled, childless elders, and martyrs' families; and major natural-disaster losses." Article 36 of the Protection Law adds tax preferences for self-employed disabled persons and exemption from administrative fees (national; since 2019-01-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国个人所得税法（2018 年修正，第五条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf724bd0609>；中华人民共和国残疾人保障法（第三十六条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Note: Benefit "small": the law gives no numbers, and most provinces' relief runs hundreds to a few thousand yuan yearly. Modest money, but filed once it lasts — worth the trip. Only the local bureau's notices count; circulating comparison tables are perennially stale. Self-employed: administrative fees exempt — raise it at licensing.

### 12. Guide dogs enter public places and transit; blind riders ride city transit free
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part: citing the statute when blocked, possibly standing your ground.
- In plain terms: The law obliges public venues and transit to accommodate guide, hearing, and service dogs. Blind persons ride city buses, metros, and ferries free with valid documents; disabled persons carry necessary aids free. Say both aloud when blocked; complain when refused.
- Benefit: Barrier-Free Law Article 46: "public venue operators and transport facilities and vehicles shall accommodate disabled persons' guide dogs, hearing dogs, and other service dogs." Article 35: hotlines and emergency systems gradually gain voice, large print, braille, and one-touch access. Article 62: anyone may complain of violations, with prompt handling and replies. Protection Law Article 50: "blind persons ride city buses, trolleybuses, metros, and ferries free with valid documents; braille materials post free." Counties-plus give transit convenience and discounts, with necessary aids carried free (national; since 2023-09-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法（第三十五条、第四十六条、第六十二条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff80818188d7430b0189018493370940>；中华人民共和国残疾人保障法（第五十条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>
- Note: The law says "accommodate" without penalties — standoff moments may not resolve instantly. A statute screenshot on the phone beats memorized numbers. Unresolved on the spot: complain under Article 62, which obliges replies. Rail and aviation run their own rules — call ahead. "Valid documents" vary locally; ask the federation which count.

### 13. Exam accommodations for disabled examinees: braille papers earn 50% more time
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free. One written application within the province's window, plus on-site confirmation.
- In plain terms: Disabled examinees may request braille or large-print papers, exemption from foreign-language listening, separate exam rooms, hearing aids and wheelchairs in, and extended time. None arrive automatically — written applications on the provincial schedule, with a second-generation-plus disability certificate. Results contestable through the provincial education department.
- Benefit: The management provisions, Article 5, list twelve accommodations — braille, large-print, or standard papers for the visually disabled; listening exemption for the hearing disabled; permitted devices from braille styli and slates through electronic magnifiers, white canes, hearing aids, cochlear implants, wheelchairs, and walkers; the extensions: "braille-paper users gain 50% beyond the standard duration; large-print or standard-paper users and those unable to write normally gain 30%." Plus priority entry, separate standardized rooms, guides, and sign-language interpreters. Article 7: formal written application per provincial rules with the certificate; expert on-site confirmation and evaluation; the province issues a Notification Letter. Article 8: disputes go to the provincial education department for review. Article 9: listening-exempted foreign-language scores compute as written score × total / written total. Article 18: other national education exams may refer to these provisions (national; effective 2017-04-07)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 教育部、中国残联 (2017). 残疾人参加普通高等学校招生全国统一考试管理规定（教学〔2017〕4 号，第五条、第七条、第八条、第九条、第十八条）. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-04/12/content_5650060.htm>
- Note: Application windows are provincial and printed in the year's enrollment rules — missed is gone, so confirm timing on the provincial examination authority's site in the fall of senior year. Article 18 extends by reference to graduate exams and adult exams. Schools not refusing enrollment: item 14. Beneficiary: you or your child.

### 14. Disabled children applying to school may not be refused; those unable to attend get home-delivered teaching arranged by the education bureau

- Cost: Free. One application, possibly one evaluation; escalation to the education bureau when schools stall.
- In plain terms: Kindergartens and schools of every level may not refuse qualifying disabled applicants, ordinary vocational schools included. Children needing full-time care, unable to attend, get home-delivered or remote teaching arranged by the county bureau — with enrollment. Admission and transfer decisions come from the bureau's expert committee, never the school alone.
- Benefit: The Education Regulations, Article 7: "preschool institutions, schools at all levels, and other educational institutions implementing disability education may not refuse qualifying disabled applicants." Article 29: ordinary vocational schools may not refuse disabled applicants meeting national admission standards. Children needing personal care and unable to attend receive compulsory education via home-delivered or remote teaching arranged by county education authorities, enrolled in the records. Article 20: county bureaus with health, civil affairs, and the federation establish expert committees assessing physical condition, educability, and school adaptation, with admission and transfer recommendations — the results the child's privacy, confidentiality-bound. Article 23: mainstreamed disabled students may follow ordinary curriculum with "moderately flexible" requirements. Article 52: necessary support and accommodations available on request for national exams (national; since 2017-05-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2017). 残疾人教育条例（第七条、第二十条、第二十三条、第二十九条、第五十二条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2017-02/23/content_5170264.htm>
- Note: A school's oral "we lack the facilities" counts for nothing — demand it in writing and take the letter to the county bureau. The evaluation comes through the bureau's expert committee. ### 15. Losing the right leg or both legs still licenses driving — the class is C5
<!-- costs: money=high time=high will=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Tuition near ordinary licenses — a few thousand yuan mostly. The car needs hand controls. Physicals every three years.
- In plain terms: Missing or immobile right or both legs, but able to sit independently with qualifying arms, licenses the disabled-person automatic small-car class — C5. A missing palm qualifies too. Monocular vision and hearing impairment each carry their own classes. Physicals run at provincially designated hospitals.
- Benefit: The licensing regulation's Annex 1, item 8: "right or both lower limbs missing or functionless but able to sit independently, upper limbs meeting item 5, may apply for the disabled-person automatic small passenger class; one palm missing, the other thumb sound with two other sound fingers, upper limbs normal and lower limbs meeting item 6, likewise." Item 6: isolated left-leg loss with a sound right leg licenses small automatics. Vision: "monocularly impaired with the better eye at 5.0-plus corrected or naked, horizontal field 150 degrees" licenses small cars, automatics, low-speed trucks, tri-wheelers, and the disabled class. Hearing: aided discrimination of sound direction licenses small cars and automatics, worn while driving. Article 85: disability-class physicals issued by provincially designated institutions; holders examine every three years, certificates submitted within 30 days of the scoring cycle's end. Article 78: the disabled-vehicle sign displayed while driving (national; Order 172, since 2025-01-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 公安部令第 172 号. 机动车驾驶证申领和使用规定（第七十八条、第八十五条、附件 1）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11866/202502/content_7004031.html>
- Note: Far lower-threshold: the powered wheelchair — the Road Traffic Law lists it as a non-motor vehicle, no license; Article 58 caps it at 15 km/h in bike lanes. C5 cars need hand throttle and brake from qualified converters, then registration change at the DMV. Missing three consecutive physicals within a year cancels the license.

### 16. Rehabilitation at proper institutions with rehabilitation-medicine departments — intensity follows the therapist, never more-is-better
<!-- costs: money=high time=high will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Inpatient rehabilitation runs hundreds daily, self-paid portions varying; months of daily, tedious practice.
- In plain terms: Taking the person home to rest and wait out natural recovery is the commonest mistake. Organized multidisciplinary stroke-unit care leaves more people alive and home-dwelling at one year. But never self-escalate: earlier-and-harder activity within 24 hours of stroke worsened three-month outcomes. How much and when — the rehabilitation physician and therapist decide.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 29 trials, 5,902 participants, organized inpatient stroke-unit care versus alternatives: at final follow-up (median 1 year), poor outcomes (death, disability, or institutional care) OR 0.77 (95% CI 0.69–0.87, the confidence range), death OR 0.76 (0.66–0.88), death-or-dependence OR 0.75 (0.66–0.85), moderate certainty — per 100 treated, 2 more alive, 6 more living at home, 6 more independent. Conversely AVERT randomized 2,104 patients across 56 acute stroke units in 5 countries to usual care versus very-early high-intensity mobilization: good 3-month outcomes (modified Rankin 0–2) 480 (46%) versus 525 (50%), adjusted OR 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.90, P=0.004); deaths 88 versus 72, no difference (OR 1.34, 0.93–1.93, P=0.113)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Langhorne P, Ramachandra S; Stroke Unit Trialists' Collaboration (2020). Organised inpatient (stroke unit) care for stroke: network meta-analysis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 4:CD000197. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000197.pub4>；AVERT Trial Collaboration group (2015). Efficacy and safety of very early mobilisation within 24 h of stroke onset (AVERT): a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 386(9988):46-55. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)60690-0>
- Note: Contested: both bodies of evidence come from stroke — extrapolating to spinal cord injury, amputation, and burns is inference. AVERT refutes "earlier and harder," not "early activity" — nearly everyone in both arms mobilized within 24 hours; the difference was frequency and total dose. Pick institutions by rehabilitation-medicine departments and credentialed therapists, not the word "rehabilitation" on the sign. Referrals and deductibles: Section 24, item 1. Community rehabilitation and devices: item 7.

### 17. Hearing loss: get hearing aids — for hearing now, not to prevent dementia
<!-- costs: money=high time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Hearing aids run thousands to tens of thousands, mostly self-paid, with fitting and repeat tuning.
- In plain terms: "Hearing aids prevent dementia" hasn't held up. A near-thousand-person three-year trial found no overall difference — effects appeared only in the already higher-risk subgroup. Fit hearing aids to hear, converse, and catch oncoming traffic — not for a brain decades out.
- Benefit: The ACHIEVE trial, 4 US communities, 977 participants aged 70–84 with untreated hearing loss and no marked cognitive impairment, randomized to hearing intervention (counseling plus aids) or health-education control, follow-up every 6 months, primary endpoint 3-year global-cognition change. In the pooled primary analysis, no significant difference: intervention −0.200 (95% CI −0.256 to −0.144) versus control −0.202 (−0.258 to −0.145), difference 0.002 (−0.077 to 0.081), P=0.96. A prespecified sensitivity analysis found effect heterogeneity across the two source populations (interaction P=0.010): the long-running cardiovascular-cohort participants were older, richer in decline risk factors, lower at baseline — leading the researchers to conclude hearing intervention may reduce 3-year cognitive change in higher-risk populations, not in lower-risk ones. No attributable serious adverse events reported
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lin FR, Pike JR, Albert MS, et al.; ACHIEVE Collaborative Research Group (2023). Hearing intervention versus health education control to reduce cognitive decline in older adults with hearing loss in the USA (ACHIEVE): a multicentre, randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 402(10404):786-797. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01406-X>
- Note: Contested: the circulating "aids cut cognitive decline nearly in half" comes from one subgroup, not the primary result — the primary result showed no difference. Fit the aids; drop the dementia rationale. Hearing-disabled examinees: listening exemption, item 13. Hearing-impaired drivers wear devices: item 15.

### 18. Declaring an adult "unable to manage their affairs" takes a court application — family say-so doesn't count
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Court filing tens to about 100 yuan; appraisal fees usually thousands, advanced by the applicant; months to conclude.
- In plain terms: Whether an adult lacks or has limited civil capacity is for the court alone — neither family, employer, nor residents' committee decides. Interested parties or organizations apply; recovery supports restoration applications. The procedure both restricts and protects.
- Benefit: Civil Code Article 21: adults unable to discern their own acts are without civil capacity, represented by statutory agents. Article 22: those unable to fully discern hold limited capacity, acting through agents or with consent-ratification, while independently performing purely beneficial acts or acts fitting their condition. Article 24: interested parties or organizations — residents' and villagers' committees, schools, medical institutions, the women's federation, the disabled persons' federation, elders' organizations, civil affairs — may apply for the determination, and later for restoration to limited or full capacity. Article 23: guardians of the incapacitated are their statutory agents (national; since 2021-01-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国民法典（第二十一条、第二十二条、第二十三条、第二十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Note: The special procedure runs faster than ordinary litigation yet still takes months — hospital diagnosis submitted, courts usually commissioning forensic appraisal at thousands advanced by the applicant. Signing or disposing of property without it risks invalidation — even a sold house can come back. And limited capacity never means nothing: acts fitting their condition and purely beneficial ones they do themselves — never blanket-proxy. Guardians: item 19.

### 19. Adult guardianship follows the statutory order; guardians pay for the ward's harm to others
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free; disputes run through committees, civil affairs, or courts.
- In plain terms: On incapacity, guardians come in order — spouse; parents and children; other close relatives; then willing persons or organizations. Disputes go to committees or civil affairs for designation, courts on appeal. While lucid, a written future guardian can be named. The ward harming others: the guardian pays.
- Benefit: Civil Code Article 28's order: "(1) spouse; (2) parents, children; (3) other close relatives; (4) other willing individuals or organizations, with the consent of the residents' or villagers' committee or civil affairs of the ward's domicile." Article 31: designation disputes go to those bodies, courts on appeal or directly, "respecting the ward's true wishes and the most-favorable principle"; interim guardianship where protection gaps. Article 33: fully capable adults may agree their future guardian in writing. Article 1188: the incapacitated harming others — guardians bear tort liability, mitigated by diligent guardianship; damages paid first from the ward's own assets, shortfalls by the guardian. Article 1189: delegated care leaves the guardian liable, delegates liable for fault (national; since 2021-01-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国民法典（第二十八条、第三十一条、第三十三条、第一千一百八十八条、第一千一百八十九条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Note: Article 33 is agreed guardianship — naming the person in writing while lucid forestalls most disputes (Section 17, item 1). The liability paragraph warns: delegating care to institutions or relatives leaves the guardian responsible, delegates liable only for fault — making guardian-liability insurance meaningful. Article 78 of the Mental Health Law: guardians abandoning patients or breaching duties bear damages.

### 20. Refused hiring, denied confirmation, or underpaid over disability: you can sue — but price the process first
<!-- costs: money=high time=high will=yes benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Attorney fees thousands to tens of thousands, yours; first instance 6-plus months, extendable; evidence self-preserved.
- In plain terms: The law bars disability discrimination in hiring, confirmation, promotion, titles, pay, and insurance. You may complain to the authorities or sue — but winning doesn't shift your attorney fees. So complain first, apply for legal aid alongside, then weigh litigation.
- Benefit: Protection Law Article 3: "discrimination on the basis of disability is prohibited; insulting and infringing disabled persons is prohibited." Article 38: no discrimination "in hiring, confirmation, promotion, title evaluation, remuneration, welfare, leave, or social insurance," with employers owing appropriate conditions, protection, and workplace adaptation. Article 64: discriminating employers face correction orders; disabled workers may sue. The Employment Regulations, Article 4: "discrimination against disabled persons in employment is prohibited"; Article 13 bars discrimination in promotion, titles, pay, insurance, and welfare. The Legal Aid Law, Article 42: applicants proving membership in "specific groups — minors, elders, disabled persons without fixed income —" skip means-testing; Article 45: aid bodies provide barrier-free facilities and services (national)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中华人民共和国残疾人保障法（第三条、第三十八条、第六十四条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8080816f135f46016f1d134c88132b>；国务院 (2007). 残疾人就业条例（第四条、第十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6646.htm>；中华人民共和国法律援助法（第四十二条、第四十五条）. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff8081817b6472a3017b6577820f0061>
- Note: Process costs come first: first instance 6-plus months extendable, simplified 3; attorney fees sit outside Article 6's litigation costs, and Article 29's loser-pays excludes them. So the two cheap roads first — complaints to HR authorities or the federation for correction orders, and legal aid with means-testing waived for disabled applicants. Evidence is the hardest gate: refusals rarely leave written reasons — record what you can. Already employed: labor arbitration, evidence and deadlines per Section 19, item 8. Beneficiary: you.
