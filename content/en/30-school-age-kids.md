# 30. School-age children

Section 20 covered the newborn year; Section 18 counted the money and time of raising children. The in-between decade-plus, ages 3 to 18, lives here. The screen-time entry starts at 0 because the official bands are 0–3, 3–6, and school-age — splitting them apart would be less usable. This section answers: when "don't disrupt school" collides with "the body," what cannot wait — things with time windows that don't reopen. Whether academics should be this grueling isn't discussed; only windows and numbers.

Entries 1–8, 12 (cycloplegic refraction), 13 (sealants), and 15–18 count death and lasting physical or psychological damage; 16 and 17 cover how households manage — no hitting, no berating, and parent classes teaching concrete methods; 18 covers never sending children into locked-down anti-addiction schools. Item 9 (no "myopia cure" products) counts money. Items 10 (sleep, homework, exercise, ranking rules), 11 (suspension with preserved enrollment), and 14 (gaming) count time. Drowning, traffic, helmets, HPV: Section 1. Infancy: Section 20. Scams and in-game charges: Section 5. Minors' own red lines: Section 9. Recognizing emergencies: Section 13. Parentally bereaved children: Section 29. None repeated.

### 1. A child reports sudden severe pain that keeps worsening: hospital now — never "hold out until school ends"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Half a day off, emergency registration.
- In plain terms: Some illnesses count in hours. A boy's sudden one-sided scrotal pain is testicular torsion — the cord's vessels twisted, blood supply cut. Treated within 6 hours, 97.2% of testicles survive; at 19–24 hours, 42.5%. That pain waits neither for the bell nor the exam.
- Benefit: A systematic review assembling 30 case series, 2,116 torsion patients — 1,283 banded by 6-hour blocks. Testicular survival from pain onset: 0–6 hours 97.2%; 7–12 79.3%; 13–18 61.3%; 19–24 42.5%; 25–48 24.4%; past 48 hours 7.4%. Banded wider: within 12 hours 90.4%; 13–24 54.0%; past 24 18.1%. The authors: the salvage window can run longer than the usual 6–8 hours — so go even if long delayed, never skip on "too late anyway"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mellick LB, et al. (2019). A Systematic Review of Testicle Survival Time After a Torsion Event. Pediatric Emergency Care. <https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001287>
- Note: Tell boys in advance: sudden severe pain down there — say so immediately, no embarrassment. Torsion clusters in adolescent boys, many of whom endure in silence. Also hourly-counted: acute appendicitis (perforation with delay), diabetic ketoacidosis, meningitis — per-item delay tables weren't verified here. Their shared signature: sudden onset, steadily worsening, unlike anything before. Recognition and transport: Section 13.

### 2. Don't defer needed treatment "until exams end" — some windows follow bone age, not exam schedules
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — the choice is early versus late; leave and follow-up take time, treatment costs sit elsewhere. The hard part: braces demand 18-plus hours daily, worn through.
- In plain terms: Scoliosis is sideways spinal curvature; the brace is a rigid shell holding the spine straight. Worn while bone grows, 75% of children avoid surgical thresholds versus 42% observed only. The wearable window follows bone maturity, not entrance exams — waiting usually ends in surgery.
- Benefit: A multicenter trial took 242 brace-eligible adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis: 116 randomized, 126 preference. Failure pre-defined as progression past 50 degrees; success as staying under at skeletal maturity. By assigned arm regardless of adherence: brace 75% versus observation 42% (OR 4.11, 95% CI 1.85–9.16) — four times the success. Randomized plus preference pooled: 72% versus 48% (adjusted OR 1.93, 1.08–3.46). More daily wear hours, more success (P<0.001). The trial stopped early for clear benefit
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Weinstein SL, et al. (2013). Effects of bracing in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1307337>
- Note: Scoliosis is the example because it carries a randomized trial, a clear window, and a school-physical staple (item 7). Same class: strabismus, amblyopia, caries, and elective surgeries like congenital heart repairs — all worse or costlier later. Ask the specialist for windows. School feared disrupted: compulsory-education suspension runs to a year with enrollment preserved — item 11.

### 3. A child bullied: report to the school the same day and demand written handling; beatings, robbery, rumor-spreading go straight to 110
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The class teacher and the school's anti-bullying body; 110 when warranted. The hard part: withstanding "don't blow this up."
- In plain terms: Peer-victimized children run about 2.2× the suicidal-ideation odds and 2.6× attempt odds. Schools must investigate on report, discipline on finding, and report serious cases to police without concealment — so report same-day, demand the determination process, keep written records.
- Benefit: Pooling 34 studies, 284,375 children: peer victimization tracked suicidal ideation at OR 2.23 (95% CI 2.10–2.37); across 9 studies, 70,102, attempts at OR 2.55 (1.95–3.34). Stable across sex, age, and study quality; cyberbullying ran stronger than offline. The School Protection Provisions, Article 21, list five behavior classes — hitting, kicking, slapping, biting, shoving, pulling; insulting, mocking, derogatory nicknames; seizing, extorting, or destroying property; malicious exclusion; and online fabrication or rumor-spreading — with advantage in age, body, or numbers plus intent, causing bodily, property, or mental harm, qualifying as bullying. Article 22 obliges staff noticing distress or injury to inquire and report; Article 23 obliges immediate investigation on report, referral to the bullying-governance body, parental participation, discipline on finding, and no concealment of public-security or criminal-grade bullying (national; since 2021-09-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: van Geel M, et al. (2014). Relationship between peer victimization, cyberbullying, and suicide in children and adolescents: a meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2013.4143>；教育部 (2021). 未成年人学校保护规定（教育部令第 50 号，第二十一、二十二、二十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-06/02/content_5614946.htm>
- Note: Unexplained injuries, school refusal, repeated loss of belongings — ask per Article 22's signals. The pooled analysis is observational — bullying alone doesn't provenly cause suicide — but direction and size are clear. The comprehensive-treatment plan separately demands distinguishing bullying from ordinary roughhousing: schools owe a determination, never "kids being kids."

### 4. Two hours outdoors daily — the only myopia prevention backed by a randomized trial
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, but daily time — mostly squeezed from homework and online classes. The hard part: every day, not a few times.
- In plain terms: Forty extra minutes of outdoor class daily in first grade cut three-year new myopia from about 40% to about 30%. The health commission's dose: 2 daytime hours daily, or 14 weekly. Cloudy counts; standing outside at recess counts.
- Benefit: Twelve Guangzhou primary schools randomized by school (intervention 952, control 951, mean age 6.6). Intervention added one 40-minute outdoor class per school day plus parental encouragement. Three-year myopia incidence: 30.4% (259 of 853 evaluable) versus 39.5% (287 of 726) — a difference of −9.1 points (95% CI −14.1 to −4.1, P<0.001). Three-year spherical-equivalent progression −1.42 D versus −1.59 D, differing 0.17 D (P=0.04); axial elongation didn't differ (P=0.07). The Ten Core Points require 2 daily daytime hours or 14 weekly, note cloudy-day benefit, and advise avoiding midday heat
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: He M, et al. (2015). Effect of Time Spent Outdoors at School on the Development of Myopia Among Children in China: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.10803>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: The trial ran 3 years from age 6; the 9.1-point gap protects some children, not all. The same Ten Points add: "one chi, one fist, one cun" reading posture; 20-second distant gazes every 20 near minutes; non-study school-age screens 15 minutes per session, 1 hour daily. "Outdoors" isn't "PE class" — recess outside counts.

### 5. Screens: none at 0–3, minimal at 3–6, school-age non-study use under 1 hour daily
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard parts: taking the screen away and parents cutting their own in view of the child.
- In plain terms: The health commission: no screens 0–3, avoid 3–6, school-age non-study screens 15 minutes per session and 1 hour daily — prolonged near work brings early myopia. Screen evidence runs softer than the 2 outdoor hours.
- Benefit: The Ten Points, article 4: "infants 0–3 do not use phones, tablets, computers, or other screen products; children 3–6 should avoid them as far as possible; school-age non-study screen use per session should not exceed 15 minutes, cumulative daily use not exceed 1 hour," adding that "prolonged near use of screens readily depletes children's hyperopia reserve, a major reason for early and high myopia." The 2018 eight-department plan matches and adds "the younger, the shorter the continuous use." Research runs weaker: a review of 33 studies, 11 pooled, ages 3 months to 33 years — phones and tablets alone OR 1.26 (95% CI 1.00 to 1.60, I²=77%); adding computers 1.77 (1.28 to 2.45, I²=87%); the authors say only "possibly associated," noting none of the 33 measured screen time reliably
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm> ; 教育部等八部门 (2018). 综合防控儿童青少年近视实施方案（教体艺〔2018〕3 号）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A17/moe_943/s3285/201808/t20180830_346672.html> ; Foreman J, Salim AT, Praveen A, et al. (2021). Association between digital smart device use and myopia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Digital Health, 3(12), e806-e818. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2589-7500(21)00135-7> ; Madigan S, McArthur BA, Anhorn C, Eirich R, Christakis DA (2020). Associations between screen use and child language skills: a systematic review and meta-analysis. JAMA Pediatrics, 174(7), 665-675. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2020.0327>
- Note: Grade B because the official text is banded regulation, not trial numbers; the pooled lower bound sits exactly at 1.00, authors saying only "possibly associated." Treat it as an enforceable house rule, never "no screens, no myopia." The randomized backing belongs to item 4. Preschool eyes: the same document mandates refractive screening at 1–3, 4–6, and past 7 — method in item 12. Language pooling: 42 studies, 18,905 children — more screen time, weaker language (r=−0.14, 95% CI −0.18 to −0.10); co-viewing and educational content ran positive (r=0.16 and 0.13) — correlation, showing only that screens don't substitute for talking with your child.

### 6. Treat myopia as lasting damage: deeper diopters, more retinal detachment, maculopathy, glaucoma
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Nothing to do here — the doing sits in items 4 and 5.
- In plain terms: Myopia neither self-heals nor recedes, and it isn't just "wearing glasses." Versus the non-myopic, myopic maculopathy odds run over tenfold at low myopia and hundreds-fold past 600 degrees; retinal detachment about 3× low and 13× high. Earlier onset and faster progression mean more to carry later.
- Benefit: A review pooling studies to June 2019, banded by diopter: mild (−0.50 to −3.00 D), moderate (−3.00 to −6.00 D), high (≤−6.00 D). Versus emmetropes, myopic macular degeneration ORs 13.57 (95% CI 6.18 to 29.79), 72.74 (33.18 to 159.48), 845.08 (230.05 to 3104.34); retinal detachment 3.15 (1.92 to 5.17), 8.74 (7.28 to 10.50), 12.62 (6.65 to 23.94); posterior-subcapsular cataract 1.56, 2.55, 4.55; open-angle glaucoma mild 1.59 (1.33 to 1.91), moderate-plus-high 2.92 (1.89 to 4.52); vision impairment past 60: 1.71 (1.07 to 2.74), 5.54 (3.12 to 9.85), 87.63 (34.50 to 222.58). The Ten Points match: past 600 degrees counts high myopia with markedly elevated blinding-disease incidence, and "myopia is preventable and controllable but irreversible"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Haarman AEG, Enthoven CA, Tideman JWL, Tedja MS, Verhoeven VJM, Klaver CCW (2020). The complications of myopia: a review and meta-analysis. Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science, 61(4), 49. <https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.61.4.49> ; 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: All observational, with unusually large odds ratios; high-myopia maculopathy's CI spanning 230–3104 marks heterogeneity — magnitudes, not precision. Most complications surface in mid-to-late life; myopia itself isn't disability, and the corrected mostly see normally. Still, myopia isn't "just glasses" — worth delaying and controlling. Follow-up after diagnosis: item 12; never buy cures: item 9.

### 7. Read the annual school physical report yourself; abnormal findings go to a hospital the same year
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — compulsory-stage physicals bill school operating funds.
- In plain terms: Schools run one physical yearly. Watch for overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal abnormality, poor vision, and caries — the most commonly shelved. Don't file the slip after the height-weight glance; abnormal items go to specialists that year.
- Benefit: The 2021 management measures: yearly physicals covering surgical (head, neck, chest, spine, limbs, skin, lymph nodes) and ophthalmic (appearance, distance vision, refraction) exams; individual reports carrying findings, overall evaluation, and guidance, with overweight, obesity, malnutrition, spinal abnormality, poor vision, and caries as guidance priorities; education authorities filing and following up flagged students; compulsory-stage costs from school funds (national; issued September 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委、教育部 (2021). 中小学生健康体检管理办法（2021 年版）（国卫医发〔2021〕29 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-10/21/content_5644100.htm>
- Note: Grade B: this governs schools' procedure, not outcomes — it guarantees yearly checks and flagging, not follow-through. Physicals screen, never diagnose. Poor vision goes to cycloplegic refraction (item 12); spinal abnormality to orthopedics or spine surgery (item 2 — never deferred for exams). Both steps ride on the parent.

### 8. Ages 12–18: one depression screen — and never treat school surveys as diagnosis
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 0 to tens of yuan for a clinic questionnaire; 12355 and 12356 are free.
- In plain terms: US guidelines screen 12–18-year-olds for major depression; under 11, evidence runs insufficient. Suicide is the second-leading killer at 10–19. School surveys are censuses, never diagnoses. A child voicing distress, or sudden shifts in eating, sleep, interest, or grades, registers directly at psychiatry or clinical psychology.
- Benefit: The USPSTF 2022 statement: screen adolescents 12–18 for major depressive disorder (grade B); under 11, insufficient evidence (I statement); suicide-risk screening in children and adolescents, insufficient either way (I). Suicide ranks second among 10–19 deaths; adolescent major depression tracks adult recurrence, other disorders, ideation, attempts, and deaths. The domestic Ten Measures build the three-tier system — class teachers, psychological teachers, psychiatrists — with one psychological survey per student per school year, referral-to-treatment and post-recovery re-enrollment mechanisms, and 12355/12356 both engaged (national; issued October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2022). Screening for Depression and Suicide Risk in Children and Adolescents: US Preventive Services Task Force Recommendation Statement. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.16946>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Note: Grade B: recommendation grades, not convertible numbers; US guidance with no domestic counterpart. Positive screens mean re-assessment, never diagnosis. Suicidal thoughts: Section 1, item 25; household response: Section 29.

### 9. Never buy products or services claiming to "cure myopia" or "reduce diopters"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free — and it saves the money.
- In plain terms: The market regulator states plainly: "under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured." Marketing "recovery," "diopter reduction," "myopia cure" draws strict enforcement. See those words, exclude — no price comparison needed. Eye-care devices are medical devices, and platforms must vet sellers.
- Benefit: The regulator's November 2021–March 2022 campaign notice states: "under current medical technology, myopia cannot be cured," ordering strict enforcement against "rehabilitation," "recovery," "diopter reduction," "myopia cure," "myopia nemesis," and similar misleading claims; against TCM-name-borrowing deception; against false advertising with patient before-after imagery or efficacy guarantees; and requiring platforms to verify qualifications for eye devices (national)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 市场监管总局办公厅 (2021). 关于开展儿童青少年近视防控产品违法违规商业营销宣传专项整治行动的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-11/12/content_5650400.htm>
- Note: Grade B: enforcement criteria, not product trials. But "myopia cannot be cured" agrees across regulators and health authorities — "preventable, controllable, irreversible." The two evidenced measures: outdoor (item 4) and proper refraction and follow-up (item 12). Blue-light glasses: Section 6.

### 10. Sleep, homework, exercise, and rankings carry written rules — schools falling short can be pressed
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free. Know the rules; raise them with the school or education authority when needed. The hard part: raising it.
- In plain terms: Written: primary students sleep 10 hours, junior high 9, senior high 8; primary mornings no earlier than 8:20, secondary 8:00; 2025 adds 2-plus daily exercise hours and no exam-score ranking. A chronically sleep-short child gets checked against these first — never blamed for dawdling first.
- Benefit: The sleep-management notice: primary 10 hours, junior 9, senior 8; primary start no earlier than 8:20, secondary 8:00; no required early arrival for unified instruction; bedtimes 21:20, 22:00, 23:00; training institutions end by 20:30, online live training by 21:00; no game service to minors 22:00–08:00 (national; March 2021). The 2025 Ten Measures add: daily comprehensive physical activity no less than 2 hours, encouraged "15-minute recesses," no exam-score ranking, no mechanical-repetitive or punitive homework, and a weekly "no-homework day" (national; October 2025)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部办公厅 (2021). 关于进一步加强中小学生睡眠管理工作的通知（教基厅函〔2021〕11 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/02/content_5597443.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Note: Both are departmental documents with wildly varying local execution — hence B. Their use: knowing what schools already owe, with grounds in hand when you talk. Adult sleep accounts: Sections 2–3.

### 11. A child at the breaking point can suspend schooling — enrollment preserved, up to one year
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free. Parents or guardians apply to the school. The hard part: accepting a pause.
- In plain terms: Suspension is a formal institution: guardians apply, schools confirm, superiors approve; one application runs to a year, renewable; enrollment stays preserved; return resumes where due. Provincial details govern — ask the school's academic office or the education bureau.
- Benefit: The Student Enrollment Measures, Article 16: guardians apply; schools confirm and report for approval; return processed promptly; one suspension no more than a year, renewed by reapplication; enrollment preserved throughout, with provincial implementation rules (national; January 2025). The Ten Measures add referral-to-treatment and post-recovery re-enrollment mechanisms and campus crisis-response plans
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 教育部 (2025). 中小学生学籍管理办法（教基〔2025〕1 号，第十六条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002620.htm>；教育部办公厅 (2025). 进一步加强中小学生心理健康工作十条措施（教基厅〔2025〕2 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202510/content_7045528.htm>
- Note: Ask three things first: what medical proof, how enrollment records the period, and whether return means same grade or one down. Grade B: conditions, materials, and re-entry are provincial; nationally only the frame. The School Protection Provisions require registering dropout, suspension, and long-leave students — suspension is routine, never a special exception.

### 12. Poor vision found: hospital cycloplegic refraction, then follow-up at the doctor's intervals
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Tens to hundreds for testing and glasses; dilated children face glare and blurred near vision for half a day to two.
- In plain terms: Cycloplegic refraction drops the eye's focusing muscle loose before measuring — hospital-based and the most accurate myopia diagnosis. The health commission states the drops don't harm healthy eyes and effects reverse. Never take an optical shop's single autorefraction as diagnosis, never withhold glasses fearing "worse numbers."
- Benefit: The Ten Points: cycloplegia with a ciliary-muscle paralytic followed by refraction is the "gold standard" in-hospital medical refraction; the drops don't damage healthy eyes, with transient glare and near blur reversing on stopping. Confirmed myopia gets timely correction — proper glasses correct blur and slow progression. Follow-up with normal corrected vision: preschool and primary every 3–6 months, secondary every 6–12; refractive screening at 1–3, 4–6, and past 7 monitors hyperopia reserve (national; July 2023)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 防控儿童青少年近视核心知识十条（国卫办妇幼函〔2023〕278 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6894284.htm>
- Note: Grade B: official guidance without trial figures. High myopia leads to retinal detachment — follow-up isn't only new glasses. "Poor vision" on school physicals is screening; full eye exams follow (item 7).

### 13. Permanent molars in: pit and fissure sealants
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Tens to one-two hundred yuan per tooth; no anesthesia, no drilling, minutes; many regions run free programs for age-eligible children.
- In plain terms: Sealants resin-fill the chewing-surface grooves so debris can't lodge. Two years on, decay odds run about an eighth of unsealed: 16% of unsealed surfaces decayed versus about 5% sealed. Still effective at four years.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooling 38 trials, 7,924 children aged 5–16, resin sealants versus none: first permanent molars at 24 months OR 0.12 (95% CI 0.08–0.19) — an eighth the odds — from 7 trials, 1,548 randomized and 1,322 examined, moderate certainty. Control caries at 16% of surfaces: sealed 5.2% (3.13–7.37%); at 40%: 6.25%; at 70%: 19%. At 48–54 months OR 0.21 (0.16–0.28) — a fifth. Conclusion: 11%–51% fewer caries at 24 months; no adverse events across 4 trials recording them; evidence insufficient between materials
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. (2017). Pit and fissure sealants for preventing dental decay in permanent teeth. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001830.pub5>
- Note: First permanent molars erupt around 6, second around 12; seal freshly erupted, undecayed teeth — caried ones get fillings. Many regions list age-eligible sealants free — ask the CDC or community health center; no verbatim national document was found, so no coverage or age details. Caries rides the physical's guidance list (item 7).

### 14. A child loving games: watch whether sleep, homework, and outdoor life got displaced — not just hours played
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=small metric=time -->
- Cost: Free. Check weekly: bedtime, homework in, outdoor time.
- In plain terms: True "gaming disorder" runs two or three per hundred; low-threshold adolescent-questionnaire surveys inflate it. Game companies' real telemetry shows playtime barely moves well-being. Minors legally play 20:00–21:00, Fridays, weekends, and holidays.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 53 studies across 17 countries, 226,247 people: gaming-disorder prevalence 3.05% (95% CI 2.38–3.91); strictly sampled studies 1.96% — variance driven 77% by questionnaire choice, with adolescents, low cutoffs, and small samples inflating. The telemetry study linked six weeks of real playtime for 38,935 players across 7 companies with self-reported well-being: nearly no evidence of playtime affecting well-being. The publishing regulator restricts minors to 1 hour, 20:00–21:00, Fridays, weekends, and statutory holidays (national / international; since 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Stevens MW, Dorstyn D, Delfabbro PH, King DL. (2021). Global prevalence of gaming disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry, 55(6), 553–568. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0004867420962851>；Vuorre M, Johannes N, Magnusson K, Przybylski AK. (2022). Time spent playing video games is unlikely to impact well-being. Royal Society Open Science, 9(7), 220411. <https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220411>；国家新闻出版署 (2021). 关于进一步严格管理切实防止未成年人沉迷网络游戏的通知（国新出发〔2021〕14 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-09/01/content_5634661.htm>
- Note: Grade B: prevalence swings with instruments and the telemetry study ran six weeks. Gaming's commonest cost is displaced sleep and outdoor time — sleep rules (item 10), outdoors (item 4), screens (item 5). Charges and refunds: Section 5, item 9. Grades, sleep, and family relations collapsing together, unresponsive to persuasion: psychiatry or child psychology — never locked-down anti-addiction schools (item 18). Beneficiary: the child.


### 15. A child says they're gay: no scolding, no eviction, no "conversion" — the household's stance tracks suicide
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part: parents digesting their own feelings without harsh words in the moment.
- In plain terms: Among Chinese middle-schoolers, same-sex- or both-attracted children ran 3–5.5× others' one-year attempted-suicide rates. Abroad, adolescents strongly rejected at home attempted far more; those sent to "conversion" most of all.
- Benefit: The domestic survey covered 506 middle schools across 7 provinces, 150,822 students. Past-year attempted suicide among boys: 2.2% opposite-sex-attracted, 6.9% same-sex, 12.2% both; girls 3.1%, 8.9%, 10.9% — roughly 3–5.5× after adjusting for age, academic pressure, family economics, and bullying: boys OR 3.13 (95% CI 2.28–4.28) and 3.83 (2.85–5.14). The 35-study pool ran sexual-minority adolescents' attempts at OR 3.50 (2.98–4.12). ### 16. Discipline without hitting — and without yelling or "stupid": frequently hit or berated children run more behavioral and emotional problems
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard parts: stopping hand and mouth in the flash of anger, then replacing the method — the replacement in item 17.
- In plain terms: Frequently spanked children run more aggression and psychological problems, more estrangement, and more severe abuse. Whether spanking buys immediate compliance, evidence can't say. Thirteen-year-olds routinely yelled at or called stupid run more misconduct and depression later — parental warmth doesn't offset it.
- Benefit: Spanking first: a meta-analysis restricted to open-handed swatting pooled 75 studies, 111 effects, 160,927 children — 13 of 17 outcomes significantly and uniformly negative: childhood aggression d = 0.37 (95% CI 0.13–0.61), externalizing 0.41, internalizing 0.24, mental-health problems 0.53, poor parent relationship 0.51, low self-esteem 0.15, lower cognition 0.17, physical abuse by parents 0.64, adult antisocial behavior 0.36, adult mental-health problems 0.24; pooled d = 0.33 (0.29–0.38); immediate compliance unlinked (d = 0.14, −0.19–0.47, five effects). The narrative review of 69 prospective studies: physical punishment consistently predicts worsening behavior, with no long-term benefit; 7 dose studies — 5 finding more hits, worse outcomes. Yelling: 976 two-parent families, harsh verbal discipline at 13 predicting misconduct and depressive-symptom increases at 13–14 regardless of parental warmth — bidirectional, as misbehavior predicts later yelling too. Legally: the Anti-Domestic-Violence Law names beating, binding, maiming, confinement, and habitual berating and intimidation; Article 12 demands civilized parenting; committees must dissuade violations, and authorities may reprimand and order parenting guidance (national)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Gershoff ET, Grogan-Kaylor A. (2016). Spanking and child outcomes: Old controversies and new meta-analyses. Journal of Family Psychology, 30(4), 453–469. <https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000191>；Heilmann A, Mehay A, Watt RG, et al. (2021). Longitudinal links between fathers' and mothers' harsh verbal discipline and adolescents' conduct problems and depressive symptoms. So the counter-side allows: rare conditional swats may not underperform alternatives; heavy, habitual hitting and berating lose by consensus. Occasional swats or a shout don't constitute statutory domestic violence — the law names beating and habitual berating or intimidation. Beneficiary: mainly the child. Others hitting your child: Section 8, item 43 (protection orders). Witnessing chronic abuse: anyone may dissuade or report to police, civil affairs, or education — for strangers' households, 110 or the residents' committee; never intervene physically door-to-door.

### 17. A hard-to-manage child and a self that can't stop hitting or yelling: take a parent class teaching concrete methods
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: School, kindergarten, and community parent schools are public and free. The evidenced classes run weekly or biweekly, one to two hours, four to twenty-four weeks, with home practice. The hard part: finishing and practicing daily.
- In plain terms: Classes teaching concrete management cut children's hitting and rule-breaking and improve parents' moods — in randomized trials. Parents' own hitting and yelling fall too, most right after the course, persisting smaller at one to two years. Shanghai trials worked as well.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 13 trials — 10 randomized, 1,078 parents of 3–12-year-olds with conduct problems: child behavior improved, parent-rated SMD −0.53 (95% CI −0.72 to −0.34), independently rated −0.44 (−0.77 to −0.11); parental mental health −0.36 (−0.52 to −0.20); negative and harsh discipline reduced, parent-rated −0.77 (−0.96 to −0.59), independently −0.42 (−0.67 to −0.16) — short-term effects per the authors. The 346-trial screen's 60 trials measuring abusive discipline across 22 countries: abuse down at course end d = −0.46 (−0.59 to −0.33); 1–6 months −0.24; 7–24 months −0.18 — shrinking with time, mostly self-reported. Domestic trials: 81 Shanghai parents randomized to group Triple P beat waitlist on child adjustment, parenting, and mood, holding at 6 months; 191 rural caregivers in a self-directed course plus two group discussions cut abusive behavior about 33% (IRR 0.67) versus booklet-only at two weeks, with child behavior improving by three months (d = −0.40). The Family Education Promotion Law directs parental learning, parent schools at schools, kindergartens, and communities — free, nonprofit, with provincial-plus online schools and hotlines (national; since 2022-01-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Furlong M, McGilloway S, Bywater T, Hutchings J, Smith SM, Donnelly M. (2012). ### 18. Never send a child into locked-down "anti-addiction schools" or "training camps": the law bars harmful methods, and children have died in confinement
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — and it saves the tuition. The hard part: holding back at the child's worst, refusing to hand them to outsiders for "closed management."
- In plain terms: The law flatly bars methods harming minors' health to treat internet addiction. A Hefei special-training school shackled a newly admitted boy in a confinement room; about 44 hours later he was dead. Nanchang's Yuzhang Academy confined students; its principals were imprisoned, and courts refused parents' tuition refunds.
- Benefit: The Minors Protection Law, Article 68: no organization or individual may intervene in minors' internet addiction by means harming their physical or mental health. Both cases carry judgments. First, Hefei, August 2017: an 18-year-old's parents signed with a training school to cure gaming addiction; staff forcibly removed him into a confinement room, shackled his wrists to the window bars, restricted food, water, and rest, and beat him; roughly 44 hours later he died — heat, restrained posture, deprivation, and trauma causing fatal water-electrolyte disturbance. On 2018-10-31 Hefei Intermediate Court sentenced the school head to 16 years for intentional injury and illegal confinement, four instructors from 1 year to 8 years 6 months; the Anhui High Court upheld on 2018-12-28, noting the school lacked approval and the defendants held no teaching credentials. Second, Nanchang: Yuzhang Academy operated 2013–2017; on 2023-04-28 the Anyuan District court convicted five of illegal confinement — four sentences from 2 years 10 months to 11 months with education-employment bans, one exempted — and refused the parents' tuition and travel claims (national / Anhui / Jiangxi)
