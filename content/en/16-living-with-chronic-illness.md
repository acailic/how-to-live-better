# 16. Living with a chronic illness

Metrics: overall mortality and money. A chronic-diagnosis life often runs decades more; this section manages those decades.

### 1. Take the full course as prescribed — never stop because you feel better
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — it saves future complication money. Daily dosing takes minutes; the hard part is never missing a day.
- In plain terms: Those completing their medication ran about half the death risk of sporadic takers over the same period. Discount that figure: even placebo-completers died less, meaning part of the benefit is "people who stick to pills take better care generally." But stopping's harms are certain — antihypertensives, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs above all.
- Benefit: Pooling 21 studies, 46,847 people (meta-analysis): versus sporadic takers, full-course takers ran about 44% lower death risk. Original value odds ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.50–0.63 (the confidence range). Restricted to genuinely effective treatments, about 45% lower (0.55, 0.49–0.62)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Simpson SH, Eurich DT, Majumdar SR, et al. (2006). A meta-analysis of the association between adherence to drug therapy and mortality. BMJ, 333(7557), 15. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.38875.675486.55>
- Note: Contested — this is the book's most discount-worthy number. In the same pool, even placebo-completers died less (0.56, 0.43–0.74), marking a real healthy-adherer effect. But stopping's harms stand clear, antihypertensives, anticoagulants, and anti-rejection drugs especially

### 2. Outpatient chronic/special-disease certification first, cross-region registration second — hypertension, diabetes, chemo-radiation, dialysis, and anti-rejection settle directly out of province
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One cross-region filing on the insurance app before departure, done from the phone.
- In plain terms: Hypertension, diabetes, malignant-tumor outpatient chemo-radiation, uremia dialysis, and post-transplant anti-rejection — these five also settle insurance directly at out-of-town clinics: no fronting money and reimbursing back home. But first the outpatient chronic/special-disease certification at your enrollment locality, then the cross-region registration — both together work.
- Benefit: The NHSA document says it's been done: "basically achieved county-level accessibility of cross-province direct settlement for the five outpatient chronic/special diseases — hypertension, diabetes, malignant-tumor outpatient chemo-radiation, uremia dialysis, and post-organ-transplant anti-rejection treatment." "County-level accessible" means handleable at county level. The document adds five more diseases on top
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家医保局办公室、财政部办公厅 (2024). 关于稳妥有序扩大跨省直接结算门诊慢特病病种范围的通知（医保办发〔2024〕19 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202409/content_6974467.htm>
- Note: The precondition is both filings — chronic/special certification first, cross-region registration second. The five added diseases roll out unevenly; your local insurance office's publications govern.

### 3. Follow-ups at the doctor's intervals; log every result in one notebook
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Each check runs tens to hundreds of yuan; hospital trips and waiting take time. The hard part: recurring visits keep slipping.
- In plain terms: Chronic disease watches change over time, not single readings. Log dates, values, current drugs and doses in one place. Changing hospitals, you show it — no repeating tests, no repeating costs.
- Benefit: Chronic disease reads how values move over time; single readings don't suffice. One log adds the switching-hospital benefit: no duplicate testing, no duplicate spending
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献
- Note: Record at least four things per visit: date, values, drugs then in use, doses. Photographing lab slips works — keep them in one place, not scattered.

### 4. Never drop regular treatment to try folk remedies or supplements
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — saves the supplement money too. No time. The hard part: holding out against repeated urging.
- In plain terms: Stopping medication's harm is certain; remedies' and supplements' benefits are not. That trades a certain loss for an uncertain gain. Want to try supplements? Tell your attending physician first — some affect blood levels of anticoagulants and anti-rejection drugs.
- Benefit: Stopping's harm is certain; folk remedies' benefit is not. Section 6 lists the common no-effect categories
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；相关证据见第 6 节
- Note: Tell the attending physician before trying supplements — some interact with prescriptions, anticoagulant and anti-rejection blood levels included.

### 5. Stable chronic patients: community clinics can dispense 12 weeks at once
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free. One question at the community health center — whether long-term prescriptions are available.
- In plain terms: Stable condition, unchanged medication: community centers dispense up to 12 weeks at once, sparing the monthly half-day of hospital queuing. Within the same insurance region, primary-hospital reimbursement runs about ten points above the next tier.
- Benefit: No monthly hospital round-trips for refills. Within one insurance pooling area, primary-hospital reimbursement exceeds the next tier by about 10 percentage points in principle
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「对于符合条件的慢性病患者，基层医疗卫生机构单次可开具不超过12周用药的长期处方。」；同文「原则上统筹地区内医疗卫生机构住院报销比例逐级拉开10个百分点左右的差距」，并要求上级医院在基层开设高血压、糖尿病、慢性阻塞性肺疾病等常见病慢性病门诊；同文「三级医院要聚焦急危重症和疑难复杂疾病，加强转诊会诊和住院服务，逐步酌减常见病复诊和诊断明确、病情稳定的慢性病等普通门诊」，「并将专家团队普通门诊向基层医疗卫生机构延伸」
- Note: "Qualified" is the doctor's judgment — generally clear diagnosis plus stable regimen. Same drugs, spared monthly half-days and registration fees. When the primary tier lacks your drug, ask about shortage registration and delivery. The same document phases tertiary hospitals out of routine refill clinics and moves the same specialists' clinics into communities — using the big hospital as a refill point gets harder.

### 6. Before signing with a community family doctor, ask what insurance covers and what's self-paid
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One extra question before signing, a minute.
- In plain terms: The basic service package bills insurance per regulation; personalized packages pay entirely out of pocket. When someone pitches an "upgrade package," ask: basic or personalized, filed or not, how much a year.
- Benefit: Basic service packages are insurance-paid per regulation; personalized packages are fully self-paid and must file with the county health authority
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院办公厅 (2026). 关于加快建设分级诊疗体系的若干措施. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>：「加强基层门诊付费与签约服务政策联动，基本服务包按规定纳入医保支付；个性化服务包由签约基层医疗卫生机构按程序向县级卫生健康部门备案，费用由个人支付。」
- Note: Signing's value is someone long tracking your condition — not how many bundled items. Three questions on any "upgrade" pitch: basic or personalized, filed or not, annual cost.

### 7. On diabetes diagnosis: one dilated fundus exam, then per the doctor's intervals; feet checked yearly
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A dilated fundus exam runs tens to hundreds of yuan; foot exams usually ride inside the consult. The hard part: remembering while eyes and feet still feel fine.
- In plain terms: Diabetes damages retina and feet almost silently before blindness or ulceration. Early finds are treatable. Type 2: one dilated exam at diagnosis; type 1: starting 5 years after onset. One or two clear exams with on-target glucose stretch to every 1–2 years. Feet yearly.
- Benefit: Retinal damage is retinopathy; foot damage is the diabetic foot — both nearly symptomless before trouble. Early finds are treatable; waiting for blindness or rot is too late
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 美国糖尿病学会 (2026). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2026·第 12 章 视网膜病变、神经病变与足部护理. Diabetes Care："People with type 2 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist at the time of the diabetes diagnosis." "Adults with type 1 diabetes should have an initial dilated and comprehensive eye examination by an ophthalmologist or optometrist 5 years after the onset of diabetes." "If there is no evidence of retinopathy from one or more annual eye exams and glycemic indicators are within the goal range, then screening every 1–2 years may be considered." "Perform a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations." <https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012>
- Note: Type 2 screens at diagnosis because it has usually simmered for years; type 1 starts 5 years in. Clear exams with on-target glucose stretch to 1–2 years. Dulled foot sensation: daily sole checks, mirrors included.

### 8. After kidney stones: drink to 2.5–3 liters daily, salt under 6 grams
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Making water ever-available habitual; sweaty days demand more. The hard part: daily remembering.
- In plain terms: First-time calcium-stone formers doing nothing re-form at about one in four within five years. Adequate daily drinkers recur at just over one in ten — more than half off. The UK guideline: 2.5–3 liters of water daily, salt under 6 grams.
- Benefit: A 5-year randomized trial took 199 first-time idiopathic calcium-stone patients: one arm drank at least 2 liters daily, the other nothing. Five-year recurrence: 12/99 (12.1%) versus 27/100 (27.0%), P=0.008 — smaller P meaning less likely chance. Mean recurrence interval: 38.7±13.2 versus 25.1±16.4 months, P=0.016. Baseline 24-hour urine ran markedly lower in stone formers: men 1,057±238 versus 1,401±562 mL (P<0.0001); women 990±230 versus 1,239±440 mL (P<0.001). NICE NG118, 1.8.1: adults 2.5–3 liters daily, children and adolescents 1–2 by age; fresh lemon juice fine, no carbonated drinks, salt under 6 grams
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Borghi L, Meschi T, Amato F, Briganti A, Novarini A, Giannini A (1996). Urinary volume, water and recurrences in idiopathic calcium nephrolithiasis: a 5-year randomized prospective study. The Journal of Urology, 155(3), 839-843. <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5347(01)66321-3>；National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2019). Renal and ureteric stones: assessment and management. NICE guideline NG118，第 1.8.1 条. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng118/chapter/Recommendations>
- Note: About 55% fewer recurrences, free — by this book's ordering, the section's best value. Watch 24-hour urine volume, target above 2–2.5 liters, not intake. Heavy sweaters and hot-environment workers drink more. The trial covered first-episode idiopathic calcium stones; uric-acid, infection, and cystine stones take their own approaches — recurrent formers deserve stone analysis and 24-hour urinalysis before adding drugs. Never quit calcium to prevent stones — Section 6, item 21. Acute flank colic means hospital, not flushing; painless hematuria has its own workup — Section 1, item 27.

### 9. Diagnosed gout: stay on urate-lowering drugs long-term, holding uric acid below 360 µmol/L
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Allopurinol runs a few to a few dozen yuan monthly; early titration checks every few weeks, stable phases every few months; the first 3–6 months add a flare-preventing drug. The hard part: continuing while painless.
- In plain terms: Gout means long-term urate-lowering below 360 µmol/L — not painkillers only when it hurts. Nurse-led titration reached 95% at target by two years versus 30% usual care; hitting target never licenses stopping. Diet alone can't reach the line — quitting alcohol lowers uric acid just 1.6 mg/dL.
- Benefit: A randomized trial of 517 adults flared within 12 months: the nurse-led arm, educating patients and titrating to target, versus usual GP care. At 2 years, uric acid below 360 µmol/L (6 mg/dL): 95% versus 30% — target likelihood 3.18× usual (RR 3.18, 95% CI 2.42–4.18), P<0.0001. Year-2 flare frequency, tophi, and quality of life all favored the arm; each quality-adjusted life-year cost £5,066. The follow-up study, 438 surveyed, 82% returned: median flares in the past 12 months — 0 (IQR 0–0) in the target arm versus 1 (0–3), P<0.001 (the bracketed range being the interquartile). Still on urate-lowering drugs: 1.19× usual (adjusted RR 1.19, 1.09–1.30). The ACR 2020 gout guideline strongly recommends starting urate-lowering for three kinds: subcutaneous tophi, imaging-visible bone erosion, and 2+ flares yearly — continuous monitoring titrated to target, below 6 mg/dL. Allopurinol first-line, stage-3-plus CKD included, starting ≤100 mg/day, with anti-inflammatory prophylaxis 3–6 months at initiation. The same guideline cites a case series: well-controlled patients stopping drugs with uric acid still under 7 mg/dL — only 13% (27/211) went five years without a flare. Diet: alcohol-limiting runs 1.6 mg/dL lower than unrestricted; a beer raises uric acid 0.16 mg/dL; healthy, Mediterranean, and DASH diets matter less. First-choice drug splits: the ACR strongly favors allopurinol — effective at full dose, well tolerated, safe, cheap — and conditionally recommends switching febuxostat in cardiovascular history or new events when alternatives exist, matching the FDA's boxed warning. One basis, the CARES trial: 6,190 gout patients with cardiovascular disease randomized — febuxostat ran all-cause death at 1.22× allopurinol (HR 1.22, 95% CI 1.01–1.47, about 22% higher), cardiovascular death 1.34× (1.03–1.73, about 34% higher). Europe's FAST trial disagreed: 6,128 patients over 60 with cardiovascular risk factors — febuxostat showed no more major cardiovascular events (HR 0.85, 0.70–1.03), deaths 7.2% versus 8.6%. For uricosuric users, the ACR conditionally advises against alkalinization — no evidence of effect, very low certainty — while insisting on fluids and barring the class with kidney stones or stage-3-plus CKD
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Doherty M, Jenkins W, Richardson H, et al. (2018). Efficacy and cost-effectiveness of nurse-led care involving education and engagement of patients and a treat-to-target urate-lowering strategy versus usual care for gout: a randomised controlled trial. Lancet, 392(10156), 1403-1412. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)32158-5>；Abhishek A, Jenkins W, La-Crette J, Fernandes G, Doherty M (2020). Nurse-led care is preferred over GP-led care of gout and improves gout outcomes: results of Nottingham Gout Treatment Trial follow-up study. Rheumatology, 59(3), 575-579. <https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez333>；FitzGerald JD, Dalbeth N, Mikuls T, et al. (2020). 2020 American College of Rheumatology Guideline for the Management of Gout. Arthritis Care & Research, 72(6), 744-760. <https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.24180>；White WB, Saag KG, Becker MA, et al.; CARES Investigators (2018). Cardiovascular Safety of Febuxostat or Allopurinol in Patients with Gout. New England Journal of Medicine, 378(13), 1200-1210. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1710895>；Mackenzie IS, Ford I, Nuki G, et al.; FAST Study Group (2020). Long-term cardiovascular safety of febuxostat compared with allopurinol in patients with gout (FAST): a multicentre, prospective, randomised, open-label, non-inferiority trial. Lancet, 396(10264), 1745-1757. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32234-0>；中华医学会内分泌学分会 (2020). 中国高尿酸血症与痛风诊疗指南(2019). 中华内分泌代谢杂志, 36(1), 1-13. <https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.1000-6699.2020.01.001>
- Note: Contested: first-choice drug differs across guidelines — the US favors allopurinol with febuxostat swaps for cardiovascular patients, while European FAST saw no added danger. Benzbromarone, a uricosuric, remains available domestically; the Chinese Medical Association's 2019 and 2024 guidelines give separate recommendations on drug choice and alkalinization. Whether benzbromarone pairs with sodium bicarbonate, the US guideline finds no evidence — follow your physician, never skipping the water. **Han Chinese starting allopurinol should test HLA-B\*5801 first**: a congenital immune-response genotype, tested once, valid for life — carried by 7.4% of Han, Korean, and Thai populations versus 0.7% of whites and Hispanics; Asian and Black allopurinol users run 3× the severe hypersensitivity risk, potentially fatal with massive skin peeling; the ACR 2020 guideline suggests considering pre-testing both groups. **Starting urate-lowering provokes flares** — the guideline mandates 3–6 months of anti-inflammatory prophylaxis; "it hurt more, so I stopped" is the classic failure. When pain comes, add anti-inflammatories per orders — never stop the urate-lowering. Targets differ in writing: <6 mg/dL ≈ 360 µmol/L, with Chinese lab slips favoring µmol/L. Checkup-found high uric acid without ever flaring is another matter — Section 6, item 19. Sugary drinks and alcohol: Section 2, items 7 and 19.
