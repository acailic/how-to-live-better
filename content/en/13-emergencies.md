# 13. Emergencies: what to do first

This section covers what to do first, then next, and what never to do. First-aid and wilderness entries count specific causes of death and survival rates; threats and scams count money and personal freedom — the two are never converted into each other. Entries run by "how common × how many lives turn on doing it right" descending. These entries serve first you and your spouse and immediate family — 79.2% of cardiac arrests happen at home, and the person you're most likely to save is family. Friends and colleagues second. Strangers last — lowest benefit tier, with extortion and entanglement risks — so self-protection steps and liability clauses come first.

Only minutes-scale actions belong here. Spotting scams and being squeezed over held leverage take days — see Section 8, item 3 (hard anti-fraud rules) and item 32 (leverage extortion). Money already transferred: call 110 or 96110 for a payment stop — Section 8, item 2.

Entries in this section are grouped by theme, with item numbers in parentheses.

**CPR and choking**: someone down and not breathing (item 1), elders fallen — don't rush to lift (item 2), choking and unable to speak (item 26), infants choking soundlessly (item 43), infant CPR (item 44).

**Sudden illness**: sudden facial droop, one-sided weakness (item 3), spinning dizziness and unsteadiness (item 4), one eye gone black (item 5), one eye swollen and aching (item 6), crushing chest pain (item 7), tearing pain (item 8), worst-ever headache (item 9), elder dullness weeks after head bump (item 10), one leg suddenly swollen (item 11), anaphylaxis (item 15), someone seizing (item 16), diabetic suddenly trembling (item 17).

**Trauma and burns**: major bleeding — press first (item 12), dog and cat bites (item 13), burns — cool water (item 14), embedded objects — don't pull (item 40), suspected fracture — immobilize (item 41).

**Poisoning, fire, accidents**: electric shock — cut power first (item 18), CO alarm sounding (item 19), swallowed cleaners, pesticides, medication (item 20), chemicals on skin (item 21), heat dizziness without sweat (item 22), heatstroke — weak salt water (item 23), fire — crawl low (item 24), someone drowning (item 25), earthquake — check the building first (item 30).

**Wilderness**: lost in remote country — stay put (item 27), shivering and slurred speech — hypothermia (item 28), snakebite — no cutting, no sucking (item 29), bears, boars, wild dogs (item 31), thunderstorms outdoors (item 32), altitude — climb slowly (item 33), tick bites (item 34), wild water — boil first (item 35).

**Conflict and after helping**: robbed in the wild (item 36), stumbling into a brawl (item 37), injured or out-of-pocket from helping (item 39).

**Sexual assault and HIV exposure**: possible HIV exposure (item 38), after sexual assault (item 42).

### 1. Someone down and not breathing: press hard on the chest immediately; have a bystander call 120 and fetch the AED
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A Red Cross or hospital CPR course, half a day to a day, lasts years. Untrained works too: stack your hands on the midpoint between the nipples and press hard about twice a second, without stopping.
- In plain terms: The person you press is most likely family — 79.2% of cardiac arrests happen at home. With compressions, survival-to-discharge rises from 3.9% to 16.1%. Unresponsive and not breathing: press. Midpoint between nipples, stacked hands, twice a second, no stopping. Have a second person call 120 and get the AED; your hands don't stop.
- Benefit: Pooling 79 studies, 142,740 people (meta-analysis): out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survival-to-discharge totals just 7.6%. Bystander CPR raises it from 3.9% to 16.1%. Witnessed onset covered 53%; actual bystander compression, only 32%. China's BASIC-OHCA registry logged 38,227 emergency cases, of which 30,282 (79.2%) arrested at home; bystander compression 20.3%, survival-to-discharge 1.2%. The order: compress first, then have the second person call and fetch the AED. When the machine powers on, follow its voice
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sasson C et al. (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576> ; Zheng J et al. (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1> ; 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: Children under 1 take a different technique with breaths — item 44. No philtrum-pinching, no hunting family first, no filming. And don't go gentle for fear of breaking ribs. Civil Code Article 184: voluntary emergency aid that injures the rescued owes no compensation.

### 2. Elder fallen, someone down: kneel, call to them, dial 120 — don't rush to lift; for strangers, walking away is lawful in China, and stopping means never manhandling
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Kneeling, asking, calling: minutes. The hard part: resisting the helping hand. With strangers, add the account: 120 logs your number, callbacks and one questioning session possible; family pursuit means months of litigation with attorney fees yours even on winning.
- In plain terms: This entry's main line is your own elders and acquaintances. Check breathing first; absent, compress per item 1. Breathing: don't lift them either — the fall may follow a stroke or faint, and hoisting worsens it. Kneel, call, ask whether they remember falling. Back pain or anything fracture-like: move nothing, call 120 in place.
- Benefit: The health ministry's fall-intervention guide states two sentences: "falls are the leading cause of injury death among Chinese over 65" and "on finding an elder fallen, do not rush to lift — handle by situation." Unconscious: call emergency immediately. Vomiting: head to the side, clear mouth and nose. Breathing and pulse stopped: chest compressions. "If moving is needed, keep it smooth, lying flat as far as possible." Conscious: ask about memory of the fall — "if unable to recall, possibly syncope or cerebrovascular accident." Severe headache, facial droop, slurred speech, limb weakness: "lifting immediately may aggravate cerebral hemorrhage or ischemia." Fracture signs, back pain, incontinence: "without relevant expertise, do not move." All such cases: call emergency. Elders wanting to rise: assist slowly, then "rest sitting or lying and observe, leaving only after confirming no harm." On the extortion fear, the law: the Supreme Court's civil-procedure interpretation, Article 90 — parties prove the facts their claims rest on, and the burdened party bears adverse consequences when evidence fails. Civil Code Article 1198: operators and managers of hotels, malls, banks, stations, airports, stadiums, and entertainment venues, and mass-activity organizers, bear tort liability for harm from unmet safety duties. Article 184: "voluntary emergency aid causing harm to the aided bears no civil liability." The Supreme Court's published typical case: a pharmacy owner's CPR broke the rescued person's ribs — 18 days hospitalized, 9,000-odd yuan claimed — the court held the rescuer free of liability
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 卫生部 (2011). 老年人跌倒干预技术指南（「老年人跌倒后的处理」）. 全文 PDF 见广东省疾病预防控制中心 <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>，现场处理部分全文见贵南县人民政府转载 <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>；全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一百八十三、一百八十四条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2023). 人民法院抓实公正与效率践行社会主义核心价值观典型案例（案例十三，齐某某诉孙某某健康权纠纷案，辽宁省康平县人民法院）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>；最高人民法院 (2022 年第二次修正，2022 年 4 月 10 日施行). 关于适用《中华人民共和国民事诉讼法》的解释（第九十条）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>；民法典第一千一百九十八条同上最高检转载全文；全国人大常委会 (2023 年修正，2024 年 1 月 1 日施行). 民事诉讼法（第一百五十二、一百六十四条）. 上海市发展和改革委员会转载全文 <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>；国务院 (2006). 诉讼费用交纳办法（国务院令第 481 号，第六、二十九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2025). 治安管理处罚法（2025 年修订，2026 年 1 月 1 日起施行，第二十六条第一项、第五十条第五项）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>；德国联邦司法部. Strafgesetzbuch (StGB) § 323c Unterlassene Hilfeleistung; Behinderung von hilfeleistenden Personen. <https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__323c.html>
- Note: Grade B: the guide states handling without lives-saved numbers. Article 184 covers harm from the rescue itself — voluntary, emergent. "Loading them into your own car" isn't covered; waiting for 120 is safer. After dialing, stay on the line and follow the dispatcher. This judgment framework is for family and acquaintances first. For a stranger on the street, walking away is lawful and cheapest — Chinese law imposes no duty to rescue. Abroad differs: in Germany, failing to help at an accident when you can without great danger to yourself is itself a crime, up to 1 year or fine. Overseas, follow local law. Stopping carries layered bills: time, logged numbers, family pursuit, litigation, workplace fallout, being filmed and posted — see [docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md](../docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md). If you do stop, the one absolute is **never manhandle** — medically, hoisting can worsen cerebral hemorrhage or spinal injury. One exception: if you operate or manage the venue, or organized the event, walking away can itself be liability — Article 1198's safety duty. Fall prevention: Section 1, item 13. Delayed symptoms after head bumps: item 10 here. The costs of helping: item 39. Online-mobbing evidence: Section 8, item 16.
### 3. Sudden facial droop, one-sided arm weakness, slurred speech: call 120 immediately — no waiting, no driving yourself
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Three moves, learned in a glance: have them smile, raise both arms, say a full sentence.
- In plain terms: Droop, arm weakness, slurred speech: call 120. Clot-busting within 3 hours makes functional independence about 1.75 times as likely as no drug; by 3–4.5 hours, 1.26; past 4.5, no measurable difference. Note the exact onset time. No water, no pills, no self-driving.
- Benefit: Pooling individual data from 16 thrombolysis trials, 6,756 patients (meta-analysis). Intravenous thrombolysis — drugs reopening the blocked vessel — within 3 hours: odds ratio 1.75 for modified Rankin 0–1 (functionally independent), 95% CI 1.35–2.27; at 3–4.5 hours, 1.26 (1.05–1.51); past 4.5, 1.15 (0.95–1.40) — statistically indistinguishable
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929-1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>
- Note: Earlier is better; past 4.5 hours, essentially useless. Call 120 rather than drive: ambulances route to stroke centers with advance notice. Record the exact onset time — physicians need it for the thrombolysis decision. No water, no pills, no "rest and see."

### 4. Sudden spinning dizziness with unsteadiness, double vision, one eye darkened, or finger-to-nose misses: treat as stroke, call 120
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Beyond face-arms-speech (item 3), add two: have them stand with eyes closed and check swaying, diplopia, or field gaps; and self-test pointing finger to nose, eyes closed.
- In plain terms: Face-arms-speech alone misses one-in-ten-odd strokes — some begin without droop or weakness. Adding unsteadiness, double vision, and field gaps cuts misses to 4.4%. Sudden spinning unsteadiness, doubled vision, finger missing the nose: same 120, same time-logging.
- Benefit: A Kentucky stroke center reviewed 736 acute ischemic strokes (vessel-blockage strokes): 14.1% presented with none of facial droop, arm weakness, or slurred speech (FAST). Adding gait instability, leg weakness, and visual symptoms cut misses from 14.1% to 4.4%
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479-481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>
- Note: Cerebellar and brainstem (posterior-circulation) blockages often show no droop or weakness — just spinning dizziness, veering gait, diplopia, and choking on water and food, most easily misread as cervical spondylosis, low blood sugar, or BPPV. Finger-miss and eyes-closed sway mark cerebellar involvement. But no maneuver excludes stroke: sudden onset without relief means 120 and exact-time logging. Single-hospital chart review; the authors call for validation.

### 5. One eye blacks out like a curtain drawn — even if it clears in minutes: same-day emergency as stroke
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Emergency evaluation and vascular imaging take half a day to a day.
- In plain terms: One eye's curtain-blackout is the retinal supply blocked — the longer the block, the less retina savable. It's also cerebral ischemia, with stroke risk peaking in the following days. Same-day assessment and treatment cut 90-day stroke recurrence from 10.3% to 2.1%. Same-day emergency at a stroke center — not next week's ophthalmology clinic.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association's 2021 statement on central retinal artery occlusion (the retina's main vessel blocked) states it plainly: "acute central retinal artery occlusion is an emergency," to be triaged as such; the longer the ischemia, the less recoverable retina. Oxford's EXPRESS study covered minor stroke and transient ischemic attack (self-resolving brain ischemia): switching to same-day assessment and treatment cut 90-day stroke recurrence from 10.3% (32/310) to 2.1% (6/281)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282-e294. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366> ; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432-1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>
- Note: Grade B: EXPRESS wasn't randomized — before/after periods in one population, other conditions possibly shifting. Minutes-long monocular blackout is most often dismissed as fatigue or low sugar; it's a transient ischemic attack with the stroke window open right after. Painless, non-red, non-tearing monocular vision loss especially. Skip next-day ophthalmology — straight to a stroke-center emergency department.

### 6. One eye swollen, aching, red, halos around lights, with headache, nausea, vomiting: same-day ophthalmic emergency
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Emergency tonometry and anterior-chamber exam take half a day.
- In plain terms: This is acute angle-closure glaucoma: the eye's fluid can't drain and pressure spikes. The longer the delay, the more optic nerve crushed — permanently. During Wuhan's lockdown, patients averaged 241 hours to treatment with 21.87% blinded; the next year, 121 hours and 7.84%.
- Benefit: A Wuhan eye hospital compared two periods of acute primary angle-closure glaucoma: lockdown 2020, 54 patients/64 eyes; same period 2021, 46/51, demographically similar. Symptom-to-treatment: 241.84±211.95 hours versus 121.53±96.12 (P=0.001). Presentation intraocular pressure: 52.63±12.45 versus 45.16±9.79 mmHg (P=0.001). Blindness: 21.87% versus 7.84%. Glaucomatous optic neuropathy: 20/64 eyes (31.25%) versus 7/51 (13.73%) (P=0.03). A Korean hospital reviewed 50 eyes post-episode post-lens-extraction: one year on, 25 (50%) retained visual-field defects; longer symptom-to-pressure-control predicted defects (P=0.005). A prospective study sat 43 shallow-chamber people prone in a dark room for an hour: both eyes' pressures rose significantly (P<0.01) — the habitually used eye by a median 3.60 mmHg, the other 2.70 (P<0.05)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhou L, Wu S, Wang Y, Bao X, Peng T, Luo W, Ortega-Usobiaga J (2022). Clinical presentation of acute primary angle closure during the COVID-19 epidemic lockdown. Frontiers in Medicine, 9, 1078237. <https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2022.1078237> ; Sung MS, Kim HJ, Park SW (2023). Predictors of long-term visual field outcome after an episode of acute primary angle closure. Clinical & Experimental Ophthalmology, 51(4), 291-299. <https://doi.org/10.1111/ceo.14206> ; Wang J, Wang J, Ng TK, Huang C (2025). Asymmetric intraocular pressure changes in dominant and contralateral eyes: the dark room prone provocative test. Seminars in Ophthalmology, 40(4), 325-331. <https://doi.org/10.1080/08820538.2024.2443972>
- Note: Grade B twice over: the Wuhan comparison is retrospective with lockdown confounds inseparable from delay, and the Korean series is 50 eyes, retrospective. The triggers aren't screens but two things stacked: dark-room pupil dilation plus long head-down posture pushing the lens forward. Shallow anterior chambers are the vulnerability — hence the truly high-risk are over 50, hyperopic, with family history of angle closure. All-night gaming rarely triggers this. The commonest misreads: gastroenteritis, migraine, or hypertension — headache and vomiting outshine the eye. Painless non-red monocular blindness is another matter: item 5. Blue-light glasses: Section 6, item 16.

### 7. Crushing chest pain, tightness, pressure over 15 minutes unrelieved: call 120 — no toughing it out, no driving
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Crushing pain, tightness, pressure beyond 15 minutes: call 120. Vessel reopened within 55 minutes of arrival: 2.8% dead at 30 days; beyond 55 minutes, 9.7%. Every delay kills more heart muscle. No self-driving — losing consciousness en route is a second accident.
- Benefit: A national Israeli acute-coronary-syndrome survey analyzed ST-elevation myocardial infarction, measuring door-to-balloon (arrival to reopening the blocked vessel by balloon). Intervals ≤55 minutes: 30-day death 5/178 (2.8%); >55 minutes: 15/155 (9.7%)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Karkabi B, Meir G, Zafrir B, et al. (2021). Door-to-balloon time and mortality in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction. European Heart Journal - Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcaa037>
- Note: The study measures in-hospital delay, not onset-to-door — but the logic is identical: every minute kills more muscle. Pain may sit in the upper abdomen, back, or jaw, or present as left-arm numbness. Diabetics and the elderly may feel no pain, only breathlessness. No self-driving.

### 8. Sudden tearing pain migrating from chest to back to waist: call 120 and say "the pain is moving"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Tearing pain marching from chest to back to waist: on the 120 call, say "like being ripped open, and it's moving downward." Untreated, drug-only mortality runs 58%. Its management is the opposite of heart attack: only if the physician suspects it do they order contrast CT instead of anticoagulation and thrombolysis. Mention greatly differing arm blood pressures or a missing pulse on one side.
- Benefit: The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD) enrolled 464 cases across 12 centers. Among first-reported symptoms, sudden severe sharp pain predominated. In-hospital mortality 27.4% overall; type-A dissection managed on drugs alone, 58% mortality
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hagan PG, Nienaber CA, Isselbacher EM, et al. (2000). The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD): new insights into an old disease. JAMA, 283(7), 897-903. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.7.897>
- Note: Dissection and infarction manage oppositely: infarction wants blood thinners and clot-busters, which feed the dissection tear. So volunteer "tearing, moving downward" to steer the physician toward contrast CT. Divergent arm pressures and a missing pulse are hints. Poorly controlled hypertension, Marfan syndrome, and family history of aortic aneurysm or dissection raise the risk. Again: no driving, no waiting until tomorrow.

### 9. Sudden "worst headache of my life," peaking within an hour: straight to emergency head CT
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Emergency care itself costs little; head CT runs a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: What matters is how fast it peaked. Severe headache peaking within an hour: about 6% is a ruptured surface-brain vessel. Over 40, neck pain or stiffness, witnessed loss of consciousness, onset during activity, instantaneous explosion, or inability to flex the neck forward — any one, go for head CT. Most checked will be fine; one missed rebleed is death.
- Benefit: Ten Canadian emergency departments prospectively followed 2,131 thunderclap headaches — peaking within an hour — with predefined criteria. 132 (6.2%) were subarachnoid hemorrhage (surface-brain vessel rupture). The Ottawa SAH rule has six criteria: age 40+, neck pain or stiffness, witnessed syncope, onset during exertion, instantaneous explosion, and limited neck flexion on exam. Any one triggers workup. The rule caught every hemorrhage — sensitivity 100%, 95% CI 97.2%–100.0% — at the price of excluding only 15.3% of non-bleeders (specificity 15.3%)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248-1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018>
- Note: Not "how bad" but "how fast to worst" — seconds-to-a-minute explosions are the danger sign; slow-building migraines aren't. Vomiting, neck stiffness, photophobia, brief fainting heighten it. Specificity 15.3% means most workups clear — but a missed rebleed is death; worth checking. Don't swallow a painkiller and sleep it off.

### 10. Elder unsteady, dull, sleepy, or one-sided weak two weeks to months after a head bump: get a head CT
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Head CT runs 200–300 yuan.
- In plain terms: After an elder's head bump, the blood typically accumulates by the third week — the moment of impact can be entirely uneventful, even unremembered. Post-surgery recurrence reaches 33%, fatality 32%. Sudden confusion, unsteady gait, constant sleepiness, one weak hand: first ask about falls in the past two or three months, then CT.
- Benefit: The Chinese Neurosurgical Society's expert consensus on chronic subdural hematoma — blood slowly pooling between the meninges, technically a chronic collection between arachnoid and dura — states it "typically forms in the third week after head trauma," predominating in elders. Post-surgery recurrence up to 33%, fatality up to 32%; among patients over 90, only 24% recover well overall. Systematic review: global incidence 3.39–39.1 per 100,000 yearly, rising with age and tripling past 80. Commonest causes: trauma and falls, then chronic alcohol misuse, anticoagulants, and assault. Geriatric review: presentation "typically includes confusion, cognitive decline, gait disturbance, or drowsiness, potentially resembling other geriatric syndromes"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhang J et al. (2021). Expert consensus on drug treatment of chronic subdural hematoma. Chinese Neurosurgical Journal, 7(1), 47. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41016-021-00263-z> ; Dziho A et al. (2025). Global prevalence and incidence of chronic subdural hematoma: A systematic review. Brain and Spine, 5, 105893. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bas.2025.105893> ; Mathon B, Shotar E (2026). Diagnostic and therapeutic management of chronic subdural hematoma in elderly patients. Gériatrie et Psychologie Neuropsychiatrie du Vieillissement, 24(1), 30-41. <https://doi.org/10.1684/pnv.2026.1274>
- Note: The impact can be uneventful and unremembered. So on finding sudden confusion, unsteadiness, sleepiness, or a weak hand, first ask back two to three months for falls or head bumps, then CT. Don't file it under dementia or old-stroke residuals — misfiling delays. Aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin, or DOAC users run higher risk, as do heavy drinkers. Fall prevention: Section 1, item 13.

### 11. One leg suddenly swollen, tight, tender: care promptly; add sudden breathlessness or chest pain: call 120
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Leg venous ultrasound runs 100–200 yuan.
- In plain terms: One leg suddenly swollen, a ring bigger than the other, tender: get a leg vein ultrasound promptly. Then sudden breathlessness, chest pain on deep breath, racing heart, faintness — the clot may have reached the lungs: call 120. Diagnosed pulmonary embolism kills 17.4% within 3 months. Sitting over 4 hours roughly doubles the risk — still very low per person.
- Benefit: The International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER) enrolled 2,454 confirmed acute pulmonary embolism patients across 52 hospitals in 7 countries — pulmonary embolism being a clot blocking the lung artery. All-cause 3-month mortality: 17.4% (426/2454). WHO's WRIGHT project: travel over 4 consecutive hours roughly doubles venous thromboembolism risk — but per-person probability stays very low, sitting immobile over 4 hours "about one in six thousand"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Goldhaber SZ, Visani L, De Rosa M (1999). Acute pulmonary embolism: clinical outcomes in the International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER). Lancet, 353(9162), 1386-1389. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(98)07534-5> ; WHO (2007). Study results released on travel and blood clots. <https://www.who.int/news/item/29-06-2007-study-results-released-on-travel-and-blood-clots>
- Note: Both legs swelling together usually means heart, liver, kidney, or venous-valve trouble. One leg suddenly swollen a ring bigger than the other is the classic deep-vein thrombosis picture. Risk clusters: long car and plane rides, bed rest after surgery or fracture, casts, pregnancy and postpartum, cancer, oral contraceptives. A clot breaking loose and blocking the lung artery is pulmonary embolism: sudden breathlessness, worse chest pain on deep breath, racing heart, faintness, coughing blood — at that stage, call 120 directly. The 17.4% is mortality among diagnosed pulmonary embolism, not "leg swelling carries this risk" — don't frighten yourself with the number.

### 12. Major bleeding: press hard on the wound with your hands; limbs that resist pressure get the tourniquet — while calling 120
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A windlass tourniquet runs tens to 100-odd yuan — car and first-aid kit. Learning "above the wound, twist until bleeding stops, note the time" takes minutes.
- In plain terms: Press hard with hands or cloth first; uncontrolled limb bleeding gets the tourniquet; then 120 — once on, never loosen. Among battlefield casualties, tourniquets before shock survived nine in ten; after shock, one in ten; the five who needed but lacked them all died. Place above the wound, twist until bleeding stops, write the time. No shoelaces or cords. Neck and torso get pressure only, never tourniquets.
- Benefit: Eleven Texas level-1 trauma centers admitted 1,026 limb-vascular injuries over 6 years; 181 (17.6%) arrived with prehospital tourniquets. Unadjusted: 3.9% death with tourniquet, 5.2% without. After multivariable adjustment, tourniquets remained independently associated with survival — adjusted OR 5.86, 95% CI 1.41 to 24.47. A Baghdad field hospital's 232 casualties: tourniquet before shock, 90% survival; after shock, 10%; the five needing-but-lacking all died, the applied surviving 87%. Order: direct pressure with cloth or towel → uncontrolled limb bleeding, tourniquet → 120 → don't loosen
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Teixeira PGR et al. (2018). Civilian Prehospital Tourniquet Use Is Associated with Improved Survival in Patients with Peripheral Vascular Injury. - Note: No thin cords or shoelaces — they neither occlude arteries nor spare skin. Once on, no periodic loosening "to let blood through." Neck and torso: pressure only. Never forearm, calf, elbow, or knee. Aim for under 2 hours total to hospital, but unreachable with no alternative means: keep it on until proper rescue. Belt, tie, or sleeve tourniquets alone rarely hold; add a stick to wind tight.

### 13. Bitten or scratched by dog or cat: alternate soap and running water for 15 minutes, vaccinate the same day
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Washing is free; the full vaccine course runs a few hundred yuan; category-III exposure adds passive immunization (ready-made antibodies injected) at hundreds to a thousand-odd.
- In plain terms: Rabies is nearly 100% fatal once it declares itself — washing plus vaccine is the only road. Alternate soap and flowing water for 15 minutes, vaccinate the same day. Punctured bites or licks on broken skin are category III, adding passive immunization. "It's a family pet, vaccinated" changes nothing: grading follows the wound, not the dog's résumé.
- Benefit: The 2023 national handling standard, verbatim: "rabies case fatality approaches 100%." Management is "alternately and thoroughly flushing all bites and scratches with soap (or other weak alkaline cleanser) and pressurized running water for about 15 minutes." Light bites on unbroken skin or non-bleeding scratches are category II — vaccinate. Penetrating bites or licks on broken skin are category III — "treat the wound and inject rabies passive immunization and vaccine." WHO: about 59,000 rabies deaths yearly worldwide, 99% dog-transmitted
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家疾控局综合司、国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2023). 关于印发狂犬病暴露预防处置工作规范（2023 年版）的通知. <https://www.ndcpa.gov.cn/jbkzzx/c100014/common/content/content_1706569159854649344.html> ; WHO. 狂犬病 实况报道. <https://www.who.int/zh/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rabies>

### 14. Burns: cool running water for 20 minutes immediately — no toothpaste, no soy sauce
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. 20 minutes.
- In plain terms: Twenty minutes of cool running water within 3 hours cuts later graft needs to about 60% of uncooled. Cool, not ice — ice packs deepen injury. Clothing stuck to the wound: don't tear; after cooling, cover with clean cloth and go. No toothpaste, soy sauce, sesame oil, or flour. Remembered late? Cooling within 3 hours still counts.
- Benefit: An Australian cohort followed 2,495 burned children: 20 minutes of cool running water within 3 hours ran an odds ratio of 0.6 for grafting (95% CI 0.4–0.8) — about 60% of the uncooled rate. A pooled analysis of 7 studies concurred: "20 minutes of cool running water within three hours significantly reduced the need for grafting and surgical intervention"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Griffin BR, Frear CC, Babl F, Oakley E, Kimble RM (2020). Cool Running Water First Aid Decreases Skin Grafting Requirements in Pediatric Burns: A Cohort Study of Two Thousand Four Hundred Ninety-five Children. - Note: Cool water, not ice — ice worsens the injury. Stuck clothing: don't tear; cool, cover with clean cloth, go. No toothpaste, soy sauce, oil, or flour — they obscure depth assessment and invite infection. Late cooling within 3 hours still works.

### 15. Sudden whole-body hives plus breathlessness or faintness: treat as anaphylaxis — call 120 and say so explicitly
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Known severe allergies carry the cost of an epinephrine auto-injector — prescription-only.
- In plain terms: Whole-body hives plus breathlessness or faintness: treat as anaphylaxis. Call 120, say "suspected anaphylaxis" so the crew brings epinephrine. Antihistamines and steroids act too slowly to substitute. After one episode, get an auto-injector prescribed — mid-outer thigh, through clothing if need be.
- Benefit: Epinephrine is the first-line drug for grade-II-and-above anaphylaxis, strongly recommended by guidelines; antihistamines and steroids act too slowly to stand in
- Evidence grade: B (guideline strong recommendation over low-grade evidence)
- Sources: 中国医药教育协会等 (2022). 过敏性休克急救临床实践指南（2020）. - Note: The EAACI guideline itself states the field's evidence "remains at a very low level" — the strong recommendation reflects benefit far exceeding risk, not large trials. ### 16. Someone seizing and falling: clear the surroundings, roll them on their side, time it — put nothing in the mouth
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Move hard objects away, roll them on their side, mouth down, watch the clock. Don't restrain, don't pry the mouth, put nothing inside — that only breaks teeth and jaws. Over 5 minutes, recurrence soon after, unarousable or breathing poorly afterward, seizure in water, or first-ever seizure: ambulance. Water only once fully alert.
- Benefit: Avoid the tooth and jaw injuries of pried mouths and forced objects; avoid restraint fractures. Over 5 minutes is the red line demanding an ambulance
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 美国疾病控制与预防中心. 癫痫发作急救："Don't hold the person down or stop their movements" "Don't put anything in their mouth. This can hurt their teeth or jaw" "Don't offer water or food until they are fully alert" "Time the seizure to track if it lasts more than 5 minutes. If it does, seek immediate medical attention or call 911". <https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html>
- Note: The same page lists the other ambulance triggers: quick recurrence, unarousable or breathing poorly afterward, injury during the seizure, seizure in water, first-ever seizure. Afterward: side-lying, mouth down, water only when fully alert. Households with epilepsy patients use this most; the same moves on strangers return less benefit to you but carry no liability (Civil Code Article 184 — item 2 here, don't rush to lift).

### 17. Diabetic suddenly trembling, cold-sweating, slurring: give 15 grams of sugar, recheck in 15 minutes
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Carry sugar cubes or a small juice box.
- In plain terms: 15 grams of sugar — cubes or a small juice box. Recheck at 15 minutes; still low, repeat. Below 3.0 mmol/L is clinically significant hypoglycemia; the point of needing another's help can kill. The unconscious get nothing by mouth — aspiration — straight to 120.
- Benefit: Glucose below 3.0 mmol/L is clinically meaningful hypoglycemia; altered consciousness or mobility requiring another's assistance is severe hypoglycemia, potentially fatal
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 美国糖尿病学会 (2025). 糖尿病诊疗标准 2025·第 6 章 血糖目标与低血糖. - Note: The unconscious get nothing by mouth — aspiration; call 120. ### 18. Electric shock: cut power first, then sweep the source away with dry wood — never grab with hands
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.

- Benefit: Prevent the rescuer joining the circuit — one casualty becoming two. Free of power and not breathing: CPR per item 1. ### 19. CO alarm sounding, or the whole household headachy and nauseous at once: get outside first, then phone
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->

### 20. Swallowed cleaners, pesticides, medication: don't induce vomiting; bring the bottle, seek care at once; eyes and skin get 15+ minutes of running water
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Store cleaners, disinfectants, pesticides, and medicines high or locked, never decanted into drink bottles.
- In plain terms: Swallowed: no vomiting — corrosives re-burn the esophagus on the way up. Drink some milk or egg white, bring the bottle and label, go immediately. Skin or eyes: copious running water at least 15 minutes; eyes flushed with lids held open, never rubbed. Disinfectant mixed with toilet cleaner releases chlorine gas.
- Benefit: China CDC's occupational-health and poisoning institute gives four routes. Ingestion: immediately drink suitable milk or egg white, "remember: do not induce vomiting"; seek care promptly with the packaging or label "so physicians can quickly identify components and plan treatment." Inhalation: move to ventilated air, loosen the collar; severe cough, breathlessness, or dyspnea means immediate care. Skin: flush the contact site with copious water at least 15 minutes, keeping rinse-off from splashing eyes or intact skin. Eyes: flush with copious water or saline at least 15 minutes, lids held open so the flow bathes the eyeball and lid lining — "never rub" — then straight to ophthalmology. High-risk products by pH: below 3 — toilet cleaners, descalers, floor cleaners, stainless polishers, some strong drain openers with dilute sulfuric acid; above 11 — drain openers, heavy-duty kitchen degreasers, dishwasher powders, oxygen bleaches, scouring powders. And the instant-disaster combination: disinfectant with hydrochloric-acid toilet cleaner releases chlorine gas, irritating airways with violent cough and breathlessness, potentially lethal
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 中国疾病预防控制中心职业卫生与中毒控制所 (2025). 暑假安全提示：警惕强酸强碱清洁剂. <https://niohp.chinacdc.cn/kpdw/202507/t20250722_308659.htm>
- Note: Grade B: official guidance covers handling and recognition without outcome numbers. The no-vomiting logic: corrosives re-burn the esophagus (the throat-to-stomach tube) on the way up. Unsure what was swallowed: bring bottle, label, and vomitus together. Prevention beats rescue: separate storage away from food and drink, caps tightened, empties discarded, never into water bottles; rubber gloves and goggles in use, ventilation, no mixing types. CO: item 19; burns: item 14. Acid and alkali splashes — clothing and durations: item 21.

### 21. Acid, alkali, or other chemicals on the body: strip contaminated clothing immediately, flood with running water; eyes flushed with lids open, full duration before leaving
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Locate eyewash stations and showers on day one of the job — not after the accident.
- In plain terms: Three steps. Strip contaminated clothing, shoes, and socks at once — pooled fabric burns worst. Flood with copious running water: skin and eyes 15 minutes minimum, Hunan's emergency authority giving 20–30 — take the longer. Eyes: lids held open, full contact, then straight to ophthalmology. Cool water, never hot.
- Benefit: Three field steps. Strip contaminated garments: poisons keep absorbing through skin, and pooled fabric burns deepest. Copious running water: China CDC's floor is "at least 15 minutes" for skin and eyes alike; Hunan's emergency authority gives 20–30 minutes for wounds, "longer for strong chemicals" — take the longer. Eyes: "hold the lids open ensuring the flow fully contacts the eyeball and lid lining," "never rub," then immediate ophthalmology. Room-temperature or tepid water; hot water forbidden. No ointments, mercurochrome, or anything on fresh wounds, and no dirty wraps — they obscure depth assessment. Legally, chemical skin and eye burns both sit in the statutory occupational-disease catalog, routing work cases through occupational disease and work injury; acute hazard incidents obligate the employer to "organize treatment, health checks, and medical observation, bearing the costs" (national)

- Sources: 中国疾病预防控制中心职业卫生与中毒控制所 (2025). 暑假安全提示：警惕强酸强碱清洁剂. <https://niohp.chinacdc.cn/kpdw/202507/t20250722_308659.htm>；湖南省应急管理厅 (2015). 常见化学品中毒事故的应急处理与治疗. <http://yjt.hunan.gov.cn/yjt/tszt/aqzs/201509/t20150919_3113778.html>；国家卫生健康委等四部门 (2024). 职业病分类和目录（2025 年 8 月 1 日起实施）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202412/content_6992843.htm>；全国人大常委会 (2018). 职业病防治法（第三十七条）. <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/201711/t20171104_779851.html>（北京市人民政府转载）

Bring the chemical's name, packaging, or safety data sheet (SDS) to hospital; physicians plan by it. Never grab another acid or alkali to "neutralize" — mixing releases heat, and dosing is uncontrolled. Hunan's "neutralizing solution" means employer-stocked specialty rinses, not improvised counter-agents. Dry powders — lime, cement — brush off before flushing. Pre-job protection and exams: Section 19, items 9–10.

### 22. Heat: dizziness, nausea, no sweat, or confusion — move to shade, strip, douse; the unconscious get no water, call 120
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Hot red skin, slurring, confusion — heatstroke. Dizziness to coma can take tens of minutes, with extreme fatality. Move to shade and airflow, strip clothes, cool with water and wet towels, call 120. The unconscious get not a sip — aspiration.
- Benefit: The US National Weather Service: heat stroke is "a severe medical emergency" — body temperature above 103°F (39.4°C), confusion, slurring, hot red skin; call emergency, move somewhere cool, cool with water and wet cloths, "do not give liquids." Heat exhaustion — heavy sweat, weakness, cool moist skin, fast weak pulse — moves to cool, loosened clothing, sips of water; vomiting, worsening, or over an hour unrelieved means care. Yunnan's CDC: move to shade and ventilation, cool with cold wet towels, salted drinks if alert, emergency call if severe; severe heatstroke carries "extremely high fatality." China CDC: cool first, transport later — cool within the "golden half hour" after loss of consciousness; where conditions allow, cold-water immersion for young, healthy adults; elders and the chronically ill, skin-spraying with evaporative cooling. The Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 heat guidelines call cold-water immersion the most important treatment for severe heat illness, cooling fastest; without ice water, use the coolest available, or spray-and-fan at roughly half the immersion rate

Wilderness & Environmental Medicine 35(1_suppl):112S-127S. <https://doi.org/10.1177/10806032241227924>（正文站点拒绝自动抓取，具体推荐按荒野医学会官网的更新摘要核对："cold water immersion … is the most important method of treating severe heat illness" "If CWI is unavailable, evaporative cooling is the recommended second-line treatment. … generally half as fast as CWI" <https://wms.org/magazine/magazine/1540/Heat-Illness-CPG/default.aspx>）；Lipman GS et al. ### 23. Heat-exhausted and alert: small repeated drinks of cool weak-salty water or sports drink — no chugging, no alcohol, no high-sugar drinks
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A sports drink runs a few yuan.
- In plain terms: Only the alert drink. Cool weak-salty water or sports drink, small and repeated, 500–1,000 ml per hour. No chugging — vomit aspirating is deadlier than the heat. No alcohol or high-sugar drinks; both lose you more fluid. Cool sponging and ice packs at neck, armpits, groin first; rehydration second. The confused or slurring get nothing by mouth.
- Benefit: China CDC's public heat-illness guide (2014): "drink at least 2–4 cups of cool water hourly (500–1000ml), not too warm, in small repeated amounts," "do not drink alcohol or heavily sugared beverages — they cause further fluid loss; also avoid very icy drinks," and for heatstroke explicitly "do not give the patient water." US NIOSH: heat exhaustion — "encourage frequent sips of cool water"; heat cramps — a carbohydrate-electrolyte sports drink every 15–20 minutes, "no salt tablets"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 中国疾病预防控制中心职业卫生与中毒控制所 (2014). 公众高温中暑预防与紧急处理指南（2014 版）. <https://niohp.chinacdc.cn/sndt/201405/t20140530_97623.htm> ; US CDC/NIOSH. Heat-Related Illnesses. <https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/illnesses.html>
- Note: Plain water alone after heavy sweating dilutes blood sodium — hence a little salt. Chugging invites vomiting, and vomit in the airway kills faster than heat. "No ice-cold drinks" comes from China CDC's 2014 guide on gastric-spasm grounds, without quantified evidence — don't misread it as "no cold anything." ### 24. Fire: crawl low, touch the door before opening, a hot door stays shut; stairs not elevators; once out, never go back
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: One or two standalone smoke alarms, tens of yuan. Glance at the corridor's two exits in advance.
- In plain terms: Drop and crawl beneath the smoke toward the exit. Before any door, feel the handle and panel — hot, or smoke seeping through, means don't open it; take the second route. Open slowly; slam shut at thick smoke. Stairs, never elevators — power loss traps you inside. Out means out — no returning for things. Clothes on fire: stop, drop, and roll, hands shielding the face.
- Benefit: FEMA's escape text: "get low to the ground and crawl to the exit." Feel doors first — "if it is hot, or there is smoke coming through the cracks, don't open the door; use a second way out." Open slowly, closing at heavy smoke. Clothes afire: "stop, drop, and roll," hands over the face. Trapped: shut the door, block gaps and vents with cloth or tape, phone in your location. The emergency ministry's guidance concurs: no elevators, run downstairs when fire is small, cover nose and mouth with a wet towel or mask, crawl or stoop low

### 30. Earthquake: first read your building — code-compliant blocks shelter in place; collapsible old housing with the door steps away, exit
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->

### 33. Altitude: climb by sleeping elevation, descend on worsening symptoms — never push through

### 44. Infant under 1 unresponsive with abnormal breathing: speakerphone 120, infant CPR — 30 compressions, 2 breaths
