# 18. Is raising children worth it?

This section counts money and time. Like Section 10's marriage accounting, it splits the accounts for you without deciding for you. Already pregnant and working through procedures: Section 27.

### 1. Count what's payable first: the national childcare subsidy, 3,600 yuan per child yearly, paid to age 3
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One trip to the child's household-registration locality to apply.
- In plain terms: Children born from 2025-01-01 onward, under 3: this money pays. One parent applies at the child's registration place. 3,600 yuan per child per year, running to age 3 — income-tax exempt, and uncounted as household income for dibao-style assistance.
- Benefit: The central plan states: infants born from 2025-01-01, under 3, paid "3,600 yuan per child per year," claimed by a parent or other guardian — the subsidy "exempt from individual income tax" and "not counted toward family or personal income" in assistance determinations
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 中共中央办公厅、国务院办公厅 (2025). 育儿补贴制度实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12206/202508/content_7035435.html>
- Note: This is the national floor; some provinces and cities stack local subsidies on top. Three years total 10,800 yuan — a small slice of child-rearing costs, no basis for the have-or-not decision.

### 2. Maternity leave: 98 days, with the maternity allowance paid by the birth-insurance fund at the employer's prior-year average monthly wage
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. The employer files; you run nowhere.
- In plain terms: At least 98 days' leave, 15 usable prenatally; difficult labor adds 15; each additional baby adds 15. The pay during leave is the maternity allowance, from the birth-insurance fund; uninsured employers pay it themselves.
- Benefit: The State Council sets the days: "female employees enjoy 98 days of maternity leave, of which 15 may be taken before childbirth; difficult labor adds 15; multiple births add 15 per additional infant." The pay is the maternity allowance: insured, "paid by the maternity insurance fund at the standard of the employer's prior-year average monthly employee wage"; uninsured, paid by the employer at your pre-leave wage standard
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第七条、第八条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Note: 98 is the national floor; provincial population regulations typically add 60-plus reward days, usually totaling 158 or more. Who pays the reward days varies by province.

### 3. Know this one: no wage cuts or dismissal for pregnancy, childbirth, or nursing
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Cutting wages or firing you during pregnancy, childbirth, or nursing is illegal — fined 1,000–5,000 yuan per person affected. With a baby under one, you get a daily nursing hour, and no overtime or night shifts may be assigned. Claims: Section 19.
- Benefit: The State Council provides: "employers may not reduce wages, dismiss, or terminate labor or employment contracts of female employees on account of pregnancy, childbirth, or nursing." Nursing mothers of infants under 1 receive one nursing hour daily, with no extended hours or night shifts. Violations draw 1,000–5,000 yuan per person
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院 (2012). 女职工劳动保护特别规定（国令第 619 号，第五条、第九条、第十三条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm>
- Note: Wrongfully dismissed: Section 19's claim path. Evidence priorities: written transfer and pay-cut notices plus chats — keep both.

### 4. The child-rearing time account counts "whose time is consumed," not "how hard it feels"
<!-- costs: money=none time=high will=yes benefit=large metric=time -->
- Cost: Free but time-consuming: under 3, days are essentially fully consumed. The hard part: every single day, no teeth-gritting through a finite stretch.
- In plain terms: Convert daily childcare hours and nightly wake counts into time, multiply by the caregiver's hourly rate. The result usually dwarfs formula and diapers. So settle the division before conceiving.
- Benefit: Convert daily care hours and nightly wakes into hours times the caregiver's hourly rate — a figure usually far beyond formula and diaper money. Unpaid care, cooking, and night duty still enter the cost this way. The method matches Section 10's housework accounting: agree the division before deciding to conceive
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；口径见第 10 节
- Note: Precision is unnecessary. What helps is two people, before conceiving, negotiating over one shared sheet — sparing the fights after.

### 5. The money account splits three ways: 0–3, compulsory schooling, and after
<!-- costs: money=high time=medium will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: No fixed total — cities and child-rearing styles vary wildly. The hard part: sitting down to split the three accounts instead of guessing one intimidating number.
- In plain terms: 0–3 money goes to caregiving — yourselves, grandparents, or hired care. Primary through junior high goes to housing and extracurriculars. After that, to the education track. The three spend nothing alike; mixed, they yield only a frightening total.
- Benefit: 0–3's bulk is the care arrangement; compulsory schooling's bulk is housing and extracurricular spending; thereafter, the schooling path. The three differ wholly in where money goes — pooled, they produce only a scary total
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献
- Note: Count the certain money first — childcare subsidy, maternity allowance, insurance reimbursements — then see the gap. Don't decide by the internet's "raising a child costs X million": unclear scope, unknown inclusions and omissions.

### 6. For the elders, for the marriage, for old age — keep separate accounts
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free, but one earnest conversation. The hard part: saying it plainly, not around.
- In plain terms: Others wanting you to bear a child may count as a factor — but time and money post to your own ledger. "Raising children against old age" takes its own account: it wagers on another person's means and willingness twenty years out. Write the consequences of not bearing children too, and set the two side by side.
- Benefit: Same frame as Section 10. Others' expectations may factor in; the costs stay on your ledger, not theirs. "Old-age insurance by children" especially needs its own account, assuming another person's economic capacity and willingness two decades ahead
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；同类分析见第 10 节
- Note: Write the consequences of not bearing children too, and compare them side by side with the consequences of bearing them.
