# 17. When there's an elder at home

Metrics: money and personal freedom. With elders, most trouble isn't insufficient money — it's words unsaid and papers unsigned in time. The last three entries — bedsores, cataract surgery, and dog-walking fractures — count in mortality terms, not compared with the rest.

### 1. While the elder is lucid: appoint the future guardian in writing
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Notarization runs a few hundred yuan; one conversation settles it. The hard part: speaking of the day they can no longer manage, face to face.
- In plain terms: While clear-headed, spend a few hundred yuan on a notarized paper naming their future guardian. Without it, incapacity means the court appoints who signs and who manages the money — and family disagreement drags it longer.
- Benefit: The Civil Code lets fully capable adults arrange their future guardian in advance — close relatives or any willing individual or organization — "determining their guardian in written form." On total or partial incapacity, the named person takes over; without the paper, court designation decides who signs and who manages
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第三十三条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: The instrument is "agreed guardianship" — one's own advance designation, ranking ahead of the statutory order. Elders living alone, children far away, complicated families: all the more reason to arrange early. Notarization recommended.

### 2. Make the will; remember later wills revoke earlier ones, and notarized wills no longer outrank
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: A handwritten will is free; notarization runs hundreds to a thousand-odd yuan. The hard part: raising death with family.
- In plain terms: Six will forms are legal, but formal defects void the whole document — the classic failure. The handwritten kind must be written, signed, and dated entirely in one's own hand. Multiple conflicting wills: the last stands. Since 2021, the notarized copy no longer ranks automatically first.
- Benefit: The Civil Code recognizes six forms — self-written, ghost-written, printed, audio-video recorded, oral, and notarized — each with mandatory formalities. Multiple conflicting wills: the last governs. Since 2021, notarization confers no priority
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大 (2020). 民法典（第一千一百三十四条至第一千一百四十二条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>
- Note: Self-written wills: wholly handwritten, signed, dated. Printed wills need two-plus witnesses, with testator and witnesses signing and dating every page. Heirs, named beneficiaries, and their close relatives cannot witness. Formal errors void the whole thing — the classic failure.

### 3. The elder's money in a separate account, with a two-person confirmation rule above a threshold
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One family conversation sets the threshold. The hard part: raising it without sounding suspicious of them.
- In plain terms: Supplement, collectible, and wealth-product scams against elders share one playbook: months of rapport, then one harvest. Agree in advance: spending past a set amount gets mentioned to a named person — delaying the payment past the impulsive moment.
- Benefit: Elder-targeted supplement, collectible, and wealth scams all bank months of trust before the single harvest. A pre-agreed "past X yuan, mention it to Y" delays the payment decision past the impulsive crest
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；防骗通则见第 8 节和第 13 节
- Note: Frame it not as "we fear you'll be scammed" but "the household keeps joint books" — easier to accept. Don't move all the elder's money to children: that breeds its own disputes.

### 4. Arm the elder with one ever-ready refusal: "I need to go home and discuss it with my family"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One rehearsal conversation, minutes.
- In plain terms: Sales and fraud run on in-the-moment pressure that makes refusal awkward. Pre-agree the sentence — "I'll discuss it with my family at home" — and the elder holds a graceful exit on the spot. Better than reasoning afterward.
- Benefit: Sales and fraud both press in the moment. One pre-agreed exit line hands the elder a step down — more effective than after-the-fact reasoning
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献
- Note: Use with item 3. Run the home fall-proofing in passing — Section 1. The line gives the elder a step down, not a shield against them; the hardest part of being cornered is refusing before a roomful — harder than judging truth.

### 5. Any elder-paid-upfront "investment retirement" is off-limits: membership cards, bed purchases, retirement apartments, residential tours, and elder-product sales are one illegal-fundraising set
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Read the formats to the elder once, minutes. On encounter: refuse outright — no lectures, no tours, no phone numbers left. The hard part: elders already attending and befriended pull back reluctantly.
- In plain terms: Elder-pay-first, return-promising retirement schemes are one scam family: membership cards, bed purchases, retirement apartments, residential tours, meeting-sold elder products. Losses are self-borne by regulation. One ring took 136 million yuan for "reserving retirement-apartment beds," burning 2,100-plus people. The only interceptable step is the payment.
- Benefit: The civil-affairs ministry lists five formats — judging by format beats judging by face. First, impersonating elder-care institutions — no entity, rented venues, fake cooperation agreements. Second, "elder-care services" — VIP, member, and prepaid cards collecting "service fees" far beyond bed capacity. Third, "elder projects" — stakes in wellness bases, fictional retirement apartments, long-term bed leases, "time banks," fronted as refund sales, sale-leasebacks, and repurchase promises. Fourth, "elder products" — buybacks, consignment sales, consumption rebates, free checkups, gifts, meeting marketing, wellness lectures, expert clinics. Fifth, "residential retirement" — cheap or free travel, stored-value rebates, points-based retirement. The money is unrecoverable: Article 25 of the anti-illegal-fundraising regulation — "losses from participation are borne by the participants themselves." Official cases: Dai and others raised 136 million yuan reserving retirement-apartment beds, 2,100-plus victims — Dai drew 15 years plus a 400,000-yuan fine for fundraising fraud; Cao raised 13.207 billion yuan through "elder-industry investment," 110,000-plus people involved — life imprisonment with political-rights deprivation and full confiscation. Supplement meeting-sales run as fraud: Ni wined, gifted, and chauffeured elders into lectures where instructors fabricated anti-cancer claims through "false personas, false cases, and inflated bargaining," selling "dihydroquercetin" at tens of times cost — 620,000-odd yuan in sales; Ni drew 10 years 3 months plus a 120,000-yuan fine (national; regulation since 2021-05-01)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 民政部 (2023). 关于养老服务领域非法集资的风险提示. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202306/content_6886556.htm>（中国政府网发布）；民政部、中国银保监会 (2021). 关于养老领域非法集资的风险提示. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>；国务院 (2021). 防范和处置非法集资条例（国务院令第 737 号，第二、二十五、三十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-02/10/content_5586632.htm>；最高人民检察院 (2022). 检察机关打击整治养老诈骗犯罪典型案例. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202206/t20220617_560010.shtml>；最高人民法院 (2025). 依法惩治涉民生领域诈骗犯罪典型案例（案例五 倪某荣诈骗案）. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/482861.html>
- Note: Judge not by "this one looks legitimate" but by "pay first, promised returns?" Elder care sells services; the moment the talk turns to investment, rebates, or principal back — disbelieve. Lectures, tours, and clinics themselves are lawful and hard to block; the payment is the interceptable step — pair with item 3's confirmation rule. Already paid: 110 or 96110 for the stop per Section 8, item 2, plus reports to the local anti-illegal-fundraising lead department. House-for-retirement is another path — item 6.

### 6. Beyond insurers' reverse-mortgage pension insurance, no "house-for-retirement" — and never mortgage the house to buy wealth products
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. The property certificate goes to no door-to-door "house-for-retirement" visitor; mortgage, guarantee, and loan agreements get a child's eyes before signing. The hard part: visitors run warm and numerous, and elders hate refusing.
- In plain terms: The only legitimate form is insurers' reverse-mortgage pension insurance, still piloted. Door-to-door versions are unrelated — tricking elders into mortgaging the house, then buying the visitor's recommended products. Some never knew their house was mortgaged: house gone, loan still on their back.
- Benefit: The regulator's consumer-protection bureau drew the line. The only legitimate form: "elderly housing reverse-mortgage pension insurance" — elders with full lawful title mortgage to an insurer, continuing to live in, use, and rent the house, disposal subject to the mortgagee, drawing pension payments until death — still piloted, thinly practiced. The fraud version is "completely unrelated" to the pilot, "borrowing state policy to stage illegal fundraising" — inducing elders into property mortgages, investing the borrowed money in recommended products, "often a Ponzi scheme of new repaying old." The consequence, verbatim: "some participants didn't even know their property was mortgaged, ultimately losing the house while still carrying the loan." The return line: "question products promising above 6%, treat 8%+ as dangerous, above 10% prepare to lose all principal — 'guaranteed principal, high returns' is financial fraud." The same notice demands "treating contract signing with care — never sign blank contracts" (national, June 2021)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 中国银保监会消费者权益保护局 (2021). 关于警惕「投资养老」「以房养老」金融诈骗的风险提示（2021 年第三期）. <https://dfjrjgj.hunan.gov.cn/dfjrjgj/yhlj/202106/t20210608_19450856.html>（湖南省地方金融监督管理局转载）；民政部、中国银保监会 (2021). 关于养老领域非法集资的风险提示（第四种表现形式：以宣称「以房养老」为名非法集资）. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n152/n164/c36171/content.html>
- Note: Grade B: regulator risk notices and experience, not research. The 6/8/10 lines are consumer reference points, not legal thresholds. The banking regulator has merged into the NFRA; the original page is down — cited via the provincial reprint. Product losses come second: a mortgaged house leaves the elder owing a loan exceeding any principal. Signing and blank contracts generally: Section 8, item 17; payment stops: Section 8, item 2.

### 7. A bedridden or severely disabled elder at home: apply for long-term care insurance at the enrollment locality — it isn't elders-only
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: One application at the local insurance office plus one disability-grading assessment; first-pass assessments are fund-paid.
- In plain terms: Long-term care insurance pays for hired care. Severe disability persisting half a year or more: apply at the enrollment locality. No fronting — employee-enrolled reimbursement runs about 70%, resident-enrolled about 50%, excluding bed and board fees. No age floor: severely disabled young people qualify too.
- Benefit: Qualified long-term care costs carry no deductible (the front-until-threshold amount — waived here). Employee-policy participants: fund-paid around 70%; unemployed-resident-policy participants: around 50%. Annual caps run no higher than 50% of the pooling area's prior-year per-capita resident disposable income
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家医保局等八部门 (2026). 关于印发《加快建立长期护理保险制度实施方案》的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202603/content_7063915.htm>：保障对象为「按规定参保缴费且失能状态长期持续（一般为6个月以上），经申请通过评估认定的失能人员」，「制度起步阶段保障重度失能人员」；「待遇享受不设起付标准。符合规定的长期护理服务费用，按未就业城乡居民参保政策参保的，基金支付比例为50%左右；按单位职工参保政策参保的，基金支付比例为70%左右，退休人员享受单位职工参保待遇」；「参保人员基金年度最高支付限额不超过统筹地区上年度城乡居民人均可支配收入的50%」；「待遇享受固定等待期原则上按照6个月设置」；另见中共中央办公厅、国务院办公厅《关于加快建立长期护理保险制度的意见》. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12666/202604/content_7065119.html>
- Note: Disability must persist "generally 6 months or more" — weeks of hospitalization doesn't qualify. A 6-month waiting period precedes benefits. Bed and board fees and medical costs payable by basic insurance are excluded, as is overlap with work-injury nursing allowances. This money and the disability nursing subsidy (Section 7, item 8) are separate procedures and separate sums — no conflict. Home- and community-based care carry favorable rates; institutional admission isn't required. Rollout is phased and assessment standards vary — call 12393 or the local office first. Failed assessment isn't final: reapply as disability deepens.

### 8. With someone bedridden long-term, treat pressure ulcers as enemy number one: powered air mattress, scheduled turning, daily inspection of bony prominences
<!-- costs: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A home alternating-pressure air mattress runs from a few hundred yuan; each turn takes minutes. The hard part: every 2–3 hours through the night — beyond one person alone.
- In plain terms: Hip-fracture patients with preoperative pressure ulcers ran about 20% higher 30-day mortality. Powered air mattresses cut new ulcers by about 60%; genuinely executed turning schedules, about 70%. Keep people off their fronts for long. Daily checks of sacrum, heels, hips, and shoulder blades; non-blanching redness means nurse or doctor the same day.
- Benefit: 19,520 hip-fracture surgical patients (US NSQIP 2016–2019): preoperative pressure ulcers tracked higher 30-day mortality, holding after adjustment (OR 1.2, 21% odds increase, P=0.004), plus deep-vein thrombosis (OR 1.59), pneumonia (OR 1.39), unplanned readmission (OR 1.43), and 0.4 extra hospital days. Among 324 hospitalized elders (mean 86), median 18-month follow-up: pressure ulcers independently predicted long-term death (HR 1.500, 95% CI 1.059 to 2.126, P=0.020) — independently meaning after other factors drop out. A network meta-analysis of 65 randomized trials: powered active-airflow support surfaces versus standard hospital mattresses ran RR 0.42 (95% CI 0.29 to 0.63, moderate certainty); powered hybrid airflow, RR 0.22 (0.07 to 0.66). A Cochrane review (2026, 11 trials, 4,462 participants): 2-hourly versus 4-hourly turning indistinguishable (RR 1.05, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.39, very low certainty), likewise other intervals. A 1,226-patient ICU trial using wearable sensors with visual alerts enforcing genuine 2-hour turning ran lower ulcer incidence (RR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.75, moderate certainty). A 116-patient trial: prone positioning ran higher ulcer rates than supine (RR 4.55, 95% CI 2.31 to 8.98)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Porter SB, Pla R, Chow JH, et al. (2022). Preoperative Pressure Ulcers, Mortality, and Complications in Older Hip Fracture Surgery Patients. JAAOS Global Research & Reviews, 6(11). <https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOSGlobal-D-22-00117>；Ottaviani S, Rondanina E, Longo E, et al. (2026). Impact of pressure ulcers and frailty on long-term mortality: a prospective cohort study of hospitalized older adults. European Geriatric Medicine, 17(2), 665-672. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01331-8>；Shi C, Dumville JC, Cullum N (2018). Support surfaces for pressure ulcer prevention: A network meta-analysis. PLoS ONE, 13(2), e0192707. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192707>；Latimer SL, Chaboyer WP, Probst S, et al. (2026). Repositioning for pressure injury prevention in adults. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 6, CD009958. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009958.pub4>
- Note: Grade B three ways: the mortality studies show "ulcer bearers fare worse," not "prevention lowers mortality"; the turning-interval trials are shaky; and the mattress evidence, though stronger, compares hospital surfaces — extrapolating to home beds. Daily checks of sacrum, heels, hips, and shoulder blades for non-blanching redness are standard nursing practice without separate literature. Insurance-covered care: item 7. A suddenly swollen leg: Section 13, item 11. The long-bedridden stretch after falls: Section 1, item 34.

### 9. When cataracts affect the elder's walking: ophthalmic evaluation for surgery; with both eyes involved, don't let the second wait too long
<!-- costs: money=high time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: One accompanied eye exam for the surgeon's judgment; most hospitals run day surgery — home the same day. Both eyes mean two operations. Out-of-pocket depends on the intraocular lens (the artificial lens implanted) chosen and local insurance — ask beforehand.
- In plain terms: Women over 70 having prompt first-eye surgery fell about 34% less often over a year, with fewer fractures — though the count of fallers didn't drop. Second-eye surgery showed no clear fall difference. Operated elders ran about 16% lower hip-fracture odds within a year.
- Benefit: A randomized trial took 306 women over 70 with cataracts: half had first-eye surgery within about 4 weeks, half waited 12 months. Over a year, prompt surgery cut fall counts about 34% (rate ratio 0.66, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.96). At-least-one-fall rates — 49% surgical versus 45% waiting — didn't drop; two-plus falls ran 18% versus 25%; fractures struck 4 (3%) versus 12 (8%). The Cochrane review included it: first-eye surgery reduced fall counts in women, second-eye didn't. The second-eye trial, 239 women over 70, ran 32% lower fall rates but possibly by chance (rate ratio 0.68, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.19). In a 5% Medicare sample of 1,113,640 cataract patients over 65 (2002–2009), observational only, the operated ran about 16% lower hip-fracture odds within a year (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.81 to 0.87) — an absolute difference of 0.20 points, about 2 fewer per 1,000
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Harwood RH, Foss AJ, Osborn F, Gregson RM, Zaman A, Masud T (2005). Falls and health status in elderly women following first eye cataract surgery: a randomised controlled trial. British Journal of Ophthalmology, 89(1), 53-59. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjo.2004.049478> ; Foss AJ, Harwood RH, Osborn F, Gregson RM, Zaman A, Masud T (2006). Falls and health status in elderly women following second eye cataract surgery: a randomised controlled trial. Age and Ageing, 35(1), 66-71. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/afj005> ; Gillespie LD, Robertson MC, Gillespie WJ, et al. (2012). Interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 9, CD007146. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007146.pub3> ; Tseng VL, Yu F, Lum F, Coleman AL (2012). Risk of fractures following cataract surgery in Medicare beneficiaries. JAMA, 308(5), 493-501. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.9014> ; Desapriya E, Subzwari S, Scime-Beltrano G, et al. (2010). Vision improvement and reduction in falls after expedited cataract surgery: systematic review and metaanalysis. - Note: Contested: pooled, the two randomized trials left faller counts unimproved (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.17). Western Australia's whole-population records found fall-injury hospitalizations up 27% in the year after first-eye surgery — 2.14× preoperative levels between operations — though before-after designs can't separate surgery from aging. Another Australian cohort found the biggest fall drop after the second eye. So with both eyes involved, don't let the second wait; between operations, extra care on stairs. Cochrane also carries a vision-management trial where falls rose about 57% — surgery fixes clarity; fall prevention still needs Section 1, item 13's balance work, and bones need items 39–40 there. The national eye-health plan notes cataract restoration is routinely available at county level — no need for the big-city hospital. Beneficiaries: your parents and grandparents.

### 10. Elder dog-walkers: leash in the palm, never wrapped; keep it short; train the dog out of lunging first
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Grip and shortening are free — one accompanied walk and lesson. Lunge-training takes time, self-taught or hired (prices vary widely). The hard part: weaning elders off retractables and wrist-wrapping habits.
- In plain terms: US over-65s fracturing bones while leash-walking rose from about 1,700 to about 4,400 yearly, 80% women. Hip fractures lead, nearly three in ten hospitalized. Among leash-injured hands, about three-quarters came from wrapped cords and a dog's pull. Hence: palm grip, short leash, trained dog.
- Benefit: US emergency surveillance projected nationally: over-65 leash-walking fractures ran about 1,671 in 2004 and 4,396 in 2017, the rise statistically significant; 78.6% of the fractured were women, 28.7% hospitalized; by single site, hips led at 17.3%; by class, upper limbs at 52.1%. Another national emergency dataset (2001–2018) projected about 360,000 leash-related injuries (356,746 exactly): 193,483 pulled by dogs, 136,767 tripped or entangled; injuries per million rose from 25.4 to 105.5. A hand-surgery clinic took 443 leash-injured hands over 5 years: about three-quarters (337, 75.9%) from wrapped hands or fingers pulled by dogs; about one-fifth (96, 21.6%) needed surgery. CDC: 2001–2006 averaged about 86,629 fall injuries yearly involving cats and dogs, 88.0% dogs; of dog-related falls, 26% occurred while walking and 21.2% from being pushed or pulled over
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Pirruccio K, Yoon YM, Ahn J (2019). Fractures in Elderly Americans Associated With Walking Leashed Dogs. JAMA Surgery, 154(5), 458-459. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamasurg.2019.0061>；Forrester MB (2020). Dog leash-related injuries treated at emergency departments. American Journal of Emergency Medicine, 38(9), 1782-1786. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2020.05.082>；Plusch K, Givner D, Wiafe B, Lutsky K, Beredjiklian P (2026). Hand and Wrist Dog-Leash Injuries in the Outpatient Setting: A Review of 443 Cases. Hand, 21(4), 582-586. <https://doi.org/10.1177/15589447241299128>；CDC (2009). Nonfatal fall-related injuries associated with dogs and cats — United States, 2001-2006. MMWR, 58(11), 277-281. <https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5811a1.htm>；Rosa R, Buckley RE (2024). Leash-related injuries associated with dog walking: an understudied risk for dog owners? Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association, 262(7), 973-978. <https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.23.11.0608>
- Note: Palm grip, never wrapped, is the hand clinic's advice — it protects fingers and wrists, not the pulled-down walker. They also recommend short leashes: short denies the dog running speed, softening the yank; long lines entangle and trip. Retractables at full extension are long lines — but no study compares retractables directly, a 2024 review noting leash-type data absent. CDC and the fracture paper both commend lunge-training, the latter adding smaller breeds for elders considering dogs. No study prices these habits in falls — hence "small" by judgment. Bone readiness: Section 1, items 39–40; balance: item 13; walking volume credit: Section 2, item 44. Beneficiaries: your parents and grandparents.
