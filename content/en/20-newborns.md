# 20. Caring for a newborn

Metrics: infant mortality and money. Only what bears on the child's survival or your spending enters — and only where differences are large and evidence hard. Soothing and parenting details aren't this book's. Pre-birth and discharge-day procedures: Section 27.

### 1. Infants sleep on their backs, on firm flat surfaces, same room but not same bed, nothing soft in the crib
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The mattress goes firm — a few hundred yuan. The hard part: withstanding the elders' ways, consistently.
- In plain terms: Infants sleep face-up on firm flat surfaces. Adults share the room, never the bed. No pillows, quilts, bumpers, plush toys, or "head-shaping" pillows. Inclined rockers and infant seats carry, never sleep. The US alone loses about 3,500 infants yearly in sleep.
- Benefit: About 3,500 US infants die yearly of sleep-related causes — sudden infant death syndrome, unknown causes, and accidental suffocation in bed. The AAP's safe-sleep environment: "supine positioning; use of a firm, noninclined sleep surface; room sharing without bed sharing; avoidance of soft bedding and overheating." Further risk reducers: breastfeeding, no nicotine or alcohol exposure, on-schedule vaccination, and pacifiers
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Moon RY, Carlin RF, Hand I, Task Force on Sudden Infant Death Syndrome and the Committee on Fetus and Newborn (2022). Sleep-Related Infant Deaths: Updated 2022 Recommendations for Reducing Infant Deaths in the Sleep Environment. Pediatrics, 150(1), e2022057990. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2022-057990>
- Note: "Nothing soft" means pillows, quilts, bumpers, plush toys, and shaping pillows all out — the harmless-looking nursing pillow included. Inclined rockers and infant seats aren't sleep places. In this section, nothing differs as much, and nothing takes more elder-pressure endurance.

### 2. The first hepatitis B shot within 24 hours of birth
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — the vaccine sits in the immunization program. Given at the hospital on day one, no extra trip.
- In plain terms: The first hepatitis B dose within 24 hours of birth. Infection in infancy runs about 95% chronic; adult infections mostly self-resolve. Earlier is stronger. HBsAg-positive mothers' babies add hepatitis B immunoglobulin simultaneously.
- Benefit: WHO: "all infants should receive the hepatitis B vaccine as soon as possible (within 24 hours)" — because "about 95% of infections in infancy and early childhood develop chronic hepatitis." Adult infections mostly self-resolve
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: World Health Organization. Hepatitis B fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hepatitis-b>
- Note: HBsAg-positive mothers: add one dose of hepatitis B immunoglobulin at birth — established at prenatal screening, not postpartum. The mother's own screening: Section 1.

### 3. Complete the national immunization program — fully free; missed doses get made up, not restarted
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — "vaccination units may charge no fee for immunization-program vaccines," by statute. Monthly trips to the clinic; keep the vaccination booklet safe.
- In plain terms: Program vaccines are wholly free; clinics cannot charge. Enrollment checks the booklet, and missed doses surface for catch-up. Missed doses补 only the missing ones — no restarting, brand switches fine. Eczema, hives, allergic rhinitis, and asthma when stable all vaccinate normally, physiological jaundice included.
- Benefit: Kindergartens and schools verify the booklet at enrollment; gaps get reported for catch-up — missed doses never slip past. The 2026 schedule applies: the HPV vaccine has entered the national program; the pertussis-containing series now starts at 2 months, five doses at 2, 4, 6, and 18 months and 6 years; Japanese-encephalitis vaccine went routine in Tibet, Qinghai, Xinjiang, and the Corps from March 2026. Same-day co-administration: all program vaccines may go together per schedule or catch-up rules; injectables land at different sites, "mixing two or more vaccines into one syringe is strictly prohibited." Non-same-day: two-plus injectable live vaccines space at least 28 days; inactivated and oral-live vaccines space freely. Under-18 catch-up: "only incomplete doses need补种, no restart," and brand switches continue (national; 2026 schedule)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2019). 疫苗管理法（第四十八、四十九条）. <http://ynswsjkw.yn.gov.cn/html/2019/faguiguizhangxin_0731/6509.html>（云南省卫生健康委员会转载）；国家疾病预防控制局、国家卫生健康委 (2026). 《国家免疫规划疫苗儿童免疫程序及说明（2026 年版）》解读问答. <https://www.ndcpa.gov.cn/jbkzzx/c100013/common/content/content_2073002930292035584.html>
- Note: The commonest myth is "weak constitution, delay vaccination." Officially: "allergic constitution," family allergy history, and prior food or drug allergies are no bar — atopic dermatitis, hives, allergic rhinitis, and asthma vaccinate when stable, antihistamines and inhaled steroids included, neonatal physiological and breast-milk jaundice likewise. Only two true bars: prior acute severe allergic reaction to the same vaccine, or severe allergy to a known component. Immunodeficient and chemo-, radiation-, or immunosuppressant-treated children get individualized assessment at the clinic. Hepatitis B first dose: item 2. Self-paid non-program vaccines charge separately — unrelated here.

### 4. Breast milk only for the first 6 months — not even water; solids from 6 months, breastfeeding continues
<!-- costs: money=none time=high will=yes benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, saving formula money — but time-heavy, daily. The hard part: 6 unbroken months.
- In plain terms: Start feeding within the first hour. First 6 months: breast milk only, no water. From 6 months, add solids — iron-rich animal foods daily — and keep breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond. Insufficient or impossible: formula, guilt-free. What you feed matters far less than sleep position.
- Benefit: WHO: "initiate breastfeeding within one hour of birth"; first 6 months "exclusive breastfeeding — no other foods or liquids, water included"; then "safe and adequate complementary foods from 6 months, with continued breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond." The national health commission's service guide likewise promotes 0–6-month exclusivity and requires, from 6 months, "daily complementary foods must include iron-rich animal foods"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: World Health Organization. Breastfeeding. <https://www.who.int/health-topics/breastfeeding>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 婴幼儿营养喂养评估服务指南（试行）（国卫办妇幼函〔2024〕452 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202502/content_7002872.htm>
- Note: Insufficient milk or medical bars: formula. The breast-versus-formula gap runs far smaller than safe sleep's. Solid-meal counts: 1–2 daily at 6–8 months, 2–3 at 9–12, covering at least four of seven food groups daily. Severe eczema or egg allergy and peanuts: item 12. Vegan households: item 14.

### 5. Mix formula with water at 70 °C or above; cool before feeding; discard leftovers
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A few minutes' cooling each time. The hard part: waiting through the crying.
- In plain terms: Formula isn't sterile — it may carry Cronobacter. Newborn infection runs 40%–80% reported fatality. Pour hot water first, let it fall to about 73.8 °C, then add powder — the mix still above 70 °C cuts the bacterium below one hundred-thousandth. Cool, feed, discard the rest.
- Benefit: Formula isn't sterile and may carry Cronobacter sakazakii; newborn infection fatality reports at 40%–80%. Experiments: hot water poured first, cooling to about 73.8 °C before powder, keeps the reconstituted mix above 70 °C in the vast majority of cases — reducing the bacterium by over 5 log cycles, below one hundred-thousandth
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Beary MA, Daly SE, Baker J, Snyder AB (2025). Assessing Hot Water Reconstitution Instructions and Labeling of Powdered Infant Formula to Ensure Cronobacter spp. Reduction. Journal of Food Protection, 88(9), 100571. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfp.2025.100571>；Sima WG, Legesse T, Girma S, et al. (2025). Emerging microbial risks: Cronobacter sakazakii in powdered infant formula for infants under six months of age in Ethiopia. BMC Microbiology, 26, 307. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s12866-025-04380-y>；World Health Organization & FAO (2007). Safe preparation, storage and handling of powdered infant formula: guidelines. <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241595414>
- Note: The 70 °C threshold comes from public-health guidance — Beary et al.: "public health guidance for caregivers of high-risk infants recommends reconstituting formula with water heated to at least 70 °C (158 °F) to inactivate microorganisms." Preterm, low-birth-weight, and under-2-month infants run highest risk. No day-old thermos water; no room-temperature holds for the next feed.

### 6. No honey under 1 year
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Under 1, no honey — honey water, honey in solids, honey pastries all count. Honey may carry botulism spores; adult flora suppresses them, infants' can't. Homemade pickles and ferments run the same logic.
- Benefit: The US CDC: "do not feed honey to children under 1" because "honey can contain bacteria that cause botulism." Adult gut flora suppresses the spores (the bacterium's dormant form); infants' cannot
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: US CDC. Preventing Botulism. <https://www.cdc.gov/botulism/prevention/index.html>
- Note: The ban covers honey water, honey-mixed solids, and honey pastries — and, by the same logic, homemade pickles and ferments for infants.

### 7. The birth-day vitamin K injection is non-negotiable
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 0 to a few dozen yuan, usually inside the delivery bill already.
- In plain terms: Newborns store little vitamin K; deficiency bleeds, and the late form often bleeds into the brain. Where prevention isn't routine, 478 per 100,000 newborns bleed; the injection cuts an estimated 78.5%-plus. Proper hospitals give it routinely; home or irregular-facility births demand asking.
- Benefit: Newborn vitamin K stores run low; deficiency causes hemorrhagic disease, the late form often intracranial. The Belgian consensus: "1 or 2 mg vitamin K intramuscularly once at birth." Where systematic prophylaxis is absent, incidence ran 478 per 100,000 — the Tashkent figure from 42,000 newborns — with prevention estimated to cut incidence "above 78.5%"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Fiesack S, Smits A, Rayyan M, et al. (2021). Nutrients, 13(11), 4109. <https://doi.org/10.3390/nu13114109>；Tursunov D, Yoshida Y, Yrysov K, et al. (2018). Nagoya Journal of Medical Science, 80(1), 11. <https://doi.org/10.18999/nagjms.80.1.11>
- Note: Proper Chinese hospitals inject routinely — no effort needed. Home or irregular births: confirm actively. Oral dosing underperforms injection; injection here means into the muscle.

### 8. Under 3 months, 38 °C means straight to hospital — no watching at home
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration and tests, a few hundred yuan.
- In plain terms: Under 3 months, 38 °C means hospital — no home observation, no fever medicine then waiting. Serious bacterial infection at this age may show fever alone, so "looks fine" is no reason to wait. Under 8 days, guidelines don't even cover it — faster still.
- Benefit: The AAP guideline covers well-appearing term infants 8 to 60 days, defining fever as "temperature ≥38.0 °C," with three age bands — 8–21, 22–28, and 29–60 days — each with its pathway. Under 8 days falls outside the guideline entirely: even more reason for immediate care
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Pantell RH, Roberts KB, Adams WG, et al. (2021). Evaluation and Management of Well-Appearing Febrile Infants 8 to 60 Days Old. Pediatrics, 148(2), e2021052228. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2021-052228>
- Note: At this age, serious bacterial infection may present as fever alone — "looks fine" justifies no waiting. No self-dosed antipyretics-then-waiting either.

### 9. However exhausted or furious: never shake a baby
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part: stopping your hands when most depleted or angry.
- In plain terms: Babies carry heavy heads on weak necks. Hard shaking bleeds the brain and eyes — killing or disabling for life. Gentle rocking to sleep isn't it; uncontrolled violent shaking is. Near the edge: baby back in the crib, you step away minutes — crying alone beats being shaken.
- Benefit: Large head-to-body ratio plus weak neck muscles: violent shaking causes intracranial and retinal hemorrhage — fatal or permanently disabling. Gentle lulling isn't the danger; uncontrolled shaking is. Medicine calls it abusive head trauma — the leading cause of traumatic head-injury death under 2, killing nearly 2,250 US children under 5 from 1999 to 2014
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Spies EL, Klevens J (2016). Fatal Abusive Head Trauma Among Children Aged <5 Years — United States, 1999–2014. MMWR 65(20):505-509. <https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm6520a1>；Choudhary AK et al. (2018). Consensus statement on abusive head trauma in infants and young children. Pediatric Radiology 48(8):1048-1065. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00247-018-4149-1>
- Note: At the edge of losing control: baby into the crib, you away for minutes. Crying alone is far safer than shaking in arms. Solo caregivers running chronic sleep debt need this most. Handling your own state: Section 3.

### 10. Diapers: judge by three things — fit, change frequency, and inspection notices — never brand
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Two-three hundred to over a thousand monthly, by tier.
- In plain terms: No official body ranks "best brand." Checkable: the market regulator's sampling notices and incident investigations. Expensive ≠ safe; imported ≠ compliant. Small packs for a week first — rash and pressure marks decide — then stock.
- Benefit: No official "best brand" ranking exists, so this book recommends none. Two checkable sources: SAMR sampling notices and regulators' incident investigations. After the June 2026 "diaper formamide" attention, SAMR convened a joint investigation team
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 市场监管总局 (2026-06-22). 市场监管总局牵头成立联合调查组核查「婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺问题」：「针对媒体反映的"婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺问题"，市场监管总局、工业和信息化部、国家卫生健康委、国家疾控局高度重视，成立联合调查组，核查婴幼儿纸尿裤甲酰胺有关问题，并依法依规处理。有关情况将及时公布。」. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/zj/art/2026/art_78c0425db7604b27bd7a3f36ba319975.html>；中国政府网转载. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202606/content_7072931.htm>；海报新闻 (2026-10-12). 甲酰胺纸尿裤调查3个月进度条0 信息公开待加强（中华网转载）. <https://news.china.com/socialgd/10000169/20261012/49790508.html>

How to buy: expensive ≠ safe, imported ≠ compliant; small packs for a week first. Diaper rash traces mostly to infrequent changes and poor breathability, brand mattering less than imagined. ### 11. Big items in the order "borrow, buy used, buy new" — never one full set at once
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: No extra spending; savings usually run to four figures. The hard part: resisting the one-shot setup.
- In plain terms: Strollers, cribs, swaddles, and toys serve briefly — used markets brim. Borrow first, buy used second, buy new last: thousands saved. Two things never used: car seats and mattresses — crash damage hides from eyes. Confinement clubs and early-education courses: 24-hour cooling before deciding.
- Benefit: Brief service lives leave second-hand supply far exceeding demand. Two true exceptions — car seats and mattresses — where crash-damaged structure stays invisible
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；安全座椅的证据见第 1 节，冲动消费见第 5 节
- Note: Confinement clubs, early-education classes, and infant swim gyms: this book neither recommends nor rejects — but all are optional big spends suiting Section 5's cooling-off.

### 12. Severe eczema or egg allergy: don't dodge peanuts — add early under medical guidance, but never whole nuts
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A jar of peanut butter runs tens of yuan; feeding a few times weekly is effortless. One doctor's evaluation precedes adding.
- In plain terms: Infants with severe eczema or egg allergy eating peanut regularly from 4–11 months ran 1.9% peanut allergy at 5, versus 13.7% among avoiders. Skin-test-positive kids: 10.6% versus 35.3%. See a doctor first. Never whole peanuts — airway.
- Benefit: The UK LEAP randomized trial: 640 infants with severe eczema, egg allergy, or both, enrolled at 4–11 months, randomized to regular peanut consumption or avoidance, assessed at 60 months. Among the 530 initially skin-test-negative, avoidance ran 13.7% allergic versus 1.9% consumption (P<0.001); among the 98 weakly positive, 35.3% versus 10.6% (P=0.004). Serious adverse events didn't differ
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Du Toit G, Roberts G, Sayre PH, et al. (2015). Randomized trial of peanut consumption in infants at risk for peanut allergy. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(9), 803-813. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1414850>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2020). 婴幼儿喂养健康教育核心信息. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-08/01/content_5531915.htm>；Perkin MR et al. (2016). Randomized Trial of Introduction of Allergenic Foods in Breast-Fed Infants. New England Journal of Medicine, 374(18), 1733-1743. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1514210>（备注里那项一般婴儿试验 EAT）
- Note: **Only thinned peanut butter or peanut powder stirred into food — never whole nuts.** The health commission's core information is plain: "whole peanuts, nuts, and jelly are easily aspirated, causing choking; infants and toddlers should avoid them." Choking response: Section 13, item 26. This entry targets high-risk children — severe eczema or existing egg allergy. **Medical evaluation precedes introduction — never home trials**: LEAP skin-tested every child, excluding reactions over 4 mm. General-population evidence runs much weaker: in the 1,162-infant EAT trial, by group assignment early introduction ran 5.6% food allergy versus 7.1% routine, nonsignificant (P=0.32), significant only among actual compliers (2.4% versus 7.3%) — an analysis prone to inflation, the authors themselves conceding per-protocol failed to establish efficacy while confirming safety. Chinese official feeding documents take no position on early-versus-avoid; this entry follows the international trials. Timing: LEAP began at 4 months, China starts solids at 6 (item 4) — high-risk start age and method follow the doctor.

### 13. Newborn jaundice red flags — yellow within 24 hours, deepening, unarousable or refusing feeds, pale stools: same-day hospital
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration and bilirubin testing, under a few hundred yuan; phototherapy usually runs about two in-hospital days. The hard part: going while the baby still looks fine.
- In plain terms: Over 80% of newborns yellow slightly and self-resolve. Yellow within 24 hours, deepening yellow, unusual sleepiness or unarousability, feeding refusal, floppy or stiff body — same-day hospital. Pale stools suggest biliary atresia — prompt workup. Bilirubin high enough injures the brain for life.
- Benefit: The AAP 2022 guideline: "more than 80% of newborns develop some jaundice." High bilirubin causes acute bilirubin encephalopathy and kernicterus — "a permanent, disabling neurologic condition" presenting with athetoid cerebral palsy, upgaze palsy, enamel hypoplasia, and hearing loss. Jaundice "within 24 hours of birth" ranks among risk factors for severe hyperbilirubinemia. The NHS parent guidance splits two tiers: emergency — jaundice with unusual drowsiness or unarousability, feeding refusal, floppy or stiff body, or seizures, or jaundice before 24 hours; prompt appointment — jaundice past 24 hours worsening or persisting, dark or brown urine, pale cream stools. Globally, 2010 saw about 480,000 newborns with Rh disease or extreme hyperbilirubinemia: 24% died, 13% developed kernicterus, and over 83% of kernicterus survivors carried one or more impairments. Pale stools suggest biliary atresia, where Kasai surgery within 60 days markedly improves outcomes: Taiwan's stool-color card in every child-health handbook lifted within-60-day surgery from 60% in 2004 to 74.3% in 2005; a nationwide study found the card era cutting biliary-atresia mortality from 26.2% to 15.9% — before-after, not trial
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Kemper AR, Newman TB, Slaughter JL, et al. (2022). Clinical Practice Guideline Revision: Management of Hyperbilirubinemia in the Newborn Infant 35 or More Weeks of Gestation. Pediatrics, 150(3), e2022058859. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2022-058859>；NHS. Jaundice in babies（2026-03-24 审阅）. <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jaundice-newborn/>；NICE (2010, 2023 修订). Jaundice in newborn babies under 28 days (CG98), 1.2.6、1.7.1. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg98/chapter/Recommendations>；Bhutani VK, Zipursky A, Blencowe H, et al. (2013). Neonatal hyperbilirubinemia and Rhesus disease of the newborn: incidence and impairment estimates for 2010 at regional and global levels. Pediatric Research, 74(Suppl 1), 86-100. <https://doi.org/10.1038/pr.2013.208>；Hsiao CH, Chang MH, Chen HL, et al. (2008). Universal screening for biliary atresia using an infant stool color card in Taiwan. Hepatology, 47(4), 1233-1240. <https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.22182>；Lee M, Chen SC, Yang HY, et al. (2016). Infant Stool Color Card Screening Helps Reduce the Hospitalization Rate and Mortality of Biliary Atresia: A 14-Year Nationwide Cohort Study in Taiwan. Medicine, 95(12), e3166. <https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000003166>；国家卫生计生委 (2017). 国家基本公共卫生服务规范（第三版），0～6 岁儿童健康管理服务规范. <https://www.nhc.gov.cn/ewebeditor/uploadfile/2017/04/20170417104506514.pdf>
- Note: Phototherapy uses special light on the skin to convert bilirubin into excretable forms. No sun-substitutes: the AAP holds home sunlight can't be made both safe and burn-free — not recommended. No water or glucose water to "flush" — likewise unrecommended. Eyeballing depth misestimates bilirubin; NICE forbids eyes-only judgment — when unsure, measure. Under 38 weeks, prior phototreated siblings, and underfed breastfed infants run heavier. Follow-up per the national basic-service rules: home visit within a week of discharge, clinic follow-up at 28–30 days, jaundice checked both times; doctor-scheduled bilirubin rechecks run on schedule. Term infants yellowing past 14 days, preterm past 21: NICE requires conjugated-bilirubin testing and stool inspection. Jaundice never delays vaccination (item 3). Newborn screening: Section 27, item 13.

### 14. Vegan during pregnancy or nursing: supplement B12 daily; the baby's solids need animal foods — insistence on full vegan needs a doctor
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: B12 supplements run tens of yuan a bottle, one daily; eggs, meat, and fish in solids cost little. The hard part: shifting a household convinced vegan is healthier.
- In plain terms: B12 lives almost only in meat, eggs, and dairy. Vegan mothers without supplements run deficient milk, and babies typically fail at 4–8 months: poor growth, developmental delay, and prolonged deficiency can leave irreversible neurologic damage. Solids need animal foods daily.
- Benefit: The US CDC's 2003 report covered two children, both breastfed by vegan mothers, both B12-deficient, both failing to grow and develop. The first mother had eaten vegan 7 years; her child hospitalized at 15 months with severe anemia and MRI-diffuse cerebral atrophy — improving on B12 but still speech- and language-delayed at 32 months. Maternal dietary deficiency is the leading infant cause; breastfed infants typically present at 4–8 months; prolonged deficits can leave irreversible neurologic damage scaled by severity and duration. Reliable unfortified sources are animal foods alone — meat, dairy, eggs; plant B12 mostly lies unusable by humans; fortified cereals, soy milk, and nutritional yeast serve as stable sources. The report asks vegans — pregnant and lactating women especially — to know their B12 intake or consult nutrition professionals. ESPGHAN's 2017 complementary-feeding position: all infants need iron-rich complementary foods including meat or fortified items; vegan diets only under appropriate medical or dietetic supervision, with parents understanding the serious consequences of non-supplementation. The national 2020 core feeding information: solids no fewer than 4 kinds daily, including at least one animal food, one vegetable, and one grain-tuber
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2003). Neurologic impairment in children associated with maternal dietary deficiency of cobalamin—Georgia, 2001. MMWR, 52(4), 61-64. <https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5204a1.htm>；Fewtrell M, Bronsky J, Campoy C, et al. (2017). Complementary Feeding: A Position Paper by the European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology, and Nutrition (ESPGHAN) Committee on Nutrition. Journal of Pediatric Gastroenterology and Nutrition, 64(1), 119-132. <https://doi.org/10.1097/MPG.0000000000001454>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2020). 婴幼儿喂养健康教育核心信息. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-08/01/content_5531915.htm>；Melina V, Craig W, Levin S (2016). Position of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics: Vegetarian Diets. Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, 116(12), 1970-1980. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.09.025>（备注里的反方）
- Note: Contested: the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics' 2016 position holds well-planned vegetarian and vegan diets appropriate at all life stages, pregnancy, lactation, infancy, and childhood included — while itself requiring reliable B12 sources, fortified foods or supplements, for vegans. The camps split on vegan infant feeding, not on B12 necessity. Lacto-ovo vegetarians run lower risk, though the second mother — 20 years vegetarian, nearly without meat, fish, or dairy — still failed. Early deficiency runs nonspecific; the CDC calls anemia markers unfit for screening. Poor growth or delay: volunteer the household's veganism at the doctor's. Don't refuse prescribed treatment for folk remedies — Section 16, item 4. Solids introduction: item 4; prenatal folic acid: Section 27, item 1.
