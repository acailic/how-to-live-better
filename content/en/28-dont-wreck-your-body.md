# 28. Don't wreck your body for looks

This section covers ways of "doing things to the body to change appearance": starving, pills, injections, surgery. These decisions are usually made by comparison with others, while the costs land entirely on you — and a good share never reverse.

This section judges nobody's chosen appearance — that's each person's own. The same goal can be pursued through "prescribed, credentialed, followed-up" channels or through "online shopping, beauty salons, self-dosing" — this section only prices the difference. The accounting counts what actually happens to bodies: blindness, hospitalization, death. The two medical-aesthetics entries add criminal liability for illegal practice.

### 1. Never control weight by extreme dieting, fasting, or purging — cut from the exercise side
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves the diet-product money.
- In plain terms: Anorexia nervosa runs death about 5.9 times the age-matched rate — 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years, with one in five of the dead by suicide. Its doorway is dieting: tracking students across 44 secondary schools for three years, severely dieting girls developed eating disorders at 18 times the rate of non-dieters. Control weight by moving more, never by starving.
- Benefit: Pooling 36 studies: anorexia nervosa accumulated 166,642 person-years, standardized mortality ratio 5.86 — about 5.9× the age-matched rate; 5.1 deaths per 1,000 person-years; about 1 in 5 of deaths by suicide. Bulimia ran SMR 1.93 and other eating disorders 1.92 — about 1.9×. The Victorian study followed 14–15-year-olds across 44 schools for 3 years, six waves: girls' new eating disorders ran 21.8 per 1,000 person-years; severe dieters developed them at 18× non-dieters, moderate dieters 5×. Adjusting for prior dieting and psychiatric symptoms, BMI, exercise, and sex no longer predicted onset. The authors: "in adolescents, controlling weight through exercise rather than food restriction appears to carry a lower risk of eating disorder onset"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Arcelus J et al. (2011). Mortality rates in patients with anorexia nervosa and other eating disorders. A meta-analysis of 36 studies. Arch Gen Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archgenpsychiatry.2011.74>；Patton GC et al. (1999). Onset of adolescent eating disorders: population based cohort study over 3 years. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.318.7186.765>
- Note: "Severe dieting" here means chronically compressing intake far below need — not skipping a meal or late-night snacks. Purging, post-binge guilt, and compulsive weighing belong in psychiatry or clinical psychology. This is a treatable illness, not a willpower matter. BMI-versus-mortality sits in Section 2, item 32 — that entry covers long-term weight bands, not weight-loss methods.

### 2. Before any injection, thread, or scalpel: check two things — the license says "medical cosmetology," and the hands belong to an attending physician
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, minutes to check both. The Medical Institution Practice License hangs in the lobby; physician credentials verify in the health authorities' registry.
- In plain terms: Anything piercing skin or entering the body counts as medical care — done only in institutions licensed for "medical cosmetology" by registered physicians. Beauty salons, studios, and hotel rooms mean illegal practice: serious cases draw up to 3 years; injury, 3–10; death, 10-plus.
- Benefit: The Medical Cosmetology Service Measures define it: "repair and reshaping of human appearance and body form using surgical, pharmaceutical, medical-device, and other traumatic or invasive medical techniques." Breaking skin, entering with needles, or entering the body counts as care. Articles 8 and 24 require the Practice License with the medical-cosmetology service category registered; licenses without that entry can't perform. Article 11 requires attending physicians (the hands doing the work) to hold registered physician qualifications — cosmetic surgery requiring 6-plus years of relevant clinical experience, cosmetic dentistry 5-plus, cosmetic TCM and dermatology 3-plus each. Article 20 requires written disclosure of indications, contraindications, risks, and precautions with signed consent. Criminal Law Article 336 governs illegal practice — practicing medicine without physician qualification: serious, up to 3 years plus fines; grave bodily harm, 3–10 plus fines; death, 10-plus plus fines
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 卫生部 (2002). 医疗美容服务管理办法（卫生部令第 19 号，第二条、第八条、第十一条、第十六条、第二十条、第二十四条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2003/content_62198.htm>；全国人大 (1997). 刑法（第三百三十六条）. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>
- Note: Article 16 confines cosmetology projects to cosmetology institutions or hospitals and clinics with cosmetology departments — door-to-door injections and studio procedures are non-compliant by location alone. Article 20 requires guardian consent for minors. Facials, massage, and nail care are life cosmetology outside this regime — but anything reaching needles or blades is medical.

### 3. Facial filler: avoid the nasal bridge, glabella, and forehead — and never economize into unlicensed injections
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A different injection site, or none.
- In plain terms: Among filler-induced blindness cases, over half involved the nose, then glabella, forehead, and nasolabial folds — over 80% hyaluronic acid. Of the blinded, two in ten fully recovered, one-sixth partially, and nearly one in five suffered brain injury. No rescue reliably works — avoidance is the only strategy.
- Benefit: Pooling world-literature cases: January 2015–September 2018 added 48 cases of partial or complete vision loss after filler injection. Leading sites: nose 56.3%; glabella 27.1%; forehead 18.8%; nasolabial folds 14.6%. 81.3% involved hyaluronic acid. 43.8% showed skin changes; 18.8% central-nervous-system complications. 10 cases (20.8%) fully recovered vision, 8 (16.7%) partially, the rest not. The authors: management varied widely, with no treatment proven consistently effective
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Beleznay K et al. (2019). Update on Avoiding and Treating Blindness From Fillers: A Recent Review of the World Literature. Aesthet Surg J. <https://doi.org/10.1093/asj/sjz053>
- Note: These numbers count published cases — numerators without denominators, so "the odds of blindness per injection" is uncomputable; only which sites fail and that rescue mostly fails. Grade B for that reason. Legitimate practice reduces risk by routine: mapping vessels, blunt cannulas over sharp needles, retrograde low-volume injection, hyaluronidase on standby — every step requiring a trained physician, itself a reason to shun informal channels. Verify per item 2 beforehand.

### 4. Never buy diet pills, slimming coffee, weight-loss candy, or "enzyme plums" promising fast loss
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, saving the product money. The hard part: accepting that "kilos a week" doesn't exist.
- In plain terms: The commonest undeclared additive is sibutramine, a withdrawn diet drug. In overweight people with heart disease or diabetes taking it over three years, nonfatal heart attacks rose about 30% and strokes about 36% — for 1.7 extra kilograms lost. By sampling time, you've already swallowed it.
- Benefit: The SCOUT trial enrolled 9,804 subjects — all 55-plus, overweight or obese, with cardiovascular disease or type 2 diabetes — randomized to drug or not, average 3.4 years. The composite endpoint (nonfatal infarction, nonfatal stroke, resuscitated cardiac arrest, cardiovascular death) ran 11.4% vs 10.0%: HR 1.16 (95% CI 1.03–1.31, P=0.02), about 16% higher, the true value between 3% and 31% higher. Nonfatal infarction 4.1% vs 3.2%, HR 1.28 (1.04–1.57), about 30% higher. Nonfatal stroke 2.6% vs 1.9%, HR 1.36 (1.04–1.77), about 36%. Cardiovascular and all-cause death didn't rise. Post-randomization, the sibutramine arm averaged 1.7 kg extra loss. The former food-and-drug regulator judged "risk outweighs benefit," halting sibutramine production, sale, and use in October 2010. Sibutramine and phenolphthalein are banned food additives, health foods included
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: James WPT et al. (2010). Effect of sibutramine on cardiovascular outcomes in overweight and obese subjects. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1003114>；天津市市场监督管理委员会. 警惕部分食品中违法添加「盐酸西布曲明」和「酚酞」. <https://scjg.tj.gov.cn/tjsscjdglwyh_52651/hdpt/cjwtyxfts/202007/t20200720_2972549.html>
- Note: SCOUT enrolled high-risk cardiac and diabetic patients; it doesn't prove healthy young users suffer identically — but since high-risk can't be sorted out, the regulator withdrew it outright. The other common adulterant, phenolphthalein, is a laxative disturbing electrolytes and provoking arrhythmia in chronic excess. How to tell: legitimate diet drugs are prescription items with verifiable approval numbers at pharmacies and hospitals. "Coffee," "candy," "plums," and "meal replacements" promising weekly kilos shouldn't be pharmacologically active by definition — activity means adulteration. The original 2010 notice page returns 412 locally; the provincial reprint carries both quoted sentences verbatim-checkable.

### 5. Never use anabolic steroids ("muscle shots," "oral gear") to build muscle
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free, saving the drug money. The hard part: accepting slow muscle.
- In plain terms: Men caught by gym doping tests ran 3× the age-matched death rate and over double the yearly clinic visits. Acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each struck over one in ten users. The drugs are prescription-only in China — anyone selling them to you is already breaking the law.
- Benefit: Danish registry work matched each user with age-peers: 545 men testing positive for androgenic steroids at gym doping controls (2006-01-03 to 2018-03-01) against 5,450 controls, plus 644 refusers and 6,440 controls for replication. Users died at 3× controls (HR 3.0, 95% CI 1.3–7.0) — the true multiple somewhere between 1.3 and 7. Median yearly hospitalizations or visits: 0.81 vs 0.36 (P<0.0001). Acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction each affected over 10% of users — at least one in ten — all significantly above controls (P<0.0001). The replication agreed. The Anti-Doping Regulations, Article 7: strict state management of listed banned substances, no unit or individual producing, selling, importing, or exporting illegally; protein-anabolic agents and peptide hormones are prescription-managed — available only on prescription, "protein-anabolic agents" being the regulatory name for this class
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Horwitz H et al. (2019). Health consequences of androgenic anabolic steroid use. J Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.12850>；国务院 (2004). 反兴奋剂条例（国务院令第 398 号，第七条、第九条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2004/content_63129.htm>
- Note: Contested: cohort, not randomized — users may be risk-takers generally, so the 3× can't book entirely to the drug, and the interval 1.3–7.0 runs wide. But acne, gynecomastia, and erectile dysfunction striking one-plus in ten are visible effects less vulnerable to that bias. The class appears in regulations as "protein-anabolic agents" — that phrase means exactly this. Ordinary protein powder and creatine sit outside this entry. The trouble: "miracle muscle" products may adulterate — same test as item 4: legitimate drugs are prescription items with approval numbers; "muscle powders" and "nutrition products" promising weeks mean adulteration if they work.

### 6. Wanting weight-loss medication: get it prescribed at a hospital — never from a no-prescription online shop
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: One registration and a trip — tens of yuan, half a day.
- In plain terms: Prescription drugs sell only on prescription, online included, with genuine prescriptions and real names. No consultation, no prescription, ship-on-order means either the product isn't what it claims or the shop breaks the law — and when things go wrong, nobody answers for your dose and contraindications. One registration buys a physician standing guard.
- Benefit: The drug management measures require retailers to observe the prescription/non-prescription classification; prescriptions retained no fewer than five years; pharmacists' absence flagged, prescription drugs unreviewed unsold; prescription drugs never open-shelf. The online sales measures, Article 9: prescription sales to individuals online require genuine, reliable prescription sources with real-name registration; Article 10: prominent risk warnings on every product page, no package inserts or purchase before prescription review — the buy button itself shouldn't exist pre-review; Article 8: specially controlled drugs — vaccines, blood products, narcotics, psychotropics, medical toxics, radiopharmaceuticals, and precursor chemicals — never sell online
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家市场监督管理总局 (2023). 药品经营和使用质量监督管理办法（总局令第 84 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2023/issue_10846/202311/content_6917322.html>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第八条至第十条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Note: The statutes verify verbatim, but "how many harms prescription-filling prevents" has no quantified study — Grade B for unquantifiability. Items 5 and 7's steroids and sex hormones run the same rules. At genuinely drug-warranted weight levels, the physician first reads BMI, comorbidity, and contraindications — a judgment you cannot buy.

### 7. Sex-hormone drugs: only on prescription with scheduled follow-up — never online, never self-titrated
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Regular registrations and blood-work follow-up — hundreds yearly, several half-days.
- In plain terms: This entry doesn't debate whether to use, only how. Transgender women on estrogen developed blood clots clearly above age-peers — about 4 extra per 1,000 over two years, 17 over eight. Such risk needs scheduled monitoring; online purchases and self-adjustment leave nobody watching.
- Benefit: Kaiser Permanente records followed 2,842 transgender women and 2,118 transgender men, averaged 4.0 and 3.6 years, each matched to roughly ten cisgender controls by birth year, race, site, and enrollment — 48,686 cisgender men and 48,775 cisgender women. Transgender women's venous thromboembolism exceeded controls: versus cisgender men, 4.1 per 1,000 over 2 years (95% CI 1.6–6.7) and 16.7 over 8 (6.4–27.5); versus cisgender women, 3.4 (1.1–5.6) and 13.7 (4.1–22.7). Ischemic stroke and infarction ran near-parity overall, but differences sharpened among those starting hormones during follow-up. Transgender men: evidence insufficient. The authors: "the findings suggest the need for continued vigilance for the vascular effects of estrogen in transgender hormone therapy"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Getahun D et al. (2018). Cross-sex Hormones and Acute Cardiovascular Events in Transgender Persons: A Cohort Study. Ann Intern Med. <https://doi.org/10.7326/M17-2785>；国家市场监督管理总局 (2022). 药品网络销售监督管理办法（总局令第 58 号，第九条）. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2022/content_5717002.htm>
- Note: The authors themselves flag the study's weakness: system-external self-purchasing is invisible, so the "on hormones" line was never cleanly drawn — strengthening the case for formal follow-up. The study answers only vascular events, never whether hormone therapy is worthwhile — a different evidence base. Every sex-hormone use shares this: contraceptives, menopausal therapy, testosterone. Self-purchasing, forum-guided titration, and never-checked clot and liver panels place the risk exactly in the unwatched stretch, regardless of purpose. Domestically: endocrinology or the relevant specialty at proper institutions — assessment, prescription, follow-up.

### 8. If "feeling ugly" has reached mirror-checking and fixating on the next fix: get a body-image assessment before any surgery
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: One psychiatry or clinical-psychology registration, tens of yuan. The hard part: going before booking surgery.
- In plain terms: About 15% of cosmetic-surgery patients carry body dysmorphic disorder — magnifying flaws others barely see into life-disrupting preoccupation — and about 13% of dermatology aesthetics seekers, both majorities women. Fixing one spot, they fixate on the next; surgery doesn't resolve it. One registration to check, then decide.
- Benefit: Pooling 33 studies systematically: plastic-surgery patients' body-dysmorphic-disorder prevalence 15.04% (studies spanning 2.21%–56.67%) — about 15 per 100 seekers, ranging 2 to 57 by study; mean age 34.54±12.41, 74.38% female. Dermatology aesthetics patients: 12.65% (4.52%–35.16%), mean age 27.79±9.03, 76.09% female. The authors recommend active screening and multidisciplinary management for high-risk patients
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ribeiro RVE (2017). Prevalence of Body Dysmorphic Disorder in Plastic Surgery and Dermatology Patients: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis. Aesthetic Plast Surg. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s00266-017-0869-0>
- Note: Study estimates run 2.21%–56.67% — questionnaires and populations differ wildly, so read "the share isn't small, screening is worth it," never 15.04% as precise — Grade B for that. The vast majority seeking aesthetics carry no disorder; this entry targets the small share with "repetitive, persistent, work- and socially-disruptive" preoccupation surgery cannot fix. Clues: long daily hours checking or concealing a body part, social and photographic avoidance, fixing one spot then fixating on the next.

### 9. Orthodontics: an orthodontically credentialed doctor examining in person, with scheduled visits — never mail-order aligners at home
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration and follow-up above the mail-order price, with periodic clinic trips over a one-to-two-year course. The hard part: sustaining visits.
- In plain terms: Clear aligners are class-2 medical devices domestically, their labeling requiring credentialed orthodontic supervision and scheduled review. The US FDA has authorized no aligners sold directly to consumers without dentist prescription and supervision. Unexamined use risks uneven bite, unintended tooth movement, pain, even tooth loss.
- Benefit: The NMPA device-review center's 2022 guidance classifies bracketless aligners "category II," requiring labeling to state "wearing during treatment requires the guidance of a physician with orthodontic professional qualifications" plus "eating, brushing, and periodic follow-up requirements," and "product names should not contain vacuous or hypothetical conceptual terms such as 'invisible.'" The FDA: "The FDA has not authorized clear aligners that are sold directly to consumers without a prescription and supervision by a dentist or orthodontist," and "In-person examination by a dentist or orthodontist can help lower the risks of uneven bite, unintended tooth movement, pain, and tooth loss" — scheduled in-person visits confirm teeth moving safely as planned. Both are regulatory documents without incident rates
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国家药品监督管理局医疗器械技术审评中心 (2022). 无托槽矫治器注册审查指导原则（2022 年第 41 号）. <https://www.cmde.org.cn/flfg/zdyz/zdyzwbk/20230103095116100.html>；国家卫生健康委办公厅等 (2023). 关于进一步推进口腔医疗服务和保障管理工作的通知（国卫办医政发〔2023〕11 号，第五条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202309/content_6903786.htm>；U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2026). Clear Dental Aligners（页面 2026 年 9 月 9 日更新）. <https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/dental-devices/clear-dental-aligners>
- Note: Benefit "small" because incident rates weren't found — judgment by consequence. Domestic regulator warnings on "mail-order at-home aligners" weren't found, so this entry rests on registration requirements and the FDA. Orthodontics is medical care: aesthetic tooth-straightening institutions need the corresponding license entry and credentialed operators — verify per item 2. The four-department 2023 notice targets unlicensed dental operations and non-health-technical staffing. Orthodontics stays unreimbursed — Section 24, item 12.
