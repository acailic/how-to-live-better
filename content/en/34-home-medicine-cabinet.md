# 34. Don't let the home medicine cabinet hurt you

Fever reducers, painkillers, cold medicines, stomach remedies, anti-diarrheals — most buyable without prescription. No prescription never means no rules. This section skips which drug for which illness, picking only the one-action items that dodge grave outcomes.

All entries count mortality endpoints — liver failure, gastric bleeding, neonatal kidney failure included. The benefit mostly reads "one fewer serious incident," without research quantifying percentages; magnitudes follow consequence severity, with each note stating its grounds.

Already covered: swallowed drugs or cleaners first aid (Section 13, item 20); finishing chronic medication (Section 16, item 1); prescription review for online drug purchases (Section 28, item 6).

Beneficiaries: you and family — the top two tiers.

### 1. Before taking two cold or pain medicines together: read the ingredients — acetaminophen may appear only once
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Reading each box's ingredient list, a minute or two.
- In plain terms: Many cold medicines embed acetaminophen; two boxes together means a double dose, and overdose injures the liver. Among US acute liver failure from it, nearly three in ten died awaiting transplant. Domestic labels cap at 2 grams daily — and no alcohol while dosing.
- Benefit: 22 US hospitals prospectively registered 662 acute liver failures over 6 years; 275 (42%) were acetaminophen-caused, median ingestion 24 grams. Of the 275, 131 (48%) were unintentional overdoses — and 38% of those had taken two-plus products containing the ingredient. 27% died before transplant, 8% were transplanted. The 2020 NMPA revision requires OTC labels to state: overdose causes serious liver injury, with a suggested daily maximum of 2 grams, and avoidance of combination with other acetaminophen or antipyretic-analgesic products. US rules require the first liver warning to name three risks: exceeding the daily maximum, combining with other acetaminophen products, and drinking 3-plus alcoholic drinks daily while dosing.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Larson AM, Polson J, Fontana RJ, et al.; Acute Liver Failure Study Group (2005). Acetaminophen-induced acute liver failure: results of a United States multicenter, prospective study. Hepatology, 42(6):1364-1372. <https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.20948>；国家药监局关于修订对乙酰氨基酚常释及缓释制剂说明书的公告（2020年第15号）及附件2《对乙酰氨基酚常释制剂非处方药说明书修订要求》（湖南省药品监督管理局转载）. <http://mpa.hunan.gov.cn/mpa/xxgk/tzgg/wjtz/202003/t20200311_11809335.html>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(1)(iii)(A) Liver warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Note: Acetaminophen is also paracetamol. Compound cold medicines whose generic names carry "氨酚" (as in 氨酚伪麻, 氨咖黄敏) contain it. Unsure — show every box to the pharmacist. Grade B: the US figures are case registries without controls — how many incidents label-reading prevents is uncomputable. Benefit "large" by consequence: nearly three in ten of those liver failures died outright.

### 2. Children's fevers: no aspirin, no nimesulide, no metamizole — including metamizole-containing cold medicines, none for minors
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One glance at generic names and ingredients when buying.
- In plain terms: None of the three for children's fevers. Aspirin links to Reye's syndrome — brain and liver injury, about 30% mortality in US surveillance, cases falling over 90% after warnings. Nimesulide is banned under 12; metamizole under 18.
- Benefit: Reye's syndrome brings encephalopathy and hepatic steatosis, typically after influenza or varicella. US national surveillance 1981–1997 logged 1,207 cases under 18; 82% had detectable salicylates (the aspirin class). Overall case fatality 31%. Warnings began in 1980 — that year's 555 cases were the peak; from 1987, never more than 36 yearly. The 2011 SFDA notice bans nimesulide oral preparations under 12; the 2020 NMPA notice bans metamizole tablets under 18, with compound products (青蒿安乃近片, 重感灵片 and capsules) banned equally.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Belay ED, Bresee JS, Holman RC, Khan AS, Shahriari A, Schonberger LB (1999). Reye's syndrome in the United States from 1981 through 1997. New England Journal of Medicine, 340(18):1377-1382. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM199905063401801>；关于加强尼美舒利口服制剂使用管理的通知（国食药监安〔2011〕209号）. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gzwj/gzwjyp/20110515145101552.html>；国家药监局关于修订安乃近相关品种说明书的公告（2020年第34号）及附件1至3. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20200317171201660.html>
- Note: Metamizole isn't first-line for adults either — labels warn of agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, and anaphylactic shock, reserved short-term for grave illness without alternatives. 重感灵 ("double-cold relief") sounds ordinary and contains metamizole. What children should take and by weight: ask pediatricians or pharmacists. Under 3 months, 38 °C means hospital directly (Section 20, item 8). Grade B: surveillance data — the case decline followed warnings temporally, without trials. Benefit "large" by the 555-to-36 drop.

### 3. Over 60, prior gastric bleeding, or on anticoagulants or steroids: ask a doctor before ibuprofen-type painkillers
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One question to a doctor or pharmacist.
- In plain terms: Ibuprofen, naproxen, and aspirin-type painkillers injure the stomach. In high-dose trials, ibuprofen users ran about 4× the risk of gastric bleeding or perforation. Older age, prior bleeding, concurrent anticoagulants or steroids, and daily drinking raise it further.
- Benefit: The class — nonsteroidal anti-inflammatories, common ones being ibuprofen, naproxen, and diclofenac — was pooled against placebo across 280 randomized trials, 124,513 people. Upper gastrointestinal perforation, obstruction, or bleeding versus placebo: ibuprofen RR 3.97 (95% CI 2.22–7.10, about 4×), naproxen 4.22 (2.71–6.56), diclofenac 1.89 (1.16–3.09). All roughly double heart-failure risk. US rules require OTC stomach-bleeding warnings naming six higher-risk groups: over 60; prior ulcer or bleeding; on anticoagulants or steroids; combining another of the class; 3-plus drinks daily while dosing; dosing beyond label directions or duration.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Coxib and traditional NSAID Trialists' (CNT) Collaboration; Bhala N, Emberson J, Merhi A, et al. (2013). Vascular and upper gastrointestinal effects of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs: meta-analyses of individual participant data from randomised trials. Lancet, 382(9894):769-779. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)60900-9>；21 CFR 201.326(a)(2)(iii)(A) Stomach bleeding warning. <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-201/subpart-G/section-201.326>
- Note: The paper's conclusions speak to high-dose use; a day or two at label doses isn't separately quantified. Many compound cold medicines carry the class — stacked with painkillers, duplication — read ingredients first. Doctor-ordered low-dose aspirin for clot prevention: never stop it yourself, never add another painkiller on top. Menstrual use and gynecology referral: Section 3, item 25.

### 4. Children under 2 with colds: never self-dose compound cold and cough medicines
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — and it saves the drug money.
- In plain terms: The NMPA requires warnings on 14 pediatric compound cold medicines: parents shouldn't self-administer under 2; use under physician or pharmacist guidance. In pediatric trials, cough and antihistamine ingredients beat placebo not at all.
- Benefit: The 2021 No. 57 announcement revised 14 products' labels — including 氨酚麻美 oral solution, 小儿氨酚黄那敏 granules, 小儿氨酚烷胺 granules. The added warning: "parents or guardians are not advised to use this product on infants under 2 without professional guidance; use under physician or pharmacist direction." Precautions add "use strictly per label dosage to avoid overdose" and "avoid combining cold medicines with the same or similar active ingredients." A Cochrane review, 29 randomized trials with 10 in children: none of the classes beat placebo in children — cough medicines, antihistamines, antihistamine-plus-decongestant, cough-plus-bronchodilator. 21 trials reported adverse events, with antihistamine and dextromethorphan preparations running more.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家药监局关于修订氨酚麻美口服溶液等14个品种药品说明书的公告（2021年第57号）及附件. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/directory/web/nmpa/xxgk/ggtg/ypggtg/ypshmshxdgg/20210423105404141.html>；Smith SM, Schroeder K, Fahey T (2014). Over-the-counter (OTC) medications for acute cough in children and adults in community settings. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (11):CD001831. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001831.pub5>
- Note: Many of the 14 carry "氨酚" — acetaminophen — doubling up with fever reducers (item 1). One reviewed trial found honey beating placebo, but never under 1 year (Section 20, item 6). Grade B: few trials per class, no pooled numbers. Benefit "medium" by judgment: no demonstrated benefit in small children, with overdose and duplication the risks.

### 5. Past 20 weeks of pregnancy: no self-dosing with ibuprofen-type painkillers
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free.
- In plain terms: Past about 20 weeks, most amniotic fluid is made by the fetus's kidneys; this class can injure those kidneys and thin the fluid. US reports include neonatal deaths. Doctor-prescribed low-dose aspirin is exempt.
- Benefit: The FDA's October 2020 safety communication: NSAID use from about 20 weeks can cause fetal renal problems leading to oligohydramnios — covering both prescription and OTC, including aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, and celecoxib. Reviewing reports to July 2017, the FDA found 35 cases of oligohydramnios or neonatal renal impairment, all serious — including 5 neonatal deaths, all with renal failure. Literature shows most oligohydramnios resolving within 72 hours to 6 days of stopping. The FDA advises avoiding the class past 20 weeks absent explicit medical direction; the 81-mg low-dose aspirin prescribed for pregnancy-specific conditions is exempt; for pain and fever, acetaminophen and others, after asking pharmacist or doctor.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2020-10-15). FDA recommends avoiding use of NSAIDs in pregnancy at 20 weeks or later because they can result in low amniotic fluid. Drug Safety Communication. <https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic>
- Note: The FDA reminds that many OTC cold, flu, and sleep products carry the class — read ingredients. US OTC labels previously warned only for the final 3 months; this moved the line to about 20 weeks. Low-dose aspirin for preeclampsia: Section 27, item 5. Grade B: adverse-event reports without denominators — dosed-but-fine counts unknown. Benefit "medium" by judgment: severe outcomes, rare occurrence.

### 6. Self-bought omeprazole for reflux: 7 days maximum; swallowing difficulty, vomiting blood, or black stools means hospital
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Follow the label's day count.
- In plain terms: Omeprazole enteric tablets went OTC in 2022 — with labels limiting self-use to 7 days and no repeat within two months. Swallowing difficulty or pain, vomiting blood, bloody or black stools: don't self-dose — those signal serious conditions. The drug can also mask stomach cancer and delay diagnosis.
- Benefit: The 2022 No. 68 announcement converted omeprazole enteric tablets to OTC with a label template: indication "short-term relief of heartburn and acid reflux from excess stomach acid"; use not exceeding 7 days, unrelieved symptoms prompting physician or pharmacist consultation; no repeat within 2 months, recurrence prompting immediate care; never during swallowing difficulty or pain, vomiting blood, bloody or black stools; marked weight loss, repeated vomiting, or those alarm symptoms require ruling out malignancy first, since treatment may mask symptoms and delay diagnosis; over-55s with new or changed symptoms should consult; clopidogrel users should avoid concurrent omeprazole.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家药监局关于奥美拉唑肠溶片处方药转换为非处方药的公告（2022年第68号）及附件2《非处方药说明书范本》. <https://www.nmpa.gov.cn/yaopin/ypggtg/20220826171517123.html>
- Note: Clopidogrel is the anti-clot drug common after stents and infarctions; omeprazole weakens it — ask first. Grade B: label rules without outcome studies. Benefit "medium" by judgment: what's avoided is delayed gastric bleeding or cancer, while most reflux stays benign.

### 7. Ordinary colds: don't ask the doctor for antibiotics
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — and it saves the drug money.
- In plain terms: Antibiotics don't speed cold recovery; adults on them ran over double the adverse-reaction risk. Purulent runny nose under 10 days gains no certain benefit either.
- Benefit: A Cochrane review pooled 6 randomized trials, 1,147 people. Antibiotics versus placebo for colds: no significant difference in unimproved or persistent symptoms (RR 0.83, 95% CI 0.60–1.14, nonsignificant). Adverse reactions ran 1.8× placebo (1.01–3.21) — adults 2.62× (1.32–5.18), children no increase (0.91, 0.51–1.63). Acute purulent rhinitis under 10 days: no clear benefit (RR 0.73, 0.47–1.13) with more adverse reactions (RR 1.46, 1.10–1.94, about 46% higher). The authors recommend against routine antibiotics in either condition.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Kenealy T, Arroll B (2025). Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 11:CD000247. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD000247.pub4>
- Note: This 2025 republication's search ran to 2013. Antibiotics are prescription-only domestically — pharmacies sell on prescription, and leftovers aren't for self-dosing. Benefit "medium" by judgment: adverse reactions prevented, not deaths.

### 8. Diarrhea: rehydrate first with oral rehydration salts; children under 5 get no anti-diarrheal drugs
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A box of ORS runs a few to a dozen-odd yuan — keep one at home.
- In plain terms: The priority in diarrhea is replacing water, not stopping stools. Mix ORS per label — never substitute juice drinks, whose sugar worsens dehydration. Under 5: no loperamide-type anti-diarrheals — severe cases bring paralytic ileus, potentially fatal.
- Benefit: WHO's treatment manual states plainly: "anti-diarrheal" drugs and antiemetics offer no practical benefit in acute or persistent childhood diarrhea — they neither prevent dehydration nor improve nutrition, some carry dangerous, sometimes fatal side effects, and must never be given under 5. Motility-slowing drugs like loperamide can cause severe paralytic ileus, fatal, and may prolong infection. Drinks too sweet in sugar draw water into the gut causing hypernatremic dehydration — soft drinks and commercial juice beverages named. WHO's recommended low-osmolarity formula (245 mOsm/L) cut unplanned IV infusions 33% versus the old formula; a Cochrane pool of 8 trials concurred (OR 0.59, 95% CI 0.45–0.79, about 40% fewer).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: World Health Organization (2005). The treatment of diarrhoea: a manual for physicians and other senior health workers, 4th revision. <https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/43209>；Hahn S, Kim S, Garner P (2002). Reduced osmolarity oral rehydration solution for treating dehydration caused by acute diarrhoea in children. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (1):CD002847. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD002847>
- Note: Buy by osmolarity on the label — the low-osmolarity formula. Bloody stools usually mean bacterial dysentery — medical care and antibiotics, not home rehydration alone. Benefit "medium" by judgment: the numbers compare two ORS formulas, not drinking versus not.

### 9. Painkillers: no more than 15 days a month, compound painkillers no more than 10 — or the headache itself may be drug-caused
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard parts: counting dosing days and not popping pills reflexively.
- In plain terms: Chronic headache met with daily painkillers can worsen the headaches. The international classification calls it medication-overuse headache: ordinary painkillers 15-plus days monthly, compounds 10-plus, for over 3 months, qualify. Most improve on withdrawing the overused drug.
- Benefit: ICHD-3 sets the criteria: a pre-existing headache disorder plus 15-plus headache days monthly plus regular overuse beyond 3 months. Non-opioid painkillers — acetaminophen, aspirin, ibuprofen — threshold at 15-plus days monthly; compound painkillers (two-plus analgesic classes, or added caffeine or codeine, e.g., acetaminophen plus codeine) at 10-plus; alternating non-opioids sum their days. The classification notes multinational epidemiology placing over half of 15-plus-day headachers in this category, with most patients improving on withdrawal and preventive treatment working better.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Headache Classification Committee of the International Headache Society (2018). The International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition. Cephalalgia, 38(1):1-211. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0333102417738202>；ICHD-3 在线版 8.2 Medication-overuse headache（含 8.2.3、8.2.5）. <https://ichd-3.org/8-headache-attributed-to-a-substance-or-its-withdrawal/8-2-medication-overuse-headache-moh/>
- Note: Painkiller names carrying "咖" generally add caffeine — compound, 10 days. Headache days climbing monthly means neurology, not more pills. Grade B: diagnostic thresholds, not trials. Benefit "medium" by judgment: headache-free days regained, deaths uninvolved.

### 10. Expired and leftover drugs: clear them out periodically, package into hazardous-waste bins — never keep them home
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One annual medicine-cabinet sweep, a dozen-odd minutes.
- In plain terms: Of children's unsupervised ingestions, nearly half are prescription tablets; about three in ten of those belong to grandparents. Clearing leftovers removes one thing a child can find. Cleared drugs go packaged into hazardous waste, or to pharmacies collecting old medicines.
- Benefit: US emergency surveillance estimated about 640,000 ED visits (640,161) for unsupervised medication ingestions under 6 from 2004 to 2013 — about 59,000 (59,092) in 2013 alone. 2010–2013: oral prescription tablets led at 45.9%, opioids (13.8%) and benzodiazepines (12.7%) topping the prescriptions. Five US poison centers surveyed callers in 2017: of children ingesting prescriptions, 30.7% took a grandparent's; over half (51.5%) of the prescriptions had been removed from original packaging before the child reached them. The domestic National Hazardous Waste Catalog (2025 edition) lists household waste medicines and packaging as hazardous household waste; Shanghai's sorting guide files waste medicines — expired drugs, capsules, tablets, inner packaging — under hazardous waste, "discarded medicines should be deposited with packaging intact," with pharmacies' collections staged by district market regulators. The FDA calls take-back sites the best disposal route.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Lovegrove MC, Weidle NJ, Budnitz DS (2015). Trends in Emergency Department Visits for Unsupervised Pediatric Medication Exposures, 2004-2013. Pediatrics, 136(4):e821-e829. <https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2015-2092>；Agarwal M, Lovegrove MC, Geller RJ, et al. (2020). Circumstances Involved in Unsupervised Solid Dose Medication Exposures among Young Children. Journal of Pediatrics, 219:188-195.e6. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2019.12.027>；生态环境部等 (2024). 国家危险废物名录（2025年版），附录《危险废物豁免管理清单》第 1 项. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_11906/202503/content_7011162.html>；上海市绿化和市容管理局 (2024). 上海市生活垃圾分类投放指引（2024版）（沪绿容〔2024〕278号）. <https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/gwk/search/content/40b9feaa-53db-472f-8a6b-2c3cf0465def>；上海市绿化和市容管理局、上海市环境保护局、上海市食品药品监督管理局 (2018). 关于规范生活垃圾分类有害垃圾全程管理的通知. <https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw12344/20200813/0001-12344_57440.html>；U.S. Food and Drug Administration (2025). Where and How to Dispose of Unused Medicines. <https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/where-and-how-dispose-unused-medicines>
- Note: Sorting catalogs vary locally — your building's posted list governs; Shanghai files herbal dregs under wet waste. Kept medicines stay in original boxes — never decanted into pill organizers — stored out of children's reach (Section 13, item 20). Leftover antibiotics: item 7. US fallback — mixing with coffee grounds or cat litter before trash, toilet-flushing for a few potent opioids — is the American practice; domestically, follow local sorting. Grade B: registries without a cleared-versus-not comparison. Benefit "small": the numbers are ED visits, not deaths, and the sweeping effect is unmeasured.

### 11. On cephalosporins or metronidazole: no alcohol during and 7 days after — including alcohol-bearing medicines like 藿香正气水 and cough syrups
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. A week-plus without alcohol, and an ingredient check for ethanol before other medicines.
- In plain terms: Some cephalosporins and metronidazole-class drugs meeting alcohol cause flushing, palpitations, chest tightness, and vomiting — severe cases dropping blood pressure. Not just liquor: alcohol-bearing medicines like 藿香正气水 and cough syrups trigger it too. Nothing from dosing through 7 days after.
- Benefit: The disulfiram-like reaction: Fujian's drug regulator's 2023 safety note — certain cephalosporins before or after drinking can trigger reactions that "seriously harm health or even endanger life": flushing, sweating, palpitations, breathing difficulty, blood-pressure drops, anaphylactic shock. Per the Pharmacopoeia's clinical handbook: cefazolin, ceftriaxone, cefoperazone, metronidazole, tinidazole, furazolidone, among others. "Alcohol and alcohol-containing medicines and foods are prohibited within 7 days of dosing" — chocolate liqueurs and 藿香正气水 the examples, the inhibited enzyme needing 4–5 days to recover. A Guangzhou hospital's 2-year series of 82 cases: cephalosporins 57 (69.51%), nitroimidazoles 10 (12.20%); alcohol from beverages 40 (48.78%), from ethanol-bearing medicines 31 (37.80%) — 藿香正气水, cough syrups, motherwort fluidextract; 68 mild, 9 moderate, 5 severe, relief within 1–3 hours, no deaths. The US metronidazole label bars alcohol during and 3-plus days after, including propylene-glycol products.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 福建省药品监督管理局（福建省药品科普与监管数据中心）(2023). 2023年"全国安全用药月"原创科普小知识——为什么吃头孢不能喝酒. <https://yjj.scjgj.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/kpzl/qgaqyyy/202312/t20231215_6334595.htm>；房财富, 涂星, 刘韬, 黄红兵, 邱孟, 梁蔚婷 (2015). 我院双硫仑样反应82例分析. 中国药房, 26(29):4089-4091. <https://doi.org/10.6039/j.issn.1001-0408.2015.29.19>；Pfizer (2026). FLAGYL 375 (metronidazole) capsules, prescribing information: Contraindications, Interaction with Alcohol. DailyMed. <https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a2883ca1-5a9a-4259-9d80-46ab67274384>
- Note: Not every cephalosporin does it — laypeople can't tell them apart, so treat all alike, the cheapest rule. When prescribed, mention recent drinking. The note suggests 3 days before dosing too, admitting no authoritative interval exists. Flushing and palpitations: stop the alcohol; mild cases self-resolve, severe need the hospital. No alcohol with acetaminophen either — item 1. Grade B: case series and labels, no controls. Benefit "small": all 82 eased within hours, 5 severe, no deaths.
