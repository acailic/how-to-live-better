# 29. After a major blow

A loved one dies, a grave diagnosis lands, a job disappears, a marriage ends. In research these share one feature: it isn't only mood that breaks — the body does too, with risk peaking in the first weeks to the first year. This section isn't about "moving on"; it covers what must be watched during this stretch, which decisions must wait, and when to register at a clinic. Most entries count death probability. Four money-and-benefits entries count money: grief counseling (item 9), 12356 and psych clinics (item 11), deferring irreversible decisions (item 12), death as debt repayment (item 13) — the two kinds never convert. For those with no kin and no friends, item 6 grounds "have someone watch you."

Procedures, the body, and collectible money at a death: Section 25. Elders' guardianship and wills: Section 17. Unemployment benefits: Section 7. Exercise and light for low mood: Sections 3 and 22. Suicidal thoughts — 12356 first: Section 1, item 25; timescales: item 32; aftermath of rescue: item 33. None repeated here.

### 1. In the days right after a death: don't stay alone; those with heart disease, hypertension, or diabetes keep medication; crushing chest pain means 120
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Company, medication as usual.
- In plain terms: The day after losing a loved one, heart-attack risk runs 21.1× baseline, declining daily after. Bereaved partners 60–89 ran about doubled heart attacks and strokes within 30 days, normalizing after a month. The cardiovascularly ill never stop medication these days; crushing pain, breathlessness, one-sided weakness: 120 now.
- Benefit: 1,985 acute-infarction patients compared within themselves across periods: 270 (13.6%) lost a significant person within 6 months pre-infarction, 19 within 1 day. Within 24 hours of the death, infarction incidence rose 21.1× (95% CI 13.1–34.1, the true multiple roughly 13–34), declining day by day. In absolute terms: among those with a 10-year infarction risk of 5%, one extra per 1,394 newly bereaved; at 20% risk, one per 320. The UK study matched 30,447 bereaved partners 60–89 against 83,588 comparable controls: 30-day infarction or stroke — bereaved 50 (0.16%) versus controls 67 (0.08%); IRR 2.20 (1.52–3.15), about 2.2×, infarction 2.14 (1.20–3.81), stroke 2.40 (1.22–4.71), weakening past 30 days. Ninety days added non-infarction acute coronary syndromes, IRR 2.20 (1.12–4.29), and pulmonary embolism, IRR 2.37 (1.18–4.75)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mostofsky E, et al. (2012). Risk of acute myocardial infarction after the death of a significant person in one's life: the Determinants of Myocardial Infarction Onset Study. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.111.061770>；Carey IM, et al. (2014). Increased risk of acute cardiovascular events after partner bereavement: a matched cohort study. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.14558>
- Note: Hand the pill box to someone else. No long drives. No drinking through — alcohol's account sits in Section 2. Recognizing infarction and stroke: Section 13. Both studies are retrospective, inseparable from background incidence — but both agree on direction and window. Those with nobody to hold the box and unavoidable solitude: item 6.

### 2. The week a grave diagnosis lands: never collect the report alone; non-treatment decisions wait
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Bring someone to the report; postpone major non-treatment decisions a few days.
- In plain terms: In the first week after a cancer diagnosis, suicide risk runs 12.6× and cardiovascular death 5.6× the undiagnosed. By year one, suicide falls to 3.1× — the harder the cancer, the sharper the rise. So bring someone that day, don't go home alone, and leave resignation and home sales to the next clinic visit.
- Benefit: Swedish registers followed 6,073,240 people, 1991–2006. Versus the undiagnosed: first-week suicide RR 12.6 (95% CI 8.6–17.8) — the true multiple roughly 8.6–17.8 — at 2.50 per 1,000 person-years; by one year, 3.1 (2.7–3.5; 0.60 per 1,000 person-years). Cardiovascular death: first week 5.6 (5.2–5.9; 116.80 per 1,000 person-years); first 4 weeks 3.3 (3.1–3.4; 65.81). Risk falls steeply within the first year and rises most for the least survivable cancers. Within-patient self-comparison agreed
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Fang F, et al. (2012). Suicide and cardiovascular death after a cancer diagnosis. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1110307>
- Note: Cancer data — other grave diagnoses lack equal-weight originals. Events are genuinely rare: 2.50 per 1,000 person-years in week one — stated not to frighten but to explain why someone should sit beside you those first days. Long-term management, reimbursement, follow-up: Sections 16 and 24. No one to bring: item 6 swaps that person for neighbors and community lists.

### 3. After job loss: fix sleep, insurance, and a job-search rhythm — no idling at home
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. File unemployment registration, claim benefits, schedule the search like a workday. The hard part: rising on time with nobody pushing.
- In plain terms: The unemployed run about 60% higher all-cause mortality than the employed — men and the early-career most, the first decade highest. Removing smoking and drinking trims about a quarter of the excess — the part you can govern. File first; while claiming, unemployment insurance covers your medical premiums.
- Benefit: Pooling 42 studies, 235 mortality estimates, over 20 million people: after age-adjustment, unemployment's all-cause mortality averaged HR 1.63 — about 60% above the employed; men above women; early- and mid-career above late; the first decade highest, declining after. Studies including health behaviors ran 24% lower HR — about a quarter of the excess trimmed
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). Losing life and livelihood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of unemployment and all-cause mortality. Social Science & Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2011.01.005>
- Note: Retrospective, causal direction unsortable — the already-ill lose jobs more. But "lifestyle adjustment removes 24%" marks the actionable share. Benefits and insurance continuation: Section 7; severance and never signing voluntary resignation: Section 19.

### 4. A loved one lost to suicide, accident, or homicide: don't count on toughing it out — seek professional help actively
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 12356 is free; psych clinics bill ordinary registration. The hard part: speaking up.
- In plain terms: Those widowed by suicide ran about 80% higher (men) and 70% (women) five-year rates of newly diagnosed mental disorder than the general population — and 70%–100% above those widowed by other causes. Such bereavement feeds isolation and shame — precisely the reason to seek help before you "feel better."
- Benefit: Danish registers followed 6.7 million adults, 1980–2014, over 136 million person-years. Spousal suicide bereavement: new mental-disorder diagnosis within 5 years — IRR men 1.8 (95% CI 1.6–2.0), women 1.7 (1.6–1.8) against the general population; against other-cause widowed, men 1.7 (1.5–1.9), women 2.0 (1.9–2.2). These bereaved also ran more suicidal behavior, death, and municipal assistance, plus liver cirrhosis and sleep disorders. A 57-study review links suicide bereavement to: partners' elevated suicide risk; parents' psychiatric admission after a child's suicide; mothers' suicide risk after adult children's; and children's depression after parental suicide. Suicide-bereaved report more stigma and abandonment than other violent-loss bereaved
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Erlangsen A, et al. (2017). Association Between Spousal Suicide and Mental, Physical, and Social Health Outcomes: A Longitudinal and Nationwide Register-Based Study. JAMA Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0226>；Pitman A, et al. (2014). Effects of suicide bereavement on mental health and suicide risk. The Lancet Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(14)70224-X>；Kristensen P et al. (2012). Bereavement and mental health after sudden and violent losses: a review. Psychiatry. <https://doi.org/10.1521/psyc.2012.75.1.76>（备注里那份综述）
- Note: Sudden violent deaths — accidents, homicide, disaster — run higher PTSD, depression, and stuck grief than illness deaths, and slower recovery. Your own suicidal thoughts: Section 1, item 25.

### 5. The first half-year of widowhood: hand meals, sleep, chronic medication, and follow-up to one fixed person
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. One named person — child, sibling, friend — visiting or calling on a schedule. The hard part: entrusting it aloud.
- In plain terms: Under 6 months bereaved, mortality runs about 40% above the non-bereaved, falling to about 15% after; men about 23%, women a gap small enough to be chance. This isn't outlasted by attitude. Land the medication, meals, and follow-up on one named person — never on your own memory.
- Benefit: Pooling 15 prospective studies, 2,263,888 people: bereavement under 6 months ran death RR 1.41 (95% CI 1.26–1.57) — about 40% higher — falling to 1.14 (1.10–1.18) after 6 months. Men RR 1.23 (1.18–1.28); women 1.04 (1.00–1.08, nonsignificant — possibly chance). Under-65 versus over-65 didn't differ. Another pool of 123 papers, 1,377 estimates, 500-million-plus: adjusted HR 1.23 (1.19–1.28) overall — about 23% — men 1.27 (1.19–1.35), women 1.15 (1.08–1.22)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Moon JR, et al. (2011). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS ONE. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0023465>；Shor E, et al. (2012). Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis and meta-regression. Demography. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-012-0096-x>
- Note: Retrospective with unremovable confounds — couples share lifestyles and diseases. But "the first half-year far exceeds the rest" held across two independent pools, marking a care window. Men run higher risk and ask less. Households with newly widowed elders: settle accounts, guardianship, and wills per Section 17. Nobody available — solo-living, childless, child-predeceased: item 6 hands it to community and phone.

### 6. No kin, no friends: swap "the person watching you" for three things — a neighbor with a key, the community visit roster, and emergency contacts in the phone
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part: telling the neighbor and the residents' committee "I live alone; something just happened."
- In plain terms: Alone, two things matter: someone finds you if things go wrong, and medication and meals happen on time. The latter runs on lists and alarms, never memory; the former on three things: a spare key with a neighbor, the community's visit roster, and emergency contacts with the phone never off.
- Benefit: 44,573 outpatients with atherothrombotic risk or history, enrolled internationally in REACH (December 2003–December 2004), followed 4 years; 8,594 (19%) lived alone. Four-year all-cause death: solo 14.1% versus cohabiting 11.1%; cardiovascular death 8.6% versus 6.8% — both log-rank P<0.01. Gaps varied by age (interaction P=0.03): 45–65, 7.7% versus 5.7%, adjusted HR 1.24 (95% CI 1.01–1.51) — about 24% higher; 66–80, 13.2% versus 12.3%, HR 1.12 (1.01–1.26); past 80, 24.6% versus 28.4%, HR 0.92 (0.79–1.06) — no elevation. The 2022 ten-department opinion mandates visit-and-care services for solo-living, empty-nest, left-behind, disabled, severely disabled, and special family-planning elders, delivered "by regular home visits, phone and video calls, remote monitoring," with townships surveying and village committees assisting into grid management — by end-2025 "ensuring a 100% monthly visit rate for special-difficulty elders" and "no fewer than one monthly visit for disabled special-difficulty elders," while promoting smart call systems, smart water meters, health monitors, and elder-care alert devices that "alert promptly on anomalies, simultaneously notifying emergency contacts," with service staff "dialing emergency numbers first" on urgent findings
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Udell JA, et al. (2012). Living alone and cardiovascular risk in outpatients at risk of or with atherothrombosis. Archives of Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2012.2782>；民政部等十部门 (2022). 关于开展特殊困难老年人探访关爱服务的指导意见（民发〔2022〕73 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-10/13/content_5718017.htm>
- Note: How to land the three: (1) spare key or temporary door code with a neighbor or the property office plus one sentence — no friendship required, only someone who knows you're inside; (2) tell the residents' committee you live alone and ask onto the visit roster — solo-living, empty-nest, and child-predeceased elders are explicit service targets; ask about free smart pendants and meters; (3) set emergency contacts and the medical ID; no silent mode, no power-offs this week. Grade B twice: solo-living mortality is retrospective, "the frail live alone more" unremovable — the paper itself flags confirmation needed — and the visit-care document covers only elders; under-60s get the grid workers' duty to "detect family upheaval, unemployment, dropout crisis risks" (item 11). Why "someone in the room" matters: Section 13, item 1 — 79.2% of out-of-hospital arrests happen at home; with compressions, survival-to-discharge runs 16.1% versus 3.9% without. Two bonus moves: the funeral itself runs through funeral homes, community health, police, and the housing fund (Section 25) — schedule one outing daily; and the hotline takes repeat calls (item 11). Long-term solo living carries its own account — odds 1.32, up about 30% (Section 22, item 9) — outside this week's business.

### 7. After a parent's death: tell the child the truth, let them attend the farewell, don't ship them off "for a change of scene"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part: hurting yourself while explaining clearly.
- In plain terms: Children losing a parent before 18 ran about 50% higher early-adult mortality — about 80% after accidental or suicidal parental death, about 30% after illness. What the household controls: honest telling, farewell participation, uninterrupted schooling, no relocation to relatives.
- Benefit: All persons born in Denmark 1968–2008 and Sweden 1973–2006 plus 89.3% of Finland's 1987–2007 births — over 7.3 million, of whom 189,094 lost a parent before 18. Their all-cause mortality ran 50% above the non-bereaved (mortality ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.43–1.58); parental unnatural death (accident, suicide) 1.84 (1.71–2.00), about 80%; natural death 1.33 (1.24–1.41), about 30%. Highest where the child's cause of death matched the parent's
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Li J, et al. (2014). Mortality after parental death in childhood: a nationwide cohort study from three Nordic countries. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001679>
- Note: No "gone on a long trip" stories; custody lands in writing. Grade B isn't about the numbers — three whole-nation cohorts are Grade A material — but the bridge from numbers to "how to tell, whether to attend" lacks trials; the authors flag missing parent-child relationship and lifestyle data, and the elevation blends constitution with bereavement's long shadow. Guardianship and property: Section 17; support and schooling: Section 18.

### 8. Grief still stuck after six months, life not running: register at psychiatry or clinical psychology
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration plus treatment — trials averaged 16 sessions over 19 weeks. The hard part: walking yourself to the desk.
- In plain terms: About one in ten bereaved adults gets stuck: past six months still yearning to the point of not eating, working, or parenting; numbness; life meaningless. That isn't insufficient toughness — it has diagnostic criteria and dedicated treatment: 51% response for targeted therapy versus 28% for generic, and faster.
- Benefit: Pooling 14 studies of adults bereaved non-violently and not in psychiatric care: prolonged-grief-disorder prevalence 9.8% (95% CI 6.8–14.0) — about 10 per 100 — rising with age. The validation study interviewed 291 bereaved at 0–6, 6–12, and 12–24 months: the core feature is intense yearning for the deceased plus at least five of nine — emotional numbness, disbelief, meaninglessness, distrust, distress over the loss, difficulty accepting, identity confusion, avoidance, inability to move on — daily or functionally disabling, persisting past 6 months and disrupting daily life. The randomized trial, 95 patients, 16 sessions averaging 19 weeks: complicated-grief treatment 51% response versus interpersonal therapy 28% (P=0.02), faster onset; number needed to treat 4.3
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lundorff M, et al. (2017). Prevalence of prolonged grief disorder in adult bereavement: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Affective Disorders. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2017.01.030>；Prigerson HG, et al. (2009). Prolonged grief disorder: Psychometric validation of criteria proposed for DSM-V and ICD-11. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121>；Shear MK, et al. (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial. JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.293.21.2601>
- Note: Don't count months rigidly — the real test is "eating, sleeping, working, parenting disrupted"; matching means register. Departments run as psychiatry, psychology, or clinical psychology at public psychiatric hospitals. Names vary — complicated grief, prolonged grief disorder — and duration thresholds run 6 or 12 months by standard. The 9.8% pools heterogeneous studies, and the authors caution against generalizing.

### 9. Don't buy grief counseling up front — first check whether your grief is actually stuck (item 8's symptoms)
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free, and it saves the fee.
- In plain terms: Counseling for every bereaved person buys little and fades — most recover on their own. For those clearly failing to adapt, treatment works as well as for other psychological problems. So match against item 8 first; spend the money only on a match.
- Benefit: Pooling 61 controlled studies: universal psychological intervention for the bereaved shows small benefit at end-of-treatment, fading to nothing on re-examination — because the untreated comparison recovers with time. Interventions admitting only the clearly maladjusted work as well as psychotherapy elsewhere; unselected studies muddy exactly because controls self-heal. A 14-trial pool: treatment of the already-affected beats control at end and follow-up, effects growing; preventive intervention for the not-yet-affected doesn't work. The 205-person study from pre-loss through 6 and 18 months post-loss found five trajectories — common grief, chronic grief, chronic depression, improvement during bereavement, and resilience — with resilience the most common and common grief relatively rare
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Currier JM, et al. (2008). The effectiveness of psychotherapeutic interventions for bereaved persons: a comprehensive quantitative review. Psychological Bulletin. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.5.648>；Wittouck C, et al. (2011). The prevention and treatment of complicated grief: a meta-analysis. Clinical Psychology Review. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2010.09.005>；Bonanno GA, et al. (2002). Resilience to loss and chronic grief: a prospective study from preloss to 18-months postloss. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1150>；Stroebe M et al. (2007). Health outcomes of bereavement. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61816-9>（备注里那份综述）
- Note: "Not everyone needs it" is not "nobody should." Item 4's high-risk bereavement and item 8's stuck grief are exactly where evidence supports treatment. A Lancet review concludes likewise: grief isn't illness, most need no professional intervention — effort belongs on the high-risk and on those with complicated grief, post-bereavement depression, and stress disorders.

### 10. After separation or divorce: don't drop insurance, don't lean on alcohol
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Keep insurance; stop drinking. The hard part: the stretch invites leaning on drink.
- In plain terms: The separated and divorced die earlier than the married, men and the younger rising most — not necessarily caused by divorce itself, since the divorced differ in health and income beforehand. What you govern: keep insurance and checkups running; never prop up on tobacco and alcohol.
- Benefit: Pooling 32 prospective studies — over 6.5 million people, 160,000 deaths, 755,000-plus divorces, 11 countries: separated and divorced adults run significantly elevated early death versus the married, men and the younger above women and elders. Four pathways discussed: social selection (the already-different divorce more); resource interruption (income and coverage cut); health-behavior change (tobacco, alcohol, sleep); and chronic psychological stress
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sbarra DA, et al. (2011). Divorce and Death: A Meta-Analysis and Research Agenda for Clinical, Social, and Health Psychology. Perspectives on Psychological Science. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691611414724>
- Note: The paper states "significantly elevated" with group contrasts and no citable multipliers — so no numbers here. Benefit "medium" by "direction known, no applicable threshold." Property, debt, and betrothal gifts at divorce: Sections 8 and 10; children's arrangements: Section 18.

### 11. For someone to talk to: 12356 — minors and teens 12355 — and psych clinics register like any other
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Both lines free; psych clinics bill ordinary registration.
- In plain terms: To talk, call 12356, the free national psychological-assistance line; minors and teens have 12355, the youth service platform. For a doctor: psychiatry, psychology, or clinical-psychology outpatient registration. Grid workers and social workers are supposed to catch family upheaval, job loss, and dropout proactively.
- Benefit: The 2026 twenty-five-department plan directs: improving 12356's working systems, stability, and answer rates; building provincial 12356 platforms; linking with 110; strengthening 12355 in coordination. The 2030 targets: psychological-counseling rooms or social-work stations delivering services in 80%-plus of villages and urban communities; every public psychiatric hospital and every county (district) with at least one hospital offering psychology outpatient service; provincial 12356 platforms built. Crisis teams comprise psychiatrists, psychotherapists, counselors, social workers, and volunteers — with grid workers, social-work professionals, and volunteers tasked to detect family upheaval, unemployment, and dropout risks early (national; issued March 2026)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 关于印发健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案的通知（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Note: Grade B because village rooms and county clinics are 2030 targets, not current universal facts; 12356 and 12355 answer today. 12356's hours: Section 1, item 25. Counseling and psychotherapy differ — diagnosis and prescriptions belong to psychiatrists, and treatment runs inside licensed institutions; hourly "healing" and "spiritual consulting" sit outside this system.

### 12. The first three months after the blow: every irreversible decision waits
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free — waiting may cost opportunities. The hard part: the feeling that opportunity won't wait.
- In plain terms: Your judgment about "opportunities" is at its worst now. Those claiming to reach the dead, to multiply your money, urging resignation and relocation, or recruiting you as a partner — all will call. Anything exceeding a month's income or costing dearly to reverse waits three months. Speak it first to someone with no stake; never decide the same day.
- Benefit: What's avoided: irreversible property dispositions at your worst judgment — selling homes, resigning, lump-summing survivor payments into investments, hasty remarriage, guaranteeing others' debts. Sums typically run tens of thousands to a house — hence "medium." Official documents likewise treat "family upheaval, unemployment, dropout" as crisis risks for community detection — this state is already flagged as one needing outside hands
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 作者经验，无直接文献；「家庭变故、失业、失学」作为心理危机风险的表述见国家卫生健康委等 25 部门 (2026). 健全社会心理服务体系和危机干预机制实施方案（国卫医政发〔2026〕8 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065035.htm>
- Note: No stake-free person nearby: speak it to 12356 (item 11), or write it down and reread after three days. "Three months" is the author's line, unsupported by literature — hence C. Common scams against the bereaved and elders: Section 17 (investment retirement, house-for-retirement) and Section 6 (fortune-telling). Guarantees and IOUs: Section 8. Some money goes the other way — collected, not deferred: housing funds, social-insurance benefits, work-death sums (Sections 25 and 19).

### 13. Death doesn't repay debts: life insurance won't pay within two years, work injury won't recognize it, and debts carve the estate first
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Just arithmetic.
- In plain terms: Life policies don't pay for suicide within two years — only the policy's cash value returns. Work injury recognizes nothing; the three work-death sums vanish. Debts carve the estate first, family inheriting the remainder — and carrying the health toll from item 4 besides.
- Benefit: Insurance Law Article 44 governs death-benefit contracts: suicide within two years of formation or reinstatement voids the death benefit, save suicides while legally incapable of civil conduct, with the insurer refunding only the policy's cash value. Life coverage typically runs hundreds of thousands — hence "large." The Work Injury Regulations, Article 16(3): self-harm or suicide is never work injury or deemed work injury — voiding Article 39's three sums (funeral grant at 6 months of local average wages, dependents' pensions, and the one-time death benefit at 20× the prior-year national urban per-capita disposable income). Civil Code Article 1159: estate division first settles taxes and debts; Article 1161: heirs repay only up to the inherited value, and renunciation waives liability
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2015 修正). 中华人民共和国保险法第四十四条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=2c909fdd678bf17901678bf7c4060811>；国务院 (2010 修订). 工伤保险条例（国务院令第 586 号）第十六条、第三十九条. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>；全国人大 (2020). 中华人民共和国民法典第一千一百五十九条、第一千一百六十一条. <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>
- Note: This entry counts only money; this section and Section 1 cover not arriving here. Article 44 covers only the first two years — stated for completeness, never as a timetable. The family's health toll: item 4 (spouses bereaved by suicide ran 70%–80% higher five-year mental-disorder incidence). Thoughts appearing: Section 1, items 25 and 32; rescue aftermath: item 33. Work-death standards: Section 19, item 15; estate procedures: Section 25, item 9.
