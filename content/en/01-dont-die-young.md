# 1. Don't die young

This section covers three things: accidental and injury deaths — car crashes, fires, poisoning; the handful of vaccines and screenings with the hardest evidence behind them; and the body signals that mean you should go get checked. The numbers in the Benefit column come from randomly assigned trials, from studies that tracked large groups of people for many years, or from official statistics. The Cost column is the author's rough estimate at market prices — useful for comparing entries against each other, not for citing.

Entries in this section are grouped by theme, with item numbers in parentheses.

**On the road**: buckle up (item 1), wear a helmet (item 2), no speeding or drunk driving (item 9), child safety seats (item 10).

**Fire, gas, and electricity at home**: install smoke alarms (item 3), replace gas hoses when they expire (item 4), never bring e-bikes into stairwells (item 6), keep fire extinguishers and a first-aid kit (item 26), fit the guard before using an angle grinder (item 42).

**Accidents involving children, the elderly, and the outdoors**: window limit locks (item 11), never take your eyes off children near water (item 12), balance training after 60 (item 13), don't eat wild mushrooms (item 5), don't pick up metal objects of unknown origin (item 36), measure ice thickness before stepping onto wild ice (item 41).

**Vaccines**: get your hepatitis B surface markers checked (item 14), tetanus (item 15), the HPV vaccine (item 16), the flu vaccine (item 20), the shingles vaccine (item 21), the pneumococcal vaccine (item 22).

**Screening and check-ups**: measure blood pressure (item 7), fasting glucose (item 8), breast cancer screening (item 17), cervical cancer screening (item 18), colorectal cancer screening (item 19), test for *H. pylori* (item 23), low-dose chest CT (item 24), bone density scans (item 39), osteoporosis drugs (item 40).

**Body signals and sexual health**: visible blood in urine (item 27), check your heart before treating erectile problems (item 28), losing weight and quitting smoking improve erections (item 29), condoms and never sharing needles (item 30), get tested for HIV (item 31), PrEP (item 38).

**Psychological crises**: call 12356 if you're thinking about suicide (item 25), tell one person first if you're suicidal (item 32), gambling until you borrow to keep gambling (item 37), the cost of surviving pesticide ingestion or gas inhalation (item 33), the cost of surviving a fall from height (item 34), the cost of living with one kidney (item 35), ask people directly whether they've thought about suicide (item 43).

### 1. Buckle up — front seat and back
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. 2 extra seconds every time you get in a car.
- In plain terms: Wearing a seat belt in the front seat roughly halves your chance of being killed in a crash. In the front of a light truck it drops by about 60%. Of the people who died in cars in the US in 2022 and whose belt use could be determined, half were unbuckled.
- Benefit: NHTSA, the US road-safety authority, estimates that seat belts reduce the risk of fatal injury for front-seat car occupants by 45%. For the front of a light truck, by 60%. Among people killed in passenger cars in the US in 2022 whose belt use could be determined, 50% were unrestrained. WHO's figure is that seat belts reduce the risk of death for vehicle occupants by up to 50%. WHO estimates 248,099 road-traffic deaths in China in 2021 — 17.4 per 100,000 people
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; WHO GHO RS_196/RS_198 (China, 2021). <https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/RS_196?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20%27CHN%27>
- Note: Buckle up in the back too. In the same report, 60% of those killed in second-row seats were unbuckled. The 45% and 60% figures are estimates NHTSA computed from crash databases (citing Kahane 2015), not randomized trials. WHO's China death toll is a modeled estimate, several times higher than what traffic police registered. The two count different things; this book uses WHO's figure.

### 2. Wear a helmet on motorcycles and e-bikes — and fasten the strap
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 100–300 yuan for a helmet. 5 seconds to put it on.
- In plain terms: A helmet with the strap fastened cuts a motorcyclist's risk of dying in a crash by roughly 40%, and head injuries by about 70%. Fasten the strap tight — a helmet dangling loose on your head doesn't count as worn.
- Benefit: A Cochrane review pooled multiple studies: helmeted motorcyclists had a 42% lower risk of death. Original value OR 0.58, 95% CI 0.50 to 0.68. Head injuries down 69% (OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.38)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Liu BC et al. (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3>
- Note: The strap must be fastened — a helmet hanging from the handlebar doesn't count. The data above come from motorcycles. There is no direct research on e-bikes; the numbers are extrapolated, because the way heads get injured in a collision is the same.

### 3. Install a smoke alarm; if you burn coal or use gas heaters indoors in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A smoke alarm runs 30–100 yuan; a CO alarm 50–150 yuan. Replace the battery once a year after installation.
- In plain terms: A working smoke alarm cuts your chance of dying in a house fire by around 60%. In two out of five fatal fires the residents were asleep — your nose alone won't wake you. If you burn coal or use gas for heating in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm. In 2018, 11,500 people in China died of CO poisoning; in the three winter months, seven in ten of those deaths happened at home.
- Benefit: A North Carolina study of residential fires compared households where a fire happened with those where it didn't. A working smoke alarm in the home cut the risk of death by about 60%. Original value OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.83. In US occupied-home fatal fires from 2018 to 2020, 24% of homes had no smoke alarm, and in 41% someone was asleep when the fire started. China reported 11,523 deaths from carbon monoxide poisoning in 2018. Of those deaths, 72.59% in December, 67.42% in January, and 66.48% in February occurred at home
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Marshall SW et al. (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018-2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf> ; You J et al. (2020). Number of Deaths due to Carbon Monoxide Poisoning by Month and by Place of Death — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2020.008>
- Note: Why only B: for smoke alarms there is just this one study, which worked backwards by comparing households that had fires with those that didn't — not a randomized trial. For CO alarms themselves there is no study showing reduced mortality. The benefit here is inferred from the fact that poisoning deaths cluster in winter and at home.

### 4. Replace gas hoses and stoves when they expire; never modify pipes yourself; you can turn away door-to-door "gas company" salesmen
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A compliant gas hose costs a few dozen yuan. Stoves need replacing every few years. When you swap one, glance at whether the other is past its date.
- In plain terms: A compliant hose costs a few dozen yuan. Replacing it on time is a legal obligation written into China's Urban Gas Management Regulations. Modifying pipes or removing your gas meter yourself is explicitly banned by the same regulations. They also say a gas company cannot require you to buy its designated products or services. When someone shows up at your door saying you "must" buy something, you can simply refuse.
- Benefit: The regulations make "promptly replacing gas-burning appliances and connecting hoses that the state has ordered phased out or that have reached the end of their service life" a user obligation: once your stove or hose hits its limit or is phased out, you must replace it. The regulations also list "installing, altering, or removing in-home gas facilities and gas metering devices without authorization" among prohibited acts — DIY pipe changes and meter removal included. They further state that gas suppliers may not require users to buy products they designate, nor to accept services they provide
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院 (2010). 城镇燃气管理条例（国务院令第 583 号）第二十七条：「燃气用户应当遵守安全用气规则，使用合格的燃气燃烧器具和气瓶，及时更换国家明令淘汰或者使用年限已届满的燃气燃烧器具、连接管等」；第二十八条禁止行为含「（一）擅自操作公用燃气阀门」「（二）将燃气管道作为负重支架或者接地引线」「（四）擅自安装、改装、拆除户内燃气设施和燃气计量装置」；第二十条燃气经营者不得「（六）要求燃气用户购买其指定的产品或者接受其提供的服务」；第二十九条用户可就收费、服务向燃气管理等部门投诉，「有关部门应当自收到投诉之日起15个工作日内予以处理」. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1758214.htm>；深圳市住房和建设局 (2024). 安全用气小知识（二）：「立即关闭家里的燃气总阀门」「迅速开门、开窗进行通风」「一定不要开、关家里的电灯、抽油烟机等电器」「人员应该迅速撤离，转移到安全的地方，并且及时拨打燃气公司的报警电话」. <https://zjj.sz.gov.cn/zcjzts/content/post_11882410.html>；张家港市应急管理局 (2024). 燃气安全使用知识：「不要情急之下打开排气扇进行通风」「应远离现场之后才能打电话求助」. <https://www.zjg.gov.cn/zjgszwz/yjaqzs/202411/72b4fad7399c48378302460c6ce2d55b.shtml>
- Note: The regulation text can be checked word for word, but it only establishes that replacing hoses on time is your obligation — not how many accidents timely replacement prevents. No official statistic was found for how much on-schedule hose replacement reduces gas accidents, hence Grade C. Some people come to your door in the name of a "safety inspection" and finish by selling self-closing valves and alarms. Clause 20(6) forbids gas companies from requiring you to buy their designated products — you can refuse, and you can complain. If you smell gas: first shut the main gas valve (for bottled gas, the cylinder valve). Then open doors and windows to ventilate — don't switch on the exhaust fan. Don't turn lights or the range hood on or off, and keep lighters away. Then leave, and only call the gas company's emergency line once you're away from the scene. Carbon monoxide poisoning is a separate matter — see Section 13.

### 5. Don't pick, buy, or eat wild mushrooms — no folk test works
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is giving up a seasonal wild delicacy.
- In plain terms: In 2025 alone there were 828 mushroom-poisoning outbreaks across China, 2,165 people poisoned, 13 dead. That same year, 34 species never before recorded as poisonous in China were newly identified. The silver-needle test, "bugs ate it so it's safe," "only colorful ones are toxic" — none of these hold up.
- Benefit: China CDC investigated 828 mushroom-poisoning incidents in 2025, involving 2,165 people and 13 deaths — a case fatality rate of 0.6%, meaning 0.6% of those poisoned died. From 2019 to 2024 there were 276–676 incidents per year, with case fatality between 0.87% and 2.86%. In 2025 alone, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, 34 of them recorded for the first time as causing poisoning in China
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mushroom Poisoning Outbreaks — China, 2025. China CDC Weekly (2026): "In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents across 27 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs), affecting 2,165 individuals and causing 13 deaths - a case fatality rate of 0.6%, the lowest of the past six years. In total, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, including 34 newly recorded in poisoning incidents in China." "From 2019 to 2024, the annual number of incidents ranged from 276 to 676, and the case fatality rate ranged from 0.87% to 2.86%." <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2026.120>；同刊 2024 年度报告 <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2025.106>
- Note: If you eat the wrong mushroom, vomit immediately, then get to a hospital taking the leftover mushrooms or photos of them. Mushroom poisoning comes in several distinct types, and identifying which mushroom you ate directly determines how doctors treat you. New poisonous species are recorded every year, so "the locals know which ones are safe" doesn't hold up as an assumption. The silver-needle test, boiling with garlic, "bugs ate it so it's fine," "only colorful ones are toxic" — none of these hold up.

### 6. Never bring an e-bike into the stairwell or elevator, and never charge it at home
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. The hard part is always using the shared charging point downstairs — a few dozen extra meters of walking.
- In plain terms: Wheeling an e-bike into a stairwell or elevator, or charging it at home, is explicitly banned by the Fire Safety Regulations for High-Rise Civil Buildings issued by the Ministry of Emergency Management. Individuals who don't comply face fines of 500–1,000 yuan; businesses, 2,000–10,000 yuan. The fine is the small thing — the point is that this kind of fire burns right across your only escape route.
- Benefit: The ministry's regulation states in so many words: "Parking electric bicycles in, or charging electric bicycles at, the public lobbies, evacuation walkways, stairwells, or emergency exits of high-rise civil buildings is prohibited." Lobbies, corridors, stairwells, and emergency exits — no parking and no charging in any of them. Those who refuse to correct it face fines of 500–1,000 yuan for individuals and non-business units, 2,000–10,000 yuan for businesses
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 应急管理部 (2021). 高层民用建筑消防安全管理规定（应急管理部令第 5 号，第三十七条、第四十七条）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/01/content_5628815.htm>；应急管理部 (2023). 关于政协第十三届全国委员会第五次会议第00969号提案答复的函：「2021年，全国共接报电动自行车火灾1.8万起，造成65人死亡、222人受伤，直接财产损失达2.21亿元，其中发生在建筑物内3782起，造成60人死亡、175人受伤」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/jytabljggk/zxwytadfzy/2022zx/202301/t20230119_440918.shtml>；应急管理部 (2025). 2025年1月15日新闻发布会：「火灾起数同比下降42.2%，伤亡人数下降超过90%」. <https://www.mem.gov.cn/xw/xwfbh/2025n01y15xwfbh_6337/>
- Note: Don't buy modified batteries, don't use no-name chargers, don't charge overnight unattended. Once a lithium battery ignites, it flashes over within seconds while giving off highly toxic smoke. A bike parked in a stairwell that catches fire burns right across your only escape route. The Ministry of Emergency Management's published figures: 18,000 e-bike fires nationwide in 2021, 65 deaths. Of those, 3,782 fires were inside buildings, killing 60 — nearly all of the dead indoors. After the 2024 crackdown, fires fell 42.2% year-on-year and casualties by more than 90%; the absolute counts for that year were not published.

### 7. Measure your blood pressure; if it's high, take medication and get it to target
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: An electronic blood-pressure monitor costs 100–200 yuan; 1 reading takes 1 minute. Most blood-pressure pills run a few to a few dozen yuan a month. The hard part is keeping taking them when you feel nothing.
- In plain terms: For every 10 mmHg your blood pressure comes down, your chance of a major cardiovascular event drops by about a fifth, stroke by 27%, heart failure by 28%, and death by 13%. Among Chinese people aged 35–75, 44.7% have high blood pressure; only 7.2% have it under control.
- Benefit: Pooled across 123 trials and over 610,000 people. For every 10 mmHg drop in systolic pressure, major cardiovascular events fall by about a fifth. Original value RR 0.80, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.83. Systolic is the higher of the two numbers in a blood-pressure reading. Stroke (brain attack) down about 27% (RR 0.73). Heart failure down about 28% (RR 0.72). All-cause mortality down 13% (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84 to 0.91). China screened 1.7 million people aged 35–75: 44.7% had hypertension. Of those, 44.7% knew it, 30.1% were on treatment, and only 7.2% had it controlled
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ettehad D et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Lu J et al. (2017). Prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension in China (China PEACE Million Persons Project). Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32478-9>
- Note: Go get one blood-pressure reading first. Fewer than 1 in 10 Chinese people with hypertension have it controlled, so that single reading is one of the highest-value moves in this book. What counts as "at target" (130 or 140) is still debated, but "knowing you have high blood pressure and bringing it down" is not.

### 8. If you're over 35 and overweight, get a fasting glucose test once — and every three years even if normal
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A fasting glucose test costs a dozen-odd yuan; an HbA1c a few dozen. One blood draw at a clinic.
- In plain terms: For a few dozen yuan and one tube of blood, you can catch prediabetes before any symptoms exist. The official US screening advice: people aged 35–70 who are overweight or obese should get tested. If normal, retest every three years. The US counts overweight from BMI 25, relaxed to 23 for Asians; China's standard counts from 24.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force is the official US body for screening recommendations. Its current advice: screen overweight or obese adults aged 35–70 for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, even with no symptoms at all. Overweight means BMI ≥25, obesity ≥30. For Asian Americans it suggests lowering the line to BMI ≥23, because at the same BMI, Asians carry higher diabetes risk. China's industry standard: BMI 24.0 to under 28.0 counts as overweight, ≥28.0 as obesity. This recommendation carries grade B. It also states: "for adults with normal blood glucose, screening every 3 years is a reasonable approach"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2021). Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes>："at a lower BMI (≥23) if the patient is Asian American"；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 居民体重管理核心知识（2024 年版）释义：「根据《成年人体重判定》（WS/T 428-2013）行业标准……24.0≤BMI＜28.0 为超重，BMI≥28.0 为肥胖」. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202407/P020240703481519509521.pdf>
- Note: A prediabetes finding doesn't mean you're headed for medication. A good share of people turn it around with lifestyle changes alone. Among Chinese adults, those who know they have diabetes have always been the minority — many find out only when a complication arrives

### 9. No speeding, no drunk driving
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free. Driving a bit slower adds minutes to a trip. The hard part is drinking less at dinners.
- In plain terms: Every 1% higher average speed raises the chance of a fatal crash by about 4%. There is no "a little is fine" line for alcohol. Risk starts climbing while blood alcohol is still very low.
- Benefit: WHO's statement: every 1% increase in average speed raises the risk of an injury-crash by 4%. Drunk-driving risk starts rising from very low blood-alcohol concentrations
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Note: The 1%/4% speed-death pairing comes from crash modeling, not randomized trials. For alcohol, WHO gives only direction, no numbers. "How much drinking raises risk by how much" — the original literature behind that was not verified for this entry.

### 10. Use a child safety seat for children under 4 — don't hold them in your arms
<!-- costs: money=high time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 300–2,000 yuan, usable for several years. 1 extra minute per trip to strap the child in.
- In plain terms: Infants under one in a safety seat are about 70% less likely to suffer a fatal injury in a crash. Children aged 1–4, about half. Holding them in your arms achieves nothing — you cannot hold on during a hard stop.
- Benefit: NHTSA, the US road-safety authority, estimates that child safety seats reduce the risk of fatal injury by 71% for infants under 1 in passenger cars, and by 54% for children aged 1–4. WHO's figure is a 71% reduction in infant deaths
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Note: Install the seat exactly per the manual — a wrongly installed seat is as bad as none. Under age 1 it must face backward, toward the rear of the car. The numbers above are estimates computed by US authorities from crash data.

### 11. If you have small children, fit window limit locks and balcony guards — a screen is not a barrier
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A set of window limiters or childproof locks costs a few dozen yuan; ten minutes to install.
- In plain terms: A set of limiters costs a few dozen yuan. New York City handed out free window guards to high-risk households with home visits to explain them, and reported child falls in one borough halved within three years. New York later simply legislated that landlords must install guards in apartments where children live. A window screen pushes open with one hand — it's not protection.
- Benefit: Starting in 1972, New York City ran a program called "Children Can't Fly," distributing free window guards in high-risk neighborhoods along with door-to-door explanation. Reported falls in the Bronx dropped 50% between 1973 and 1975. New York City subsequently amended its health code to require landlords to install window guards in apartments where children aged 10 and younger live
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Spiegel CN, Lindaman FC (1977). Children can't fly: a program to prevent childhood morbidity and mortality from window falls. American Journal of Public Health: "Significant reduction in falls resulted, particularly in the Bronx, where reported falls declined 50 percent from 1973 to 1975"，1976 年纽约市卫生法典修订 "to require that landlords provide window guards in apartments where children ten years old and younger reside". <https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.67.12.1143>；(2021). Unintentional Window Falls in Children and Adolescents. Academic Pediatrics：2007 年 1 月至 2017 年 8 月全国电子伤害监测系统 38,840 例急诊就诊，"The majority of falls occurred in children under the age of 6 and were related to falls from a second story or below". <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acap.2020.07.008>
- Note: Keep beds, sofas, and boxes — anything a child can climb — away from windows and balconies. A screen stops mosquitoes, not children; it pops out with a push, so don't treat it as a guard. And it's not just high-rise dwellers: in that US dataset, most falls were from the second story or lower.

### 12. Never take your eyes off children near water; wear life jackets when boating or swimming in the wild
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A life jacket costs 50–200 yuan. The other price is the attention spent watching the child.
- In plain terms: Wearing a life jacket when you end up in the water roughly halves your chance of drowning. Drowning is still the leading cause of death for Chinese children aged 1–14. Rural drowning mortality is about double the urban rate.
- Benefit: A study using US Coast Guard data matched similar people into pairs for comparison. Among recreational boaters who fell overboard, those wearing life jackets had roughly half the drowning-death risk. Original value adjusted RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.74. Drowning mortality among Chinese under 20 fell from 6.60 per 100,000 in 2013 to 3.28 per 100,000 in 2021. The rural rate is about twice the urban one. Drowning remains the top killer of children aged 1–14. Of injury deaths at ages 0–19 in 2021, drowning accounted for 31.1% and road traffic 27.9%
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cummings P et al. (2011). Association between wearing a personal floatation device and death by drowning among recreational boaters. Injury Prevention. <https://doi.org/10.1136/ip.2010.028688> ; Li Z et al. (2023). Unintentional Drowning Mortality Among Individuals Under Age 20 — China, 2013–2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2023.198> ; Zhou J et al. (2024). Injury Mortality of Children and Adolescents Aged 0–19 Years — China, 2010–2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2024.057>
- Note: The Grade A applies to the life-jacket half only. "Never out of sight" is consensus but on its own would be Grade C — no randomized trial, and there never will be. Drowning deaths in China cluster in rural areas, in summer, and among boys aged 15–19. For playing on ice in winter, see item 41 (measure wild ice thickness first).

### 13. If you're over 60, train balance and leg strength, and retrofit your bathroom and stairs
<!-- costs: money=low time=high will=yes benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 2–3 30-minute sessions a week of tai chi or balance training. Non-slip mats, grab bars, and night lights for the bathroom run a few dozen to a few hundred yuan. The hard part is keeping it up long-term.
- In plain terms: 2–3 balance sessions a week — tai chi, say — cut older people's falls by about 23%, and the number of people who fell by 15%. Anti-slip work and grab bars at home cut falls by another fifth or so. Falls are the leading injury-related cause of death for Chinese people over 65, and more than half of them happen inside the person's own home.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled 108 trials with 23,407 people. Exercise reduced the rate of falls in older adults by 23%. Original value rate ratio RaR 0.77, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.83. The number of people who fell dropped 15% (RR 0.85). Having a professional do a home safety assessment and modifications cut the fall rate by about a fifth (RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68 to 0.97). Tai chi specifically: fall risk down about 29% (RR 0.71). In China, falls are the top injury-related cause of death in people over 65. Of monitored falls among older people in 2018, 55.97% happened at home
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sherrington C et al. (2019). Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012424.pub2> ; Gillespie LD et al. (2012). Interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007146.pub3> ; Lu Z et al. (2021). Characteristics of Falls Among Older People — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2021.013>
- Note: This entry is for people over 60 living in their own homes, not in care institutions. The studies above count falls, not deaths. From a fall to a hip fracture to death, the road is short for an older person. Poor vision also causes falls — for cataracts affecting navigation, see Section 17, item 9 (cataract surgery). For older people pulled down while walking a dog, see Section 17, item 10 (dog walking). For whether your bones can take a fall, see item 39 in this section (bone density scans).

### 14. Get your hepatitis B panel checked; if you have no antibodies, get vaccinated
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: The test is a few dozen yuan. The adult vaccine is 3 shots totaling 100–300 yuan, completed within half a year.
- In plain terms: One test, a few dozen yuan. In the Qidong trial that ran 30 years, people vaccinated against hepatitis B at birth were 84% less likely to develop primary liver cancer later, and 72% less likely to carry the virus. Adults who test negative for antibodies get three catch-up shots over six months, one or two hundred yuan.
- Benefit: The Qidong trial randomized by community rather than individual and followed people for 30 years. Neonatal hepatitis B vaccination cut primary liver cancer by 84%, with 95% CI 23% to 97%. HBsAg positivity — the share of people testing as carrying the virus — fell 72% (95% CI 68% to 75%). Across the whole Chinese population, HBsAg positivity fell 52% from 1992 to 2014, and by 97% in children under 5
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Qu C et al. (2014). Efficacy of neonatal HBV vaccination on liver cancer and other liver diseases over 30-year follow-up of the Qidong hepatitis B intervention study. PLoS Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001774> ; Cui F et al. (2017). Prevention of Chronic Hepatitis B after 3 Decades of Escalating Vaccination Policy, China. Emerging Infectious Diseases. <https://doi.org/10.3201/eid2305.161477>
- Note: This entry is for adults born before 1992 who were never vaccinated or whose antibodies have faded. The 84% liver-cancer figure comes from people vaccinated at birth. In the same trial, those who caught up as adolescents had only 21% protection against carrying the virus. For adults, the point of vaccination is avoiding new infection — don't directly apply that 84%. If you're already infected, the vaccine does nothing; go to a hepatology clinic for regular follow-up. "Already infected" means the HBsAg line on your lab report reads positive.

### 15. Stepped on a nail or splinter, or got dirt in a wound? Deal with it the same day, and ask about a tetanus shot
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration plus wound care runs a few dozen yuan. If the doctor advises it, vaccine or immunoglobulin adds a few dozen to a few hundred.
- In plain terms: Once tetanus takes hold, roughly 1 in 10 dies. Deep wounds and dirt-contaminated wounds carry the highest risk. For a dirty wound more than 5 years past your last shot, you need 1 booster. Getting it handled the same day costs a few dozen yuan — don't wait for the wound to swell.
- Benefit: About 1 in 10 US tetanus cases is fatal. Deep wounds and wounds contaminated with soil are higher-risk. China's 2024 clinical guidelines split wounds into two tiers. A shallow wound treated within 6 hours with no contact with soil, feces, or animal saliva is low-risk. Treated more than 6 hours later, contaminated with any of those, or a puncture, avulsion, crush, burn, frostbite, or a wound with something left inside — high-risk. If you've had 3+ doses with the last within 5 years, nothing is needed. Past 5–10 years, high-risk wounds call for 1 booster. Past 10 years, 1 booster regardless of tier. If you haven't completed 3 doses or can't remember, complete the vaccine series — and for high-risk wounds also get passive immunization (immunoglobulin or antitoxin, i.e., an injection of ready-made antibodies)
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 美国疾病控制与预防中心. 破伤风："Tetanus bacteria can get into someone's body through broken skin, usually through injuries." "Tetanus can lead to death (1 in 10 cases in the United States are fatal)." "People who didn't complete the primary series or who aren't up to date with their 10-year tetanus booster shots are also at increased risk." "Vaccination also helps prevent tetanus in people with wounds, depending on their tetanus vaccination history." <https://www.cdc.gov/tetanus/about/index.html>；国家卫生健康委办公厅 (2024). 关于印发非新生儿破伤风诊疗规范（2024 年版）的通知（国卫办医急函〔2024〕381 号，风险分级见附件第 16 页，表 4 见第 20 页）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/content_6982262.htm>；附件 PDF（扫描件）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202410/P020241023483425528555.pdf>
- Note: Let the doctor at the hospital decide — don't make the call yourself, and don't skip asking out of laziness. Not every wound needs a shot. The doctor grades the wound, asks when your last shot was, then decides. "Vaccinated as a kid means covered for life" is wrong. For dirty wounds, a booster is due once 5 years have passed since your last shot. So before you go, try to recall the year you last had a tetanus shot.

### 16. Women: get the HPV vaccine, the earlier the better
<!-- costs: money=high time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: The domestic two-valent runs just over 300 yuan a dose; the imported nine-valent about 1,300. 2–3 doses in total, half a day each trip.
- In plain terms: In Swedish data on 1.67 million women, those vaccinated against HPV before 17 later developed invasive cervical cancer at about one-eighth the rate of the unvaccinated. Even vaccinated at 17–30, only about half the rate. Earlier is worth more.
- Benefit: Sweden followed 1.67 million women statistically: the vaccinated had lower invasive cervical cancer incidence than the unvaccinated. Vaccinated before 17: about 88% lower (IRR 0.12, 95% CI 0.00 to 0.34 — the parentheses give the original value and its confidence interval). Vaccinated at 17–30: about 53% lower (IRR 0.47, 95% CI 0.27 to 0.75)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lei J et al. (2020). HPV Vaccination and the Risk of Invasive Cervical Cancer. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1917338>
- Note: Ages 9–14 get the most benefit. At 17–30, more than half the reduction remains. After 30 the benefit shrinks steadily. Even vaccinated, you still need cervical cancer screening — the vaccine doesn't replace it; see item 18 (cervical cancer screening for women over 30).

### 17. Women: start breast cancer screening at 40, with a mammogram every two years
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A mammogram runs 200–300 yuan each time; many regions have free screening programs. Half a day every two years.
- In plain terms: A mammogram every two years for women aged 40–74 is what the official US screening recommendation explicitly endorses. For those over 75, and for adding ultrasound or MRI for dense breasts, the official verdict is that evidence is still insufficient. When a checkup package charges extra for those, you can skip them.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force is the official US body for screening recommendations. Its current advice: "recommends biennial screening mammography for women aged 40 to 74 years." Screening mammography is the ordinary mammogram. This recommendation carries grade B. For women 75 and older, and for supplementing ultrasound or MRI for dense breasts, it ruled the evidence insufficient
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2024). Breast Cancer: Screening. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening>
- Note: Starting ages differ by country; some Chinese guidelines and local programs start at 45. Those whose parents, children, or siblings had breast cancer, and BRCA mutation carriers, are high-risk and need an individual plan from a doctor — this entry doesn't apply to them

### 18. Women over 30: cervical cancer screening, HPV testing first
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: An HPV test runs 100–300 yuan. With a negative result, once every 5 years is enough. Sampling takes minutes.
- In plain terms: In that rural-India trial, women screened for HPV just once in their lives were about half as likely to die of cervical cancer, and about half as likely to be found with late-stage cervical cancer. One or two hundred yuan, one visit, and a negative result holds for 5 years.
- Benefit: The rural-India trial randomized by community, not individual, covering women aged 30–59. A single round of HPV screening cut the risk of dying of cervical cancer by about 48%. Original value HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.83. The risk of being found with advanced cervical cancer fell about 53% (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.69)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sankaranarayanan R et al. (2009). HPV screening for cervical cancer in rural India. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0808516>
- Note: People who've had the HPV vaccine still need screening, because the vaccine doesn't cover every viral type. What makes this study rare: it was a randomized screening trial that used deaths as the outcome, and it screened only one round.

### 19. From 45–50, screen for colorectal cancer: a fecal immunochemical test, or a colonoscopy
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: The stool test costs a few dozen yuan every 1–2 years. A colonoscopy runs several hundred to over a thousand; with a clean result, once per 10 years. Budget a day for bowel prep beforehand.
- In plain terms: A few dozen yuan for a stool occult-blood test every year or two cuts your chance of dying of colorectal cancer by about 16%; those who complete at least one round, by 25%. People invited for one colonoscopy saw their 10-year colorectal cancer rate go from 1.20% to 0.98%.
- Benefit: Cochrane pooled multiple studies. Fecal occult-blood screening cut colorectal cancer death risk by about 16%. Original value RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.90. Those completing at least one round: about 25% lower (RR 0.75). Then the NordICC randomized trial: those invited for colonoscopy saw 10-year colorectal cancer incidence fall from 1.20% to 0.98%. Original value RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.93
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Hewitson P et al. (2007). Screening for colorectal cancer using the faecal occult blood test, Hemoccult. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD001216.pub2> ; Bretthauer M et al. (2022). Effect of Colonoscopy Screening on Risks of Colorectal Cancer and Related Death. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2208375>
- Note: Contested. NordICC counts "invited to colonoscopy": colorectal cancer deaths 0.28% vs 0.31% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.64 to 1.16), a difference still within the margin of error. That suggests colonoscopy's mortality benefit was previously overestimated. The stool test actually has harder evidence on mortality. Starting ages vary by guideline between 45 and 50. Anyone with an affected close relative should start earlier.

### 20. People with cardiovascular disease and older adults: a flu shot every year
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 50–150 yuan a dose, once each autumn.
- In plain terms: In the post-heart-attack flu vaccine trial, the one-year death rate fell from 4.9% to 2.9%. People with cardiovascular disease and older adults: one shot each autumn, 50–150 yuan.
- Benefit: A trial after myocardial infarction randomized 2,571 people; neither doctors nor patients knew who got which injection. Flu vaccine: 2.9% all-cause mortality over 12 months. Placebo: 4.9%. Original value HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.89. A placebo is a comparison injection with no active ingredient. Cardiovascular death HR 0.59. A separate pooled analysis: major cardiovascular events 3.6% with flu vaccine vs 5.4% without. Original value RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.83. Cardiovascular death RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.42 to 1.30 — still within the margin of error, so not statistically significant)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Fröbert O et al. (2021). Influenza Vaccination After Myocardial Infarction: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Multicenter Trial. ### 21. Get the shingles vaccine after 50
<!-- costs: money=high time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: The recombinant shingles vaccine is two doses, about 3,000–4,000 yuan in total. Entirely out of pocket.
- In plain terms: In phase-3 trials the vaccine blocked 97% of shingles, about evenly across age groups. The catch: two doses at 3,000–4,000 yuan, all self-paid. What that money buys is a 97% reduction in incidence.
- Benefit: A phase-3 trial across 18 countries enrolled 15,400 people, randomized to vaccine or a control injection, followed for 3.2 years on average. Result: 6 shingles cases in the vaccine group, 210 in placebo. That converts to 0.3 vs 9.1 per 1,000 person-years — per 1,000 person-years meaning the cases arising among 1,000 people observed for one year. The paper states "the overall vaccine efficacy against herpes zoster was 97.2% (95% CI 93.7–99.0, P<0.001)." Efficacy by age group ran from 96.6% to 97.9%
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Lal H, Cunningham AL, Godeaux O, et al. (2015). Efficacy of an adjuvanted herpes zoster subunit vaccine in older adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(22), 2087-2096. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1501184>
- Note: It ranks low on value for one reason: price. Shingles itself is rarely deadly — the trouble is post-herpetic neuralgia, which can hurt for months to years. After the shot, injection-site pain and fever are both common; the severest tier (grade-3 symptoms) was 17.0% vs 3.2%

### 22. Get the pneumococcal vaccine after 65
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A few hundred yuan; free for older adults in some regions.
- In plain terms: In a trial of 84,500 older adults, the 13-valent pneumococcal vaccine blocked about 46% of pneumonia caused by the types it covers, and 75% of the most dangerous invasive infections. But it showed no effect on "pneumonia from all causes" — don't expect it to cover all pneumonia.
- Benefit: The Netherlands randomized 84,500 people over 65 to compare, using the 13-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine. Efficacy against vaccine-type pneumonia: 45.6%, with 95.2% CI 21.8–62.5. "Vaccine-type" means the bacterial strains this vaccine covers. Against invasive pneumococcal disease — the most dangerous class of infection — efficacy was 75.0% (95% CI 41.4–90.8). Against pneumonia from all causes combined, it showed no effect

- Sources: Bonten MJ, Huijts SM, Bolkenbaas M, et al. (2015). Polysaccharide conjugate vaccine against pneumococcal pneumonia in adults. New England Journal of Medicine, 372(12), 1114-1125. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1408544>
- Note: It only protects against covered serotypes — being vaccinated doesn't mean never getting pneumonia. What Chinese older adults commonly get is the 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine, not the 13-valent conjugate used in this trial; the efficacy numbers above don't transfer directly

### 23. Test for Helicobacter pylori; if positive, eradicate it
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A carbon-13 or carbon-14 breath test costs 100–200 yuan. Eradication means four drugs at once; two weeks of medication runs 200–500 yuan. The hard part is taking all of them on schedule for those two weeks.
- In plain terms: In the Shandong Linqu trial that ran 22 years, people who eradicated H. pylori were about half as likely to later develop stomach cancer, and about 40% less likely to die of it. One test at one or two hundred yuan; eradication is two weeks of pills.
- Benefit: The randomized Linqu trial in Shandong followed people for 22 years. Those who received H. pylori eradication therapy had about half the risk of developing stomach cancer. Original value OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.71. Risk of dying of stomach cancer fell about 38% (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.99)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Li WQ et al. (2019). Effects of Helicobacter pylori treatment and vitamin and garlic supplementation on gastric cancer incidence and mortality: follow-up of a randomized intervention trial. BMJ. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l5016>
- Note: Reinfection after eradication is possible, so have the whole household tested and treated together. The trial population lived in a high-stomach-cancer region; people in low-incidence areas gain much less in practice.

### 24. Heavy smokers: a low-dose chest CT once a year
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=no benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: 200–400 yuan per scan, 10 minutes. Add the follow-ups after a scare, and the anxiety of those weeks.
- In plain terms: Eligible heavy smokers getting an annual low-dose CT are 20% less likely to die of lung cancer than with chest X-rays alone, and 6.7% less likely to die overall. The price is follow-up testing after suspicious findings, and the worry in between.
- Benefit: The US NLST randomized trial enrolled 53,454 people, all aged 55–74 with a 30-pack-year smoking history who quit no more than 15 years ago. A pack-year is the unit for smoking amount: one pack a day for a year is one pack-year. The results: those screened with low-dose CT had a relative 20.0% drop in lung-cancer death compared with chest X-ray. 95% CI 6.8 to 26.7. All-cause mortality fell a relative 6.7%, 95% CI 1.2 to 13.6
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Lung Screening Trial Research Team (2011). Reduced Lung-Cancer Mortality with Low-Dose Computed Tomographic Screening. New England Journal of Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1102873>
- Note: This applies only to high-risk smokers. In the same trial, 24.2% of low-dose CT participants had suspicious findings, and of those, 96.4% were false alarms. For non-smokers, this scan is buying anxiety.

### 27. Blood visible in your urine — even if painless, even if it clears by tomorrow — get checked once
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: A urinalysis is a few dozen yuan, a urinary ultrasound one to two hundred. If the doctor deems it necessary, add a cystoscopy, around a thousand.
- In plain terms: In people over 60 with one episode of visible blood in the urine, the chance it turns out to be bladder cancer is about 2.8%; at 40–59, about 1.2%. Not a high number — but high enough that you must get checked once. Even if the bleeding stops the next day.
- Benefit: A UK study used electronic records from community general-practice (primary care): 4,915 bladder cancer cases against 21,718 controls. Among those presenting with visible haematuria, the share ultimately diagnosed with bladder cancer: 2.8% at over 60 (95% CI 2.5 to 3.1); 1.2% at 40–59 (0.6 to 2.3). At over 60 that share is 1.6%, CI 1.2 to 2.1

### 28. When erections fail, check your heart first — it isn't just "a bedroom matter"
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=no benefit=medium metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Registration is a few dozen yuan. Blood pressure plus glucose and lipid bloodwork, one to two hundred. One hospital trip, results in a few days.
- In plain terms: Men with erectile dysfunction later develop cardiovascular disease about 40% more often than others, and heart attacks about 60% more. The younger you are, the bigger the gap. - Benefit: A meta-analysis pooled 14 cohort studies, 92,757 men, average follow-up 6.1 years. Compared with men without erectile dysfunction, those with it had these later risks: overall cardiovascular events about 44% higher (RR 1.44, 95% CI 1.27 to 1.63 — the confidence interval). Cardiovascular death didn't separate significantly (RR 1.19, 0.97 to 1.46). The younger the age, the higher the multiplier. Men at intermediate baseline cardiovascular risk showed higher multipliers than both high- and low-risk men. Studies using questionnaires computed a multiplier of 1.61 (1.38 to 1.86); single-question studies, 1.27 (1.18 to 1.37)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Vlachopoulos CV, Terentes-Printzios DG, Ioakeimidis NK, Aznaouridis KA, Stefanadis CI (2013). - Note: All 14 are cohort studies — following groups and observing outcomes — not randomized trials. So "40% more" is an association, not proven causation. ### 30. Use a condom every time, start to finish; never share needles
<!-- costs: money=low time=low will=some benefit=large metric=mortality -->
- Cost: One or two yuan each. The hard part isn't the money — it's using one every single time, and putting it on from the start.

### 31. After any high-risk episode, get one HIV test — free at the CDC, results confidential

### 32. The moment a suicidal thought appears, tell one person around you — hand over those dozens of minutes

- In plain terms: Of people who attempted suicide, nearly half say no more than 10 minutes passed between the thought appearing and the act — so what you need to survive is often just those dozens of minutes. Among those hospitalized for self-harm, about 2% die of suicide within 1 year, about 7% over 9-plus years — the overwhelming majority never die of suicide.

### 36. Don't pick up, dismantle, or sell metal objects of unknown origin; keep away from anything marked with the trefoil warning

### 43. Ask the person whose mood has changed, directly: "Have you thought about suicide?" Asking doesn't plant the idea
