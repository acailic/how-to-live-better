# 23. What skills are worth learning

This section counts money and time — item 2 adds mortality. The first six entries answer one question: keep studying or not? Item 1 states which options the law removes; item 2 counts education against lifespan; item 3 shows where the nation's education levels sit. Item 4 covers funding when unaffordable; item 5 the paths when regular high school is missed; item 6 turns "study or work" into a self-computed problem. The next seven answer: once you decide to learn, how to choose. China's occupation-by-occupation wage statistics resist verification from public official channels, so only sourced items appear: what an extra year of schooling adds to wages (the returns to education), how to verify certificates, where training subsidies come from, and which skill dimensions resist replacement. Specific monthly wages by trade: no numbers here. Items 14–19 answer the third question: how to learn with the least time — counted in time, never converted to money. Items 20–23 answer the fourth: with professional-technical work underway — where to file professional titles, how to sit them, which traps, what they're worth.

Entries in this section are grouped by theme, with item numbers in parentheses.

**Keep studying or not**: under 16 means no "go work" option (item 1), education counted in mortality (item 2), the national education structure (item 3), unaffordable means computing by policy first (item 4), paths after missing regular high school (item 5), study-versus-work as one problem (item 6).

**What to learn**: the returns to another year (item 7), check the occupational-qualification catalog before certificates (item 8), training through subsidized channels (item 9), pick hands-on, on-site-judgment skills (item 10), check whether the skill has graded evaluation (item 11), check local shortage lists (item 12), choose job-ready short programs (item 13).

**Learning efficiently**: self-test after closing the book (item 14), spread practice across days (item 15), don't make highlighting the main method (item 16), interleave problem types (item 17), don't pick methods by learning styles (item 18), self-quiz what must be memorized (item 19).

**Professional titles**: find the filing channels (item 20), accounting junior-intermediate titles by national exam (item 21), no agency-filed titles (item 22), a title doesn't equal a raise (item 23).

### 1. Under 16, "going to work" isn't an option: the hiring unit pays 5,000 yuan monthly per child laborer — anyone willing is operating black
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=freedom -->
- Cost: Free. One minute checking your age.
- In plain terms: Junior-high graduates run 15–16, straddling the line. Under 16, hiring you is illegal — 5,000 yuan per child laborer per month. Whoever takes you knowingly breaks the law; when things go wrong, medical bills and compensation have only that unit to answer, and if it stonewalls or runs, no one stands behind it.
- Benefit: Compulsory Education Law Article 11: "children who have reached six shall be enrolled by parents or guardians to accept and complete compulsory education." Article 14: "employers are prohibited from hiring school-age children and adolescents subject to compulsory education." Article 58: failure without justified reason to enroll draws local-government criticism and correction orders. The Prohibition of Child Labor provisions, Article 2: state organs, social organizations, enterprises, institutions, private non-enterprises, and individual businesses "may not hire minors under 16." Article 6: fines "at 5,000 yuan per child laborer per month," doubling to 10,000 on uncorrected continuation; Article 7: introducers fined 5,000 per child placed; Article 10: sick or injured child laborers get employer-funded treatment with full medical and living costs, plus lump-sum compensation for disability or death; Article 13's sole exception: arts and sports units may, with guardian consent, hire professional performers and athletes under 16 (national)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2018). 义务教育法（2018 年第二次修正，第十一、十四、五十八条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>（教育部刊登）；国务院 (2002). 禁止使用童工规定（国务院令第 364 号，第二、六、七、十、十三条）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>
- Note: The beneficiary is yourself. Past 16, working is lawful — but the posts open to a 16-year-old without credentials are exactly item 10's most replaceable: fixed procedures, machine-vulnerable. Section 19's overtime, leave, and injury benefits presuppose employment relationships with evidence; black work lacks both, taking the money with it.

### 2. Count "is schooling useful" in the mortality account too: each added year of education lowers adult death risk about 1.9%
<!-- costs: money=none time=high will=yes benefit=small metric=mortality -->
- Cost: Free — the cost is the years themselves; tuition is item 4's business. The hard part: reading through without dropping out.
- In plain terms: Each additional year of schooling runs about 1.9% lower adult mortality — strongest at 18–49 (about 2.9% per year), 0.8% past 70. Those who keep reading usually started advantaged; how much is schooling itself, the design can't split.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis (literature January 1980–May 2023; 603 studies, 59 countries, 70 sites, 10,355 observations) found a dose-response between education and all-cause adult mortality: each year averages 1.9% lower risk (95% UI 1.8–2.0); ages 18–49, 2.9% (2.8–3.0); past 70, 0.8% (0.6–1.0). No sex difference, none by country development level
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024). Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet Public Health, 9(3), e155-e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>
- Note: Contested: the authors themselves report publication bias (p<0.0001) and extreme between-study spread. All observational, no assignment; years of schooling tangle with family means, region, and occupation — the 1.9% can't isolate schooling from "those who kept reading started ahead." The reverse path tangles too: childhood illness and psychological distress drive both dropout and early death, inflating schooling's apparent effect. So it strongly establishes co-direction, not "attending buys 1.9% longevity." Benefit "small": readers face 3–4 more years, compounding under a tenth by this book's threshold. Beneficiary: yourself.

### 3. Before judging whether credentials "devalued": the national structure — only 15,467 per 100,000 hold college education (2020 census; 2025 sampling projects about 19%)
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One glance at the numbers.
- In plain terms: At the 2020 census, about 15% of the population held associate degrees or above; 2025 sampling projects about 19% — a few points in five years. Junior-high remains the modal attainment both times. The over-15 average ran 9.91 years of schooling — barely past junior high. So junior-high in China isn't "sufficient" — it's the most ordinary tier.
- Benefit: The Seventh Census (reference moment 2020-11-01): 218,360,767 with college education or above; 213,005,258 senior high (including secondary vocational); 487,163,489 junior high; 349,658,828 primary. Versus the 2010 Sixth Census, per 100,000: college rose from 8,930 to 15,467; senior high from 14,032 to 15,088; junior high fell from 38,788 to 34,507; primary from 26,779 to 24,767. Average schooling of the over-15 rose from 9.08 to 9.91 years; illiteracy from 4.08% to 2.67%. The 2025 1% sample (reference moment 2025-11-01, 20.52 million sampled) projects 1,405.45 million total: 272.33 million college-plus, 242.72 million senior high, 452.83 million junior high, 331.22 million primary — the bulletin omitting per-100,000 figures; computing them, college runs about 19.4% and junior high about 32.2%, still the modal tier
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国家统计局、国务院第七次全国人口普查领导小组办公室 (2021). 第七次全国人口普查公报（第六号）——人口受教育情况. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>；国家统计局 (2026). 2025 年全国 1% 人口抽样调查主要数据公报. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202605/t20260522_1963788.html>
- Note: These numbers speak only to scarcity in the population; individual worth gets computed by item 6's method. Note the census counts everyone including children and elders — the young run far more college-heavy, so comparing against all ages overstates your edge. Conversely, using it to rebut "schooling is useless" holds — the denominator is the entire competing workforce. The 2025 figures are sample projections against the census's enumeration; the two run on different frames, so they're listed side by side, the new never replacing the old; 19.4% and 32.2% computed from the bulletin's own counts. Beneficiary: yourself.

### 4. "Can't afford it" — compute by policy first: secondary-vocational tuition mostly waived, stipends 2,300 yuan, student loans to 20,000 yearly
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One visit with documents to the school's student-aid office before term.
- In plain terms: Public secondary-vocational schools waive tuition for rural students, urban agriculture-track students, and hardship cases; vocational and ordinary-senior-high stipends run 2,300 yuan yearly from spring 2025; college stipends 3,700, student loans to 20,000 yearly. Money shortfalls can defer or reroute — rarely must one quit for work now.
- Benefit: The tuition waiver has run since fall 2012: "all rural (including county-town) students, urban agriculture-track students, and financially disadvantaged students among full-time registered grades 1–3 of public secondary vocational schools exempt from tuition (arts-performance majors excepted)." The 2024 adjustments: college national stipends from 3,300 to 3,700 yuan; the encouragement scholarship from 5,000 to 6,000 (both from fall 2024, the scholarship from 2024); secondary-vocational and senior-high stipends from 2,000 to 2,300 (spring 2025); national student loans — undergraduates "from no more than 16,000 to no more than 20,000 yuan per person per year," graduate students "from 20,000 to 25,000," both from fall 2024, at LPR minus 70 basis points (national)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 财政部、发展改革委、教育部、人力资源社会保障部 (2012). 关于扩大中等职业教育免学费政策范围 进一步完善国家助学金制度的意见（财教〔2012〕376 号）. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>；财政部、教育部、人力资源社会保障部有关司局负责人 (2024). 就调整学生资助政策有关问题答记者问. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>；财政部、教育部、人民银行、金融监管总局 (2024). 关于调整完善国家助学贷款有关政策的通知（财教〔2024〕188 号）. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>
- Note: Beneficiary: you and your children. Waiver coverage and stipend tiers are provincial; private schools subsidize at the local public rate with the gap yours — verify against local documents before enrolling. Student loans are loans, not grants — repaid after graduation — but they split "can't pay now" from "not worth it." Section 7 covered unemployment-period training subsidies and temporary assistance; with income truly cut, apply there first.

### 5. Missing regular high school isn't a dead end: secondary vocational runs through-programs and separate exams, and skill-post recruitment may lower credential bars
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check of the provincial vocational-enrollment platform before exam volunteering.
- In plain terms: The 2022 Vocational Education Law states vocational and general education hold equal standing. Secondary vocational schools run through-programs with higher vocational; higher vocational must reserve plans or separate exams for vocational graduates; employers may not bar vocational graduates. Missing the regular-high cutoff means switching roads, not stopping at junior high.
- Benefit: The 2022 law: "vocational education is a type of education of equal importance with general education" (Article 3); secondary vocational schools "may, per state provisions, implement through enrollment and training with higher vocational education in relevant majors," and higher vocational "may enroll by combined academic-quality and vocational-skill assessment," with provincial-plus education departments running unified enrollment platforms (Article 37). Article 53: vocational-school students "enjoy equal opportunity in further study, employment, and career development"; higher vocational and vocational-education universities "shall set aside corresponding proportions or separate examinations to enroll vocational-school graduates exclusively," and "employers may not set application, hiring, or engagement conditions impeding equal employment and fair competition for vocational-school graduates," with public-institution posts carrying skill-grade requirements allowed to "appropriately lower academic requirements" (national)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 全国人大常委会 (2022). 职业教育法（2022 年修订，第三、三十七、五十三条）. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202204/t20220421_620064.html>（教育部刊登）
- Note: Beneficiary: you and your children. These are statutory channels, not admission guarantees — through-program majors, quota shares, and vocational-exam subjects are provincial; check the current platform and plans, never transplant another province's experience. Run major choices through item 10's dimensions: on-site, non-standard environments resist replacement. School legitimacy: presence in the current provincial plan; certificates verified by item 8's three steps. Any "guaranteed placement" or "guaranteed pass" promise: refuse.

### 6. Study-versus-work as one problem: three years of early wages against decades of annual income difference
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Half an hour of paper arithmetic.
- In plain terms: Lay both options side by side. Early work earns three years of low-threshold wages once; continued study buys the yearly increment across the remaining 30–40 years. Urban China's return per schooling year ran 4.0% (1988) to 10.2% (2001) — dated, magnitude only.
- Benefit: Estimates for urban China 1988–2001 — the returns to schooling: "rising sharply from 4.0% per year of schooling in 1988 to 10.2% in 2001," the rise mostly post-1992, tracking the growing higher-education wage premium. This is the usable domestic magnitude for converting "one more year" into annual income difference; the global average sits in item 7
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhang J, Zhao Y, Park A, Song X (2005). Economic returns to schooling in urban China, 1988 to 2001. Journal of Comparative Economics, 33(4), 730-752. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jce.2005.05.008>
- Note: Before computing, deduct item 4's waivers and stipends and add item 2's mortality account. Data end at 2001, urban-only — never today's numbers directly; item 7's global 9% likewise. Results vary by person: expensive tuition, mismatched majors, and long programs can genuinely fail the arithmetic — a verdict on that program, not on schooling. Several items stay outside the formula: statutory credential bars (item 5), replaceability (item 10), and industry mobility. Beneficiary: yourself.

### 7. Memorize the baseline first: one more year of education, global average private return, about 9% a year
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Memorize the number as the yardstick for every "learn something" scheme.
- In plain terms: Globally averaged, one more schooling year raises personal income about 9% yearly, women's returns consistently above men's. This speaks to formal schooling, not directly to any course. Use it as a ruler: a course demanding 30,000 yuan and half a year must answer whether its income change matches another year of school.
- Benefit: A review spanning 1950–2014, 139 countries, 1,120 estimates: "the global average private return to one additional year of schooling is 9% per year" — private meaning the part landing on one's own income. Higher-education private returns are rising; social returns remain high; women's average returns persistently exceed men's
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Psacharopoulos G, Patrinos HA (2018). Returns to investment in education: a decennial review of the global literature. Education Economics, 26(5), 445-458. <https://doi.org/10.1080/09645292.2018.1484426>；世界银行政策研究工作论文 8402. <https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-8402>
- Note: This is the global average for "one more year of school" — not a course's yield, not China's specific figure, a magnitude reference only. Its use is the ruler: any course demanding 30,000 yuan and half a year must answer for a year-of-schooling-equivalent income change. Averages also hide vast field-level and country-level differences.

### 8. Before paying for a certificate: check it against the national occupational-qualification catalog and the filed evaluation-agency list
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check, a dozen-odd minutes.
- In plain terms: Some outfits run disorderly training and fee collection under "new occupation" and "skill grade" banners, some impersonating authorities — the HR ministry has run dedicated cleanup. Three steps before enrolling: is the occupation in the catalog, is the issuer a filed evaluation agency, does the certificate number verify on the official platform? Unverifiable means the fee is lost.
- Benefit: The ministry ran a national cleanup naming the disorders: "some institutions and units arbitrarily hold training, evaluation, and certification under new-occupation qualification and skill-grade names, charging disorderly fees and issuing certificates indiscriminately; some conduct false or exaggerated publicity, even impersonating authoritative institutions." Measures: joint enforcement with cyberspace, public security, and market regulation, "severely striking counterfeit appraisal institutions and evaluation-certificate issuance in the name of HR-filed agencies"; noncompliant promotion shut down; the public guided to "know certificate-verification channels and judge legality and value"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 人力资源社会保障部 (2022). 关于开展技术技能类「山寨证书」专项治理工作的通知. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-03/18/content_5679641.htm>
- Note: Verify in order: occupation in the National Occupational Qualification Catalog (absent means no national qualification); issuer among HR-filed evaluation agencies; certificate number findable on the official skill-talent evaluation platform. Unfindable means don't enroll. "International," "senior," "registered" branding with "guaranteed pass" or "passive rental income" promises carries the highest risk.

### 9. Training through subsidized government channels first — never straight to commercial classes
<!-- costs: money=none time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free or heavily reduced; the training itself costs time. The hard part: checking the subsidy catalog before impulse-enrolling.
- In plain terms: 2025 through end-2027, the state plans 30-million-plus subsidized training seats. Money flows from employment-assistance funds, unemployment insurance, and enterprise education budgets. The scarcer the trade and costlier the training, the higher the subsidy. The employed ask their employer first; everyone else reads the local HR bureau's subsidy catalog before choosing.
- Benefit: The national scale is explicit: 2025 through 2027, "subsidized training of over 30 million person-times focusing on high-end industries, urgent sectors, and key employment groups." Funding pools employment-assistance funds, unemployment insurance, industry-development funds, east-west cooperation funds, and enterprise education budgets. Subsidy levels follow occupational urgency and training cost by trade. Enterprises must "fully extract and use staff education funds per regulation, ensuring 60%-plus goes to frontline staff education and training"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Note: Claiming and amounts are provincial; the entry points are the local HR bureau's training-subsidy catalog and public training bases. Section 7 covered unemployment-period training subsidies (3 claims per person) and social-insurance subsidies; the employed start with the employer's education budget. Choosing from the subsidy catalog beats choosing first and hunting subsidies after.

### 10. When picking skills, weigh "does it need hands, does it need on-site judgment" — the hardest for automation to displace
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=large metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. Run candidate trades through this dimension first.
- In plain terms: US estimates put about 47% of jobs at high computerization risk — later task-level estimates ran far lower. The machines' barriers are three: manual dexterity, generating novelty, and dealing with people. On-site, non-standard, judgment-heavy work resists; fixed-procedure information work runs highest risk.
- Benefit: Rating 702 US occupations by "probability of computerization" put about 47% of employment in the high-risk band. The model's drivers are three "engineering bottlenecks" machines hadn't crossed: perception and manipulation — finger and manual dexterity, working in cramped awkward spaces; creative intelligence; and social intelligence. Non-standard environments demanding hand skill and live judgment run consistently lower replacement probability — structural, not incidental. Fixed-procedure, information-processing posts run highest
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Frey CB, Osborne MA (2017). The future of employment: how susceptible are jobs to computerisation? Technological Forecasting and Social Change, 114, 254-280. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019>
- Note: Contested: this began as a 2013 unpublished working paper rating whole occupations; later task-level work found far lower high-risk shares, and it never dates the transition. Use it for the three bottlenecks, not the 47%. On-site installation, maintenance, and care resist replacement over pure paperwork for exactly this reason — "learning a skill" should favor the on-site, non-standard kind.

### 11. Before learning, confirm the skill has graded evaluation — and whether grades convert to money
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Free. One check.
- In plain terms: The state is pushing the "new eight-grade" worker system, asking enterprises to tie post wages to skill grades, with outstanding workers grade-certified regardless of seniority. It's policy direction with vast local variation. Skills carrying national standards and grade ladders can plausibly rise by grade — check before learning.
- Benefit: The state is advancing the "new eight-grade" vocational-skill system. The documents explicitly tie grades to pay: "strengthen skill-oriented compensation distribution... guiding enterprises to implement wage allocation doubly linked to post value and skill grade." Localities are to "increase evaluation and appointment intensity and batch counts above senior-worker." Qualifying enterprises may evaluate their own staff, and outstanding workers "may be directly certified at the corresponding vocational-skill grade, breaking conditions of working years, grade, and seniority"
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>；人力资源社会保障部 (2021). 「技能中国行动」实施方案. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/06/content_5622619.htm>
- Note: Policy direction, not realized wage numbers — local and enterprise implementation varies widely. Skills with neither grades nor entry barriers leave you hunting work alone after learning.

### 12. Don't take "the internet says it pays well" for shortage — check the local shortage-occupation and subsidy catalogs

- Benefit: Subsidy levels themselves are the official pricing of "which trades are short." The 2025 figures: above-scale enterprises averaged 106,080 yuan yearly (exactly), mid-and-above managers 210,016, professional-technical 155,491, clerical 94,936, production 80,739, social and life services 79,857. ### 13. Same money and time: prefer job-ready short-cycle programs
<!-- costs: money=low time=medium will=some benefit=medium metric=money -->
- Cost: Modest course fees plus a stretch of time. The hard part: honoring the exit condition when halfway through shows no results.
- In plain terms: Policy favors short-quick employment training and vocational micro-majors. One criterion: does a concrete post await your résumé at the end? Programs demanding a year plus "further experience accumulation" cost far beyond the sticker. Write the exit condition before starting: stop at a defined point of no work found.
- Benefit: Policy aligns: supporting "private training institutions' market-oriented high-quality vocational programs and 'short-quick' programs enabling short-term employment," and vocational colleges "focusing on urgently needed knowledge and skills... opening micro-majors and vocational-competence courses"
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: 国务院就业促进和劳动保护工作领导小组 (2025). 关于开展大规模职业技能提升培训行动的指导意见. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202507/content_7032222.htm>
- Note: The single criterion: a concrete post to apply for at completion. Programs demanding a year plus post-course "experience accumulation" cost far beyond sticker price. Section 4's exit conditions apply: write "stop at X progress with no work found" before starting.

### 14. After studying: close the book and test yourself — don't reread
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=time -->
- Cost: Free, no added time — the rereading time becomes recall-and-question time. The hard part: self-testing exposes what you didn't retain, and feels worse than rereading.
- In plain terms: Rereading feels familiar but doesn't hold. Closing the book and self-testing works clearly better; classroom quizzes lift grades by a medium effect. Among ten common methods ranked, self-testing sits top-tier. After each section, recall the points from memory, then check back.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 222 studies, 48,478 students: classroom quizzing lifts achievement at a medium effect (g = 0.499), moderated by the control's method, quiz-exam format match, materials, corrective feedback, repetitions, timing, duration, and design. The ten-method review rates "practice testing" and "distributed practice" top-tier (high utility) — effective across ages and abilities, validated across assessments and classrooms
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yang C, Luo L, Vadillo MA, Yu R, Shanks DR (2021). Testing (quizzing) boosts classroom learning: A systematic and meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 147(4), 399–435. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000309>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Note: Self-testing forces retrieval, not grading. Practice: recall section points from memory, then check. Corrective feedback amplifies — read the errors. Benefit "large" by effect size and rating; both sources measure grades, not hours, so no time-threshold conversion. Pairs with item 15, the review's other top-tier method.

### 15. Spread the same time across days — never one sitting
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=large metric=time -->
- Cost: Free, total time unchanged — 3 hours become 1 hour over 3 days. The hard part: starting days early rather than the final night.
- In plain terms: The same hours spread over days retain better than crammed — 317 experiments agree. One practical rule: the retention horizon sets the interval — testing tomorrow means reviewing today; a month out means spacing days between passes.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 184 articles, 317 experiments, 839 assessments compares massed versus spaced study (the spacing effect) and short versus long gaps (the lag effect): retention peaks when the interval matches the retention horizon — the longer the horizon, the longer the optimal gap. The ten-method review rates distributed practice top-tier (high utility)
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cepeda NJ, Pashler H, Vul E, Wixted JT, Rohrer D (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 132(3), 354–380. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354>；Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Note: Best stacked with item 14: distribute the self-tests themselves. Cepeda's materials lean toward word-list recall — complex content gets no universal day-count; "farther test, longer gap" is the review's rule, no calendar. Final-night cramming works on the spot and evaporates after.

### 16. Don't make highlighting, rereading, or summarizing your main methods
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free — it saves time. The hard part: these feel easiest and most "studious."
- In plain terms: Students most use highlighting and rereading — both bottom-tier among ten methods. Summarizing, keyword mnemonics, and imagery-for-text join them. Not useless, but their working conditions run narrow; the same time buys more as self-testing.
- Benefit: The ten-method review grades by generalizability across conditions, students, materials, and assessments. Top-tier: practice testing and distributed practice. Middle: elaborative interrogation, self-explanation, interleaved practice. Bottom five: summarization, highlighting (underlining), keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, rereading — "most students report rereading and highlighting, which do not reliably improve performance and should be replaced, e.g., practice testing in place of rereading"
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques: Promising Directions From Cognitive and Educational Psychology. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 14(1), 4–58. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
- Note: Highlighting may mark "where I'll self-test later" — the failure is treating the marking itself as learning. Bottom-tier isn't zero, the review notes — narrow conditions, more research needed. Replacements: items 14 and 15.

### 17. Interleave problem types — never twenty of one kind in a row
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free — reorder the workbook by type into shuffled order. The hard part: interleaving drops in-the-moment accuracy, feeling like regression.
- In plain terms: Massed same-type blocks feel smooth but blur later discrimination of which method applies. Shuffled practice holds better long-term at a medium effect — but it picks materials: strongest for visual categorization, weaker for mathematics, and word learning actually favors blocking.
- Benefit: A multilevel meta-analysis of 59 studies, 238 effect sizes, 158 samples: interleaved over blocked practice runs a medium overall effect (g = 0.42). By material: painting best (g = 0.67), other visual materials strong; mathematics smaller (g = 0.34); expository texts and taste materials nonsignificant; vocabulary reverses toward blocking (g = −0.39). Regression: the more similar the categories, the less similar within-category items, and the more complex the materials, the bigger interleaving's advantage
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Brunmair M, Richter T (2019). Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators. Psychological Bulletin, 145(11), 1029–1052. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209>
- Note: Item 16's review graded interleaving middle-tier on thinner evidence then. Combined reading: interleave what requires "recognize the type, pick the method" — math problem types, cases, statute application; block what needs raw memorization — vocabulary, term definitions.

### 18. Don't pick study methods by "I'm visual, he's auditory"
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=no benefit=small metric=time -->
- Cost: Free — stop selecting materials and courses by the typology, and stop paying for style assessments.
- In plain terms: Many believe everyone has a learning style — visual, auditory — and matching teaching to it teaches better. Rigorous tests are few, almost none support it, and several run directly contrary. Preferences exist; evidence that preference-matched teaching learns better doesn't.
- Benefit: A commissioned review: proving "teach to the style" requires grouping students by style, randomizing each group across methods, and testing identically — the valid signature being a crossover interaction where one style's best method isn't another's. "Almost no" such interactions were found; the literature is vast, properly designed studies few, several contradicting the matching hypothesis outright — insufficient evidence for style assessment in education
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Pashler H, McDaniel M, Rohrer D, Bjork R (2008). Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence. Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9(3), 105–119. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x>
- Note: The review also notes most style theories were never tested at all, let alone refuted. Avoid paying for style assessments and style-matched courses. Selection criteria: items 9 and 13.

### 19. For memorized content, quiz yourself in the form it will be used — never mere verbatim recitation
<!-- costs: money=none time=low will=some benefit=medium metric=time -->
- Cost: Free, no extra time — swap recitation for use-form self-quizzing. The hard part: thinking through how it will actually be used.
- In plain terms: Self-testing's benefits travel beyond the exact items. Across 192 comparisons and 10,000-plus people, it works on changed question formats, application, and reasoning — small-to-medium — while two cases nearly fail: reversed cue-and-answer, and material never tested before.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 40-plus years — 67 articles, 122 experiments, 192 transfer effects, 10,382 participants: versus re-exposure without testing, test-enhanced learning transfers at d=0.40 (95% CI 0.31 to 0.50). Strongest transfer: changed test format, application and inference questions, medical diagnoses, synonym cues. Weakest: stimulus-response reversal, studied-but-never-tested material, worked examples. Response-format match, retrieval practice with detailed feedback, and first-test success strongly modulate positive transfer. Publication-bias corrections barely moved moderator effects but compressed intercepts: with none of the conditions met, positive transfer often vanishes
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Pan SC, Rickard TC (2018). Transfer of test-enhanced learning: meta-analytic review and synthesis. Psychological Bulletin, 144(7), 710-756. <https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151>
- Note: "Memorize" blurs two acts: re-exposure (rereading, highlighting) and closed-book retrieval. The first class bottoms the ten-method rankings (item 16); do the second — item 14, distributed per item 15. Practice: work out the future use first — selecting on an exam or judging on-site — and quiz in that form. Reverse-direction quizzing stays worth doing: this meta-analysis found reversal-direction transfer weakest, so train both directions.

### 20. Professional titles: first your series and level, then the filing channel by employer type

- Benefit: 27 series nationally — economics, engineering, health, publishing — across senior, intermediate, and junior tiers. Applicants file level by level; employers review and publicize no fewer than 5 working days. Private-enterprise and freelance applicants may route through personnel agencies for review, publicity, and recommendation. ### 23. A title isn't a raise: ask whether your employer evaluates within post quotas or evaluates without appointing

- In plain terms: Titles ground hiring, assessment, and promotion — but evaluation and appointment are two things. - Benefit: MOHRSS Order 40, Article 2: evaluation results are an important basis for hiring, assessment, and promotion.
